    Chapter 17 Misaligned Incentives | Principal-Agent

    What this chapter covers: how each layer's rational choice adds up into a loss for the whole; the failure structure of the principal-agent chain, four scenes on the ground, and three ways to write externalities back into assessment. When to turn back to it: when you are designing an assessment scheme, signing an outsourcing or agency contract, or have just found that everyone did the right thing and the result was still bad. Reading time: about 50 minutes.

    Misaligned incentives is the most counterintuitive mechanism in this book, because it asks you to accept an uncomfortable fact: the organization that failed may contain no bad person, and possibly no one who made an error at all. The auditor maintains the client relationship by commercial logic. The tax authority completes its task by target logic. The regional carrier presses cost down by market structure. The seller races to list by platform rule. Each layer's choice is correct on its own ledger, and the loss their sum produces is paid by someone outside the chain. This chapter supplies no villains, only structure, which is why every defence in it is structural as well: change the assessment, change the contract, change who owns the verification. Changing the people does nothing.

    1 What It Is

    Definition: misaligned incentives is a structural disease of the principal-agent chain. Every party optimizes the quantity it is assessed on, and the sum of the parties' choices damages the whole. The identification signature is clean enough to replace criteria with three questions:

    1. Take each layer's choice on its own. Is it rational inside that layer's own objective?
    2. Has anyone broken a written rule?
    3. Add the choices up. On whose head does the loss land, and does that person hold a seat at the decision table?

    The third question carries the diagnosis. When the loss lands outside the chain, on passengers, investors, taxpayers or users, no link inside the chain has a reason to change its behavior on that account. The people in a position to find the problem are the people who benefit from it, as Chapter 37's question set puts it, and where the beneficiary runs the verification the finding persists for years in the form "verified, does not exist".

    The academic frame takes one paragraph. An agency problem appears when one party's interest depends on another party's action and the quality of that action cannot be verified cheaply. Jensen and Meckling's 1976 paper splits the cost into monitoring costs, bonding costs and residual loss, and the sum of the three is the price of the misalignment. This book skips the formal model and keeps one corollary: verification cost sets the depth of the misalignment. Auditing is the textbook industry because the quality of an audit is extremely expensive to verify (readers of the statements cannot observe whether audit procedures were compressed). Listing on a platform is the consumer edition, because the authenticity of a compliance document is just as expensive for a buyer to check. Wherever verification is expensive, misalignment runs deep. The economic content of every defence in this chapter is one sentence: make verification cheap, or move it to someone with a reason to look.

    One distinction has to be made early: misalignment against corruption. Corruption is the agent selling the layer's objective, taking money to let something pass. Misalignment is the agent faithfully executing the objective he was assessed on: collecting tax to hit a target, pressing cost under a per-departure fee, loading goods to hold a listing window. The line matters because the remedies belong to different trades. Corruption removes people. Misalignment changes structure. An organization that confuses the two chases a misalignment as corruption, replaces the people, issues warnings, makes an example, then finds the structure intact and the next outbreak under a new name. The two firms that failed one after the other in case 1 are the ready-made footnote on how little that achieves.

    2 How It Grows

    Three generating conditions.

    Condition one: the externality cannot be priced at its own layer. Your decision damages the ledger of another link (audit distortion damages investors, over-collection damages enterprises, fatigued flying damages passengers), and your own ledger cannot see it. The invisibility is not a management lapse. It is what the accounting categories were designed to do.

    Condition two: the assessment measures only what the layer controls, and cross-layer consequences have no owner. No post signs for the outcome of the chain as a whole, so every layer's optimization contributes its own error to the whole. Chapter 18 picks that up from here.

    Condition three: tenure is shorter than the consequence cycle (Chapter 28). When the cost is paid by a successor or by a third party outside the system, the current-period rational choice keeps tilting, and the successor inherits the tilt.

    Misaligned incentives is the main disease of the incentive layer in Chapter 9's three-layer model. Almost every technical implementation of it runs through a metric, which makes Chapter 12 its standing complication. Almost every defence of it needs someone to call a stop, which makes Chapter 18 its standing precondition.

    One length variable sits outside the three conditions: the longer the chain, the further the sum drifts from the overall objective. Two reasons. Each subcontracting layer attenuates the principal's visibility of end quality by one notch, which is Chapter 16's mechanism replayed at contract level, and the agent's room for interpretation expands to fill it. Each layer's assessment cycle is also shorter than the level above it: headquarters reads the year, the supplier reads the order, the order's upstream reads the delivery week. The shorter the cycle, the more completely the externality leaves the field of view. The key position in defending a long chain is therefore the contract layer. Verifiable end-quality clauses, and not process-compliance clauses, written into every level of the contract are the only thing that lets every link see the end of the chain.

    Price is the resource the incentive layer most often leaves lying. The cheapest form of writing an externality back into assessment is to let payment terms that already exist carry it: an audit fee paid in installments with the final payment tied to the regulator's inspection result, platform settlement to a seller delayed in step with compliance spot checks. None of these creates a post or a report. Each changes the trigger condition on money that was going to move anyway. Two of the three write-ups in section 5 belong to this class. What can be written with money does not need a person watching.

    The material deserves separating, because different materials take different tools. Money (commission, audit fee, pay per unit) is fixed by changing the payment trigger: fastest to work, fastest to regress, since restoring the clause brings the behavior back with it. Assessment (KPI, ranking, performance review) is fixed by changing the definitions and the weights, which is the whole toolkit of Chapter 12: half a beat slower, and it holds. Identity ("we are a safe company", "professionalism") is the cheapest and the most fragile. In quiet years it substitutes for a great deal of money; on the night something happens it is gone. Chapter 13's narrative assets meet the incentive layer at this point. The three usually appear together. An auditor draws an audit fee, carries a partner assessment and believes in professionalism, and the misalignment runs along the resultant of the three. Diagnose them separately, then repair in the order money, assessment, identity: change the payment trigger, then the metric definition, then rebuild the identity. Run that backwards, starting with the slogan, and the identity you shout gives the misalignment cover.

    3 The Shape in Four Domains

    Case 1 (Finance): Paying the Auditor to Be Strict

    fi-008. Zhongshen Zhonghuan Accounting Firm, audits of Yuebo Power's 2019-2021 annual reports. The China Securities Regulatory Commission's administrative penalty decision (〔2026〕 No. 56, page checked for this book): the audit was not performed with due diligence and the reports issued contained false records; the CSRC confiscated the audit fee income of 3,679,245.18 yuan and imposed a fine of the same amount, and the three signing certified public accountants were fined 400,000, 300,000 and 200,000 yuan. The market structure of auditing is this case's entire structure. The auditor's fee is paid by the party being audited, while the real consumers of the audit product, the readers of the statements, neither set the price nor inspect the goods. When keeping the client conflicts with standing up to the client, the direction of the tilt was settled on the day the engagement letter was signed.

    Stop at the decision point: the moment a check in the audit programme is compressed, or an anomaly is waved through. On the desk: the cost of the procedure, the survival of the client relationship, the accumulated experience that "it went through before and nothing happened". 〔What happened〕Confiscation plus a fine of the same amount. The same issuer later failed the same way under another firm (Zhongshen Yatai, fi-009, Appendix B): the structural disease relapsed on the same chain, with one firm swapped out between the first occurrence and the second, which rules out the explanation from individual quality. Primary source: CSRC 〔2026〕 No. 56, 2026, link at Appendix B fi-008.

    One account in case 1 is worth working out for the reader. Confiscation of 3,679,245.18 yuan plus an equal fine, plus 900,000 yuan across the three signing accountants: that is the price tag of the structure. The comparison is the audit fee itself, roughly 3.68 million yuan of annual report audit income across the three years, around 1.2 million a year. When the expected cost of waving one anomaly through (probability of being caught times the penalty) sits below the certain cost of holding the line against a client (the fee income and the renewal), the tilt is a matter of arithmetic rather than morals. The function of a regulatory penalty is to change the arithmetic, lifting the expected cost above the certain one. The same view explains why penalty severity is a core parameter of incentive governance: a rule that fines too lightly is not a rule; it is a price list.

    Case 2 (Public): Over-collection under a Revenue Target (full card)

    po-008.

    When and where: 23 June 2026, the 23rd session of the Standing Committee of the 14th National People's Congress (Beijing). The audited collection behavior took place in the 2025 budget year, on the tax and customs collection front lines and in the treasuries of 21 provinces and municipalities.

    What and who: the National Audit Office, Auditor General Hou Kai reporting to the NPC Standing Committee on behalf of the State Council. The implementing bodies named are the tax and fee collection posts of the tax and customs departments, and local finance and treasuries in 21 provinces and municipalities. The report runs 25 pages in seven parts; this item sits in Part One, audit of central fiscal management, as the first item and the first sub-item, which makes it the opening of the entire problem list. The report discloses at aggregate level and names no individual enterprise.

    What they were doing: in March 2025 the third session of the 14th NPC approved that year's budget, with "organizing fiscal revenue" ranked first among the five items of this central fiscal management audit (p.3). The approved frame was a central general public budget with revenue of 9.737253 trillion yuan, expenditure of 14.597253 trillion yuan and a deficit of 4.86 trillion yuan (p.3). Once execution began, the institutional calendar laid this out as a sequence of actions: corporate income tax prepaid by quarter and settled by the end of May the following year; VAT declared and paid monthly; import-stage taxes collected by customs on behalf of the tax authority; local finance booking expenditure and making transfers at the treasury end. The audit entered after the budget year closed and aggregated the year's collection records by enterprise. The aggregation produced this: tax and customs departments over-collected or collected in advance corporate income tax, VAT and other levies from 1,954 enterprises and others, 40.427 billion yuan (p.4). The same account holds two further sets of actions: 21 provinces and municipalities inflated fiscal revenue by 59.651 billion yuan by booking treasury funds as expenditure and then routing them back (p.4), and 4,976 enterprises evaded 10.935 billion yuan of tax by issuing invoices irregularly for deduction (p.4). The item also lists separately 20 enterprises that evaded 41.4702 million yuan of domestic VAT on export goods (p.4). Four groups of numbers close the item, and the audit gives them one common description: over-collection of "excess taxes and fees", inflation of fiscal revenue and other unhealthy tendencies (p.4).

    The decision point: three irreversible moments, each with a documentary carrier.

    The first is the budget approval of March 2025. The carrier is the budget resolution the NPC approved, which the report records as "strictly implementing the relevant resolutions of the third session of the 14th NPC" (p.1). That moment fixes the twelve-month baseline: revenue of 9.737253 trillion yuan, checked against the table quarter by quarter and month by month.

    The second is a single-enterprise operation inside some filing period of 2025. The carrier is the tax return and the treasury receipt: the prepayment ratio, the supplementary collection inside the filing period, the cross-period adjustment, each action with legal parameters in the collection system. The report discloses no single-enterprise documents and no signing post, only aggregate figures (p.4). Both places read "from 1,954 enterprises and others", and the "and others" leaves collection targets that never entered the enterprise count (p.4; 2024 report p.4). The opacity at this level is a matter of scope: the audit report's problem list stops at enterprises times amount and breaks down nothing below that. The technique in this position differs from one year to the next. The 2024 report had cultivated-land occupation tax, VAT and land transfer revenue (2024 report p.4); the 2025 report has corporate income tax and VAT (p.4). The technique changes. The position does not.

    The third is the rectification closure of 22 December 2025. The carrier is item (1)1 of the State Council report on rectification of the problems found in the 2024 audit of central budget execution and other fiscal revenue and expenditure: tax departments in 28 provinces and municipalities completed rectification of 33.189 billion yuan of over-collected or prematurely collected taxes and late fees by processing refunds and offsets and coaching enterprises to file corrected returns. In the same month the State Taxation Administration's working mechanism against excess taxes and fees, revenue quality monitoring and accountability notices among it, was written into the rectification results. The person reporting the rectification is the Auditor General on behalf of the State Council; the parties executing it are 28 provincial tax bureaus.

    The order of the three is this: the first fixes the number, the second produces it, the third returns it and then stands the mechanism up.

    What was visible then: the approved budget figures of March 2025 (p.3); monthly and quarterly treasury progress in the collection system; individual enterprises' returns, prepayment records and tax source ledgers; the full text of Article 55 of the Budget Law, which forbids collection departments to over-collect or collect in advance budget revenue that is due, and whose other paragraph provides that governments at all levels may not issue revenue targets to budget revenue collection departments and units, in force since 1 January 2015; the previous year's audit report published on 24 June 2025, which put over-collection of excess taxes and fees and similar at 88.997 billion yuan, of which tax departments in 28 provinces and municipalities over-collected or collected in advance 33.189 billion yuan in taxes and late fees from 631 enterprises (2024 report p.4); the recommendation on p.24 of that report, in its own words, to "vigorously rectify the collection of excess taxes and fees, arbitrary charges and arbitrary fines" (2024 report p.24); the rectification ledger of December 2025, with the refund amounts and the number of enterprises coached into corrected returns reported by 28 provincial tax bureaus (rectification report, item (1)1); the correction and refund menus in the collection system, the route the December 2025 rectification actually took; the deficit of 4.86 trillion yuan and the conclusion that the budget was on target (p.3); and the draft for comment and problem list the audited units received before the report was published, recorded on the report's last page, where the National Audit Office "solicited the views of the audited units in accordance with law, issued the audit report and put forward rectification requirements" (p.24). What became known only afterwards: the list of the 1,954 enterprises and the audit's sampling basis; the full chain of the 59.651 billion yuan on the treasury books; the total of 111.013 billion yuan across the three items, which the book arrives at by adding the three figures on p.4. This item also carries no example anywhere in the report's format, being composed entirely of enterprise counts and amounts, where the "for instance, in one province" that appears elsewhere in the report is absent (p.4).

    You are in the seat now, with the material above on the desk.

    Stop here. Decide before you turn the page.

    1. What would you do? Write the action, not "pay more attention".
    2. Which item here are you most likely to miss, and why does it look unimportant?
    3. If you may set one checkpoint, when is it and what does it check?

    What happened: for 2025, 1,954 enterprises were over-collected or collected in advance to the tune of 40.427 billion yuan; 21 provinces and municipalities inflated fiscal revenue by 59.651 billion yuan; 4,976 enterprises evaded 10.935 billion yuan of tax; 20 enterprises evaded 41.4702 million yuan of domestic VAT on export goods (p.4). On 11 June 2026 a State Council executive meeting took up rectification of the problems found in the 2025 audit and set specific requirements (p.24), and the National Audit Office undertook to report full rectification before the end of the year (p.25). The same item in the previous year had been closed with 33.189 billion yuan returned in full through refunds and offsets (rectification report, 22 December 2025). One year later the new year's figure was 40.427 billion yuan. Back to the three questions. The commonest answer to the first is "refund the over-collection", an action already performed once, a full refund, at the cost of a year and a sum of late fees, and the second year's number did not get smaller. The item most often missed in the second question is the phrase "treasury funds booked as expenditure and then routed back", which readers take for an accounting error, while it takes three parties' vouchers aligned, finance, treasury and the spending unit, before it can be done at all. The landing point for the third question is the December rectification closure, and the check is whether those enterprises' prepayment parameters returned to tax source levels after the refund.

    Options ruled out: four, each with a procedure ready at the time, none with a record of use in the report or the public material.

    The first is the budget adjustment procedure, lowering the revenue expectation. The Budget Law requires a budget adjustment to be approved by the standing committee of the people's congress at the same level; the procedure exists, and the cost is waiting for a session.

    The second is lawful approval of deferred payment. The Tax Collection and Administration Law gives provincial tax bureaus authority to approve deferral of tax payment, for up to three months, and the reduced revenue lands on that year's treasury receipts.

    The third is refund and offset inside the filing period. This route was used later, by the rectification: the 33.189 billion yuan was returned through processing refunds and offsets and coaching enterprises into corrected returns (rectification report, 22 December 2025). The same channel was in the system during the collection year.

    The fourth is a written report to the superior level that the jurisdiction's tax source was insufficient, with a request to adjust the task. The report holds no record of this kind.

    All four share one cost: each reduces the current-period treasury receipts. No record exists of the reasons for ruling them out. The only cost readable from the institutions is that one, and that one is visible on the monthly progress table.

    Early signals: ① On 24 June 2025 the previous year's audit report named over-collection of excess taxes and fees and similar at 88.997 billion yuan, of which 33.189 billion yuan fell on tax departments in 28 provinces and municipalities and 631 enterprises, with a further 55.808 billion yuan of land transfer revenue over-collected in 14 provinces and municipalities (2024 report p.4). The finding was classified as irregular organization of fiscal revenue and rectified as a single item. ② The same page carries two numbers pointing the other way, 21,000 eligible enterprises not receiving tax and fee reductions of 280.631 billion yuan in time, and 72.423 billion yuan of taxes and fees under-collected since 2021 because data were not shared (2024 report p.4). Over-collection and under-collection are listed as two items, rectified separately, never put in one place. ③ The recommendation of 24 June 2025 was to rectify vigorously; the recommendation of 23 June 2026 was to clean up vigorously, one character changed, the same recommendation in the same first sentence under fiscal revenue both years, read as last year's problem already under rectification (2024 report p.24; p.22). ④ The rectification report of 22 December 2025 listed the 33.189 billion yuan as rectified and the mechanism as standing, with completion judged by refund amounts and institutional text and with no relation to whether the audited year's behavior had stopped. ⑤ The 59.651 billion yuan technique of booking expenditure and then routing back requires three parties' vouchers aligned, and any one party seen alone shows a compliant entry (p.4). It was read as an accounts technique problem and handled apart from the collection behavior.

    The point of escalation: 22 December 2025, the rectification closed on refunds and offsets, coaching into corrected returns and a new mechanism, with 33.189 billion yuan returned in full and revenue quality monitoring and accountability notices online (rectification report, 22 December 2025). Six months later, the report of 23 June 2026 lists 40.427 billion yuan again in the same subsection at the same ordinal position, with the enterprise count up from 631 to 1,954 (p.4; 2024 report p.4). The two numbers cannot be subtracted from each other. Audit scope and sampling targets differ year by year, and a count that tripled more likely means smaller enterprises were sampled, the average per enterprise falling from 52.6 million to 20.69 million yuan by the book's calculation; what is comparable is the position, not the size. The two reports also place the question in the same position, under "(1) irregular organization of fiscal revenue, policy implementation not in place" (2024 report p.4; p.4). The escalation has no single signatory. Its landing point is the correspondence between the two reports: the definition of rectification stops at money returned and mechanism standing, while the audited behavior ran its same course inside that same year. The State Council executive meeting of 11 June 2026 deployed the same kind of rectification again (p.24), which is the second turn of this loop, and the body that certifies rectification complete is the body that finds the relapse.

    Failure chain: technical layer. Every action in the collection system carries legal parameters, prepayment ratio, filing period, receipt account, corrected return, all of them on the menu; the booking and the routing back at the treasury end use different vouchers, each complete on its own; the data sit in segments across tax, finance, treasury and the People's Bank of China, and every segment adds up. Organizational layer. The audit is ex-post external sampling whose scope stops at enterprises times amount, and the 1,954 enterprises have no list; collection is ex-ante and enterprise by enterprise, decided at the console inside the filing period; rectification acceptance is self-reported by the party being rectified, with refund amounts compiled and reported by the tax departments themselves. Incentive layer. Revenue is a monthly measurable output of the layer; tax source authenticity, enterprise cash flow and business environment are cross-period unmeasurable externalities that enter no monthly table; once the approved 9.737253 trillion yuan becomes the progress baseline, every period's shortfall has to be made up in the next. All three layers broke at once. The break at the boundary sits between the organizational and the incentive layer: Article 55 of the Budget Law closed the door on issuing revenue targets in 2015, while approved budget figures and execution progress were not prohibited, so the number stayed on the table and only the door plate changed its name. By the end of April 2026, rectification of the 2024 audit findings had reached 1.41 trillion yuan in amounts, more than 1,150 institutional documents drawn up or improved, and more than 3,690 people disciplined or handled (p.3), with the scale of rectification and the recurrence of the same problem present at the same time. The three units of measurement are money, documents and people (p.3), and none of them measures onto the accounts of the enterprises that were over-collected.

    Minimum counterfactual: before the rectification closed in December 2025, draw 100 enterprises from the 33.189 billion yuan refund list and check their prepayment parameters in the next filing period. One action, in December 2025. Refund data, filing data and tax source ledgers already sit in the same system, so the step needs no new authority and no new body, and the cost is one sample. It also needs nobody to admit a problem: prepayment parameters are numbers, and one comparison with the previous year's actual payment produces them. The 100 enterprises are a workload figure, one review team finishing in two weeks, and the result has two forms, parameters back at actual payment or parameters not back. The second form, arriving in December, lands just before the next year's budget is issued. If the parameters are still above actual payment, the 40.427 billion yuan gets seen as it happens instead of a year later, when the audit aggregates it enterprise by enterprise.

    Outcome and sources: across the three items of 2025, 111.013 billion yuan (40.427 plus 59.651 plus 10.935, p.4). The report publishes at the aggregate level of 21 provinces and municipalities, with no province and no enterprise named. Rectification enters the next budget year's procedure, with a report to the NPC Standing Committee before the end of the year (p.25). The page references cited in this item are concentrated on pp. 3, 4, 22, 24 and 25. Primary source: National Audit Office, Report of the State Council on the Audit of Central Budget Execution and Other Fiscal Revenue and Expenditure for 2025, delivered on 23 June 2026 at the 23rd session of the 14th NPC Standing Committee, PDF of 25 pages, all page numbers in this card being pages of that PDF, link at Appendix B po-008. The comparison documents are the same-named report for 2024 (24 June 2025, 27 pages) and the report on rectification of the problems found in the 2024 audit (22 December 2025).

    Case 2 has one layer left to take apart. The 59.651 billion yuan of inflated fiscal revenue was produced by techniques of the kind described as booking treasury funds as expenditure and routing them back. The existence of such a technique is itself a diagnosis of the incentive layer: it needs several links to cooperate, booking, routing back, reporting, which tells you the inflation was not an individual collector improvising but an organizational craft for completing a target. Past a certain intensity of target pressure an organization invents its own craft for meeting the target, and the complexity of the craft is proportional to the pressure. The lesson for whoever designs targets is written in the arithmetic: every unit by which a target exceeds the real tax source makes the craft one notch more precise, and the 59.651 billion yuan the audit found is the full width of the gap between target and source.

    Case 3 (Aviation): The Chain Where Every Layer Saved a Little

    av-032. Colgan Air 3407 (Continental Connection), 12 February 2009, Clarence Center, New York. NTSB final report (AAR-10/01, PDF downloaded and checked for this book): during a night approach, control error after the stall warning, the aircraft stalling into a house, 49 people on board and 1 on the ground dead. An economic structure sits inside the report's factor chain. The regional carrier operated for the mainline airline under a per-departure fee structure, with the mainline brand and the regional capacity on two separate ledgers. Pilot pay levels, commuting-style scheduling and their interaction with fatigue are discussed explicitly in the report: the captain had commuted a long distance to reach base that day, and fatigue management and the scheduling rules were compliant on their face. The company optimized capacity cost, the pilots optimized their personal living arrangements, the regulator set its defences by carrier class. Every layer's choice made sense on its own books.

    Stop at the decision point: the operating day on which the scheduling and fatigue management rules took effect for a commuting crew. On the desk: the roster, the commuting legs, rest rules met on paper, and a question no party was required to answer, which is how much safety margin this combination was worth in that night's approach. 〔What happened〕50 dead; the accident drove revisions to the fatigue rules and to captain experience requirements. Primary source: NTSB, AAR-10/01, 2010, link at Appendix B av-032.

    The coldest layer in case 3 is compliance on paper, since the fatigue management and scheduling rules were met on their face. The compliance measure tests duty and rest duration for a single segment, and it cannot measure what a cross-city commute actually costs; the parameters are written in an office, the cost is incurred on the red-eye commute. In a chain where every layer is compliant, the safety margin leaks out at the seams between layers: the seam between company and pilot, where whether commuting counts as duty time is undefined; the seam between mainline and regional, where the per-departure fee turns speed into revenue; the seam between regulator and carrier, where tiered defence assumes regional capacity carries lower risk. The rule revisions after the accident, fatigue rules and captain experience requirements, are in essence an assignment of the seam's account to whoever holds signature authority. The reader's self-check follows from that: at the seams of your chain, whose margin is leaking now.

    Case 5 (Platform and Data): A Structural Conflict That Survived Two Redesigns (full card)

    sw-007.

    When and where: 15 December 2020, when the consent mechanism went live, to 1 September 2025, when the decision issued; France. The inspections ran online and in Paris: a remote inspection from 20 October to 4 November 2022; three on-site inspections at Google France on 29 November 2022, 6 December 2022 and 5 September 2023, at 8 rue de Londres in the 9th arrondissement; an online check of the cookies on google.fr, YouTube, Google Maps and Google Search on 20 April 2023 (§10, §11, §12, §13, §42).     What and who: Google LLC (California, a wholly owned subsidiary of Alphabet Inc.) and Google Ireland Limited, which handles Google products for the European Economic Area and Switzerland.     The decision point: three dates, each with a carrier.

    The first is 15 December 2020, when the account creation consent mechanism went live. The carrier is the account creation path on google.fr itself; the decision records the date and the two-step and five-step structure, with no signing post appearing (§113, §117). The first path's page is titled "Confirm your personalisation settings and cookies", and it holds two buttons, "Confirm" and "Back" (§118). The decision records the change as intended to reduce the risk of confusion with messages, and the restricted committee held it insufficient to overturn the legal characterization (§160, §249). The same period holds the second on-site inspection of 5 September 2023 and company submissions of 26 September and 14 November 2023 (§12, §14).

    The third is October 2023, when the path was redesigned. Quick personalisation (1 step) was renamed "quick choice (1 step)", two buttons were added, "Refuse all" and "Accept all", and clicking through led to a "Confirm your settings" page that still offered "Confirm" or "Back" (§121). The manual path was renamed "manual choice (4 steps)", with the content of pages 3 and 5 untouched and page 3 still titled "Enable the 'customised ads' parameters to see more relevant ads" (§122, §123). The decision's conclusion splits in two: from that date the freedom limb of consent was no longer missing (§136), and the flow as a whole satisfied the informed limb neither before nor after the redesign, because nowhere from beginning to end did it state that access to the service is conditional on advertising cookies being dropped (§138, §141).

    The notice sentence at step 5 of the manual path still comes after the user has picked an advertising type (§124). The order of the three moments is structure first, appearance second, buttons last. The round that added buttons changed the click count and left the disclosure alone, and the choice on page 3 still happens before the explanation on page 5, so a user has to pick an advertising type before learning that what he picked is a whole set of cookies (§143).

    What happened: on 1 September 2025 the restricted committee fined Google LLC 200 million euros and Google Ireland Limited 125 million euros, 325 million euros in total, the two amounts split by company with each covering both violations and no split by violation (§264, §265 and the operative part). It also ordered both to remedy within six months of notification: French users must be made to understand that creating an account necessarily means advertising cookies, and prior consent must be obtained before advertising is inserted between messages; after the deadline the penalty is 100,000 euros a day (§269, §270, §271). The decision was published, with the names removed after two years, and an appeal to the Conseil d'État lies within four months (last page of the operative part). Back to the three questions. The commonest answer to the first is another round of remediation, though two rounds have already shown that the same structure regenerates the same violation, so the third round's output will most likely be a third remediation document. The item most often missed in the second question is the revenue function itself: whether the consent mechanism is bolted onto the architecture or built into it decides whether remediation fixes the boundary or the engine. The correct landing point for the third question is the interface design review, whose mandatory question is "what is the revenue state of this architecture with all consent switched off", a question that forces revenue dependence and compliance boundary into the same room.

    Options ruled out: four, each with ready practice at the time.

    The first is an explicit cookie wall. The restricted committee says plainly that tying access to the service to cookies is not in itself unlawful, provided consent is free and refusal carries no loss (§127); the decision even gives two sample lines, one for creating a free account and accepting cookies for personalised advertising and content, one for creating a free account and accepting only cookies for generic advertising (§146). The route was there and was not taken, and the decision's assessment is that where the company could have chosen a cookie wall that is unambiguous to users, it chose a path that was complex and ambiguous (§147).

    The second is making refusal one step. The "Refuse all" button of October 2023 proves it can be done (§121); it did not appear before the redesign, and after the redesign it solved only the click count (§136).

    The third is stating the condition on the first screen. The remediation order requires exactly that sentence (§269); the decision notes that neither path carried it at any time (§141), and that the manual path split it across steps 3 and 5, leaving the user to join the two (§143).

    The fourth is making advertising entries plainly unlike messages. The change of April 2023 did part of it (§160); the entries in the Court's case had a grey background and showed no sender (§190), while Gmail's have a white background and a sender name in the same format as messages (§193).

    All four leave the revenue model untouched and move disclosure and clicks instead. The decision holds no record of ruling them out.

    Early signals: ① The two penalties of 7 December 2020 and 31 December 2021, same defendant and same subject, both on cookie consent under Article 82, the 2021 one naming the inconvenience of refusal on the home pages of google.fr and youtube.com (§84, §244). They were read as a fine on the website banner and not on the account creation flow, a distinction the company itself raised in defence (§28, §34, §35). ② The Court's judgment of 25 November 2021 (§157), given nearly a year before the inspection (§242), was read as a civil judgment about advertising that is not mail, the company arguing against applying it by analogy (§162), and the appearance change was pushed back to April 2023 on that reading (§160). ③ The guidelines of 17 September 2020 required refusal to be as easy as acceptance (§101), and the penalty of 31 December 2021 had already stated as a principle that the two options must take an equal number of operations (§135), while the company's own path still ran two against six (§133). It was read as already offering a refusal path; the decision acknowledges the choice existed and finds the manner of expression distorted it (§134). ④ After the buttons went in during October 2023 the informed limb was still unrepaired (§138), and that was read as remediation complete, with the company arguing that the order no longer served a purpose under Article 82 because the redesign had been finished (§267). ⑤ From September 2024 the company stopped using some cookies connected with personalised advertising (§115, §249), which is a different matter from opting out of all advertising cookies; the decision records it as a mitigating circumstance (§249, §247) without changing the finding, and the account scale kept rising through the same period, with the measurements of 13 May 2023 and 10 September 2024 each higher than the one before (§241).

    The point of escalation: the October 2023 redesign is that step. The date is in the decision, the action was adding two buttons and renaming the paths (§121), and no signatory appears. The timing sits after the inspections closed and nine months before the report was served on 25 July 2024 (§16). In the year before, both inspection procedures had run their course (§8, §11, §12), and the April 2023 format change had already been made once (§160). The escalation holds because the redesign closed the freedom limb while fixing the informed limb in place: the path set from then on, and every later claim of compliance landed on "the user can choose", which is the defence's own wording at §267, while the gap the decision finds is exactly that the user does not know he has no choice (§141, §142). Adding the sentence about access being conditional on cookies after the redesign would mean overturning the whole path design of December 2020; leaving it out, every step of the path can be called a choice. The acceptance standard of that round shows here too. The deliverable list is buttons and renaming, and the decision, comparing page by page, records that the content of pages 3 and 5 of the manual path was unchanged (§122), so the deliverable is two buttons live while the running state is whether the user knows what he is choosing. A deliverable can be accepted. A running state can only be sampled.

    Failure chain: technical layer. The advertising entries share the size, the white background and the sender format of messages and differ only in a green Ad and the date position, the web version lacks a checkbox, and clicking opens the whole advertisement in the form of a message (§187, §188); account creation holds two paths, two clicks to accept and six to refuse (§133); the October 2023 redesign added buttons and left disclosure split across steps 3 and 5 (§143). Organizational layer. Both changes land on the product path, and the decision records dates and structures only, with no signing post (§113, §121); the joint-controller finding rests on the parent's full ownership and the decision chain and has nothing to do with who signed (§262); the approval column is empty in the decision; remediation was delivered by the party being remediated, out of its own path and its own explanation (§267); after the report was served on 25 July 2024 the company's four rounds of written submissions argued jurisdiction and ne bis in idem (§28, §33, §37), not facts. Incentive layer. Gmail is provided free, and its maintenance and development are funded by the group's advertising revenue, a layer the decision's own text describes as therefore intrinsically connected with the dropping of cookies (§130), and it provides that a party adopting a cookie wall must show its purpose is to keep an economic balance between providing the service and its cost (§129, §131). The difference between the personalised and the generic inventory is recorded as degree of intrusiveness (§109, §110), with the unit price absent from the file; group revenue in 2024 was 350 billion dollars, about 312 billion euros (§263), and the advertising line's own revenue figures are redacted in the published version (§252). All three layers broke at once. The break at the boundary runs between the technical and the incentive layer: every click on the path is at the same time a pricing of inventory, and the ease of the click is decided by the technical implementation.

    Minimum counterfactual: when the path went live on 15 December 2020, put the sentence "creating an account requires accepting advertising cookies" on the first screen, beside the two options. One action, on the day the mechanism went live, changing no button and adding one line of copy. The cost is a screen of copy and one copy review, with the two sample lines at paragraph 146 of the decision serving as template; no architecture change, no change to the revenue model, and the six-click asymmetry can be left as it is. The step was doable at the time and did not depend on luck: the guidelines of September 2020 already required clear disclosure (§101), and the remediation order at paragraph 269 requires exactly that sentence. Acceptance takes one person and one pass: open google.fr, walk the first path, see whether the first screen carries the sentence. It does not erase all liability, since the freedom problem before the redesign still stands, and it erases the informed limb, which is the one thing this Article 82 violation lacked from 2020 to 2025 (§138, §153, §154).

    Laid out by industry, the seven cases run audit, tax collection, regional aviation, platform retail, platform data, small-batch manufacturing and adviser identity, and the distribution follows one rule: the more expensive verification is in an industry, the heavier the case. Audit and securities, cases 1 and 7, verify quality in units of years, and their cases end in criminal exposure and large penalties. Platform retail, cases 4 and 6, verifies in units of spot checks, and its cases end in a recall catalogue. Data platforms, case 5, sit between the two and end in a seven-figure euro fine. Verification cost grades the failure style of an industry: expensive verification produces big cases, cheap verification produces catalogues. Inverted, the rule is the budget table for defences. Whichever grade your industry's verification cost sits in is the grade your incentive-layer governance budget should sit in.

    One diagnostic tool belongs beside that budget table: principal identification. Every agency relation has a nominal principal, the one who pays, and a real principal, the one who carries the consequence, and the separation between the two is the depth of the misalignment. The nominal principal of an audit is the audited company, the real principal is the reader of the statements. The nominal principal of goods on a platform is the seller, the real principal is the buyer. The nominal principal of regional capacity is the mainline carrier, the real principal is the passenger. The tool is one question: when this thing breaks, who loses most, and who has no seat today? Find that person and you have the legal starting point of every defence in this chapter, since Device 2's whole-result signatory acts, nominally, for this absent party. The absent party's seat is the origin of the whole corporate governance literature, which this chapter does not re-expand; it only notes that any proposal claiming to solve misaligned incentives while giving the absent party no place to speak is circling inside agency upon agency.

    4 What Shows Up Earliest

    Seven weak signals, each with the reason that usually presses it down.

    1. The phrases "we followed the contract" and "we followed the rules" appear at abnormal density in meeting minutes. Reason given: following the rules is not a fault.
    2. Every piece of bad news reaching a given post comes off another link's ledger. Reason: that is the division of labor.
    3. The beneficiary is the verifier of some problem, and the verification reads "no issues" year after year. Reason: it is running normally.
    4. Decisions with large externalities are never made by anyone who meets the people who carry them. Reason: hierarchical division of labor.
    5. The assessment metrics fight each other, and every side in the fight is winning. Reason: multi-dimensional assessment.
    6. The same structural disease recurs at different suppliers or divisions, and every response is to replace the people. Reason: an execution problem. If replacing people worked, the second occurrence would not have happened.
    7. In contract negotiation, risk clauses get far less time than price clauses. Reason: price is the business.

    Put the seven back against this chapter's cases. Signal 2, bad news arriving from another link's ledger, is case 1: the first victim of an audit quality problem is the statement reader, who appears on no ledger of the firm. Signal 3, the beneficiary verifying and finding nothing year after year, covers cases 1 and 4: the party maintaining the client relationship shares a table with the party guarding audit quality, and on the platform the spot check sits on the same side as the listing. Signal 4, deciders who never meet the bearers, is case 3: the designer of the capacity cost structure does not sit in the cockpit on a night approach. Signal 5, metrics fighting while every side wins, is case 2: the revenue metric wins and tax source reality loses. Signal 6, a recurring structural disease handled by replacing people, covers the two firms of case 1 and applies as well to the three recalls of case 6. Signal 7, risk clauses getting short negotiation time, is the native form of the listing terms in case 4.

    One indicator of the layer's own deserves writing down: the whole-chain checkup, an extension of question 5 in section 7. Take the most recent loss that crossed a link boundary, write out each layer's choice and its rationality in that layer, and put the summed result and the party carrying it on the last line. The layer-by-layer rationality table is the incentive layer's examination sheet: every row, read alone, is innocent, and the last row is the lesion. It is also the strongest tool for talking to outsiders, since any party shown the table can find on it the evidence of his own correctness and, on the last line, the cost he took part in. The persuasion problem of misaligned incentives is hard exactly there, and that is also where it is solved.

    The three questions need operating instructions. For the first, on whether each layer is rational, the material to check is each layer's assessment documents and contracts, never interviews: an interview will say of course we put safety first, an assessment document tells the truth. For the second, on whether anyone broke a rule, the material is the compliance audit's conclusion: an all-green compliance record next to a bad outcome is the signature of the second question holding. For the third, on whose head the loss lands, the material is the consequences chapter of the accident report set against the seating chart of the decision table, and the seat gap shows up in the first comparison of the two lists. All of it is available inside the organization, which makes this one of the lowest-threshold diagnoses in the book: it needs no industry knowledge, only the one act of laying the documents side by side.

    5 How to Prevent It

    Device 1: externality accounting. Write the foreseeable effect of this layer's decisions on the other parties of the chain into this layer's own assessment. Audit firms get assessed retrospectively on later penalties and litigation records, and the CSRC penalty is an instance of the externality returning to the account, since what it fines is the consideration the audit fee bought. The quality of lawful collection enters the collection agency's assessment weights. What will not fit a quantitative clause goes in as a qualitative item with mandatory reporting at every review.

    Device 2: a signatory for the whole result. Every multi-link chain names one role answerable only to the whole: the engagement partner answerable to the readers of the statements, the collection quality officer answerable to the taxpayer's experience, the operations control chief answerable to passenger safety. Its assessment is tied to no output of the layer. An empty title achieves nothing. The person must hold the power to stop (Chapter 18), and the stop must not be subject to performance assessment by any party inside the layer (Chapter 19's protection interface).

    Device 3: beneficiary recusal from verification. Whoever benefits from a problem may not act as its verifier, and an arrangement already in place is replaced by external sampling. The whole institutional history of audit independence is the implementation record of this one clause, and its failures always begin with an exception: "this one is special, let the in-house team review it first".

    Device 4: a structure-review trigger. When the same failure mode appears at two or more suppliers or divisions, escalation to a structure review is mandatory: examine the contracts and the assessments, not the people. Recurrence is the incentive layer speaking. A problem that yields to a personnel change does not recur, and a problem that recurs never yields to one.

    The four devices against this chapter's cases. Device 1 on case 1: add regulatory penalties and litigation lookback to partner assessment, with the 3,679,245.18 yuan of this case as a ready-made lookback entry. Device 2 on case 3: add a co-signing role answerable for passenger safety to the capacity clauses of the mainline and regional contract. Device 3 on case 5: move the verifier of the consent mechanism's remediation from the advertising line to outside it. Device 4 on case 6: the third recall of the same kind triggers a structure review of the platform's small-batch import channel automatically.

    The three write-ups for putting an externality back into assessment are worth giving in full. Write-up one is money: final payment, revenue share and settlement cycle tied to verifiable end quality, as described above. Write-up two is a post: the whole-result signatory, assessed on the end result alone, which is Device 2. Write-up three is an account: convert the externality into an internal cost and book it, as a compliance cost line for collection quality or a fatigue risk provision inside the capacity structure. The common enemy of all three is the excuse that it cannot be quantified: the accounting version of write-up three allows interval estimates (Chapter 11's Device 2, the digitizing of margin), and what will not fit a quantitative clause goes in as a qualitative item reported at each review. None of the three is expensive. What is expensive is the reason for refusing to write them, which is that the layer does not want to see its own externality. Seeing it is the first step of every defence in this chapter.

    The four failure modes deserve equal space, since each has a respectable way to die. Externality accounting dies of account inflation: the lookback line takes everything, and in the end it is indistinguishable from an ordinary cost line. The whole-result signatory dies of being an empty title: signatures get signed and the stop power is never wired, with no budget veto and no direct reporting line. Whether a signatory is real takes two facts, whether he has ever stopped something and what happened afterwards. Beneficiary recusal dies of spreading exceptions: the list of special cases grows longer every year until recusal is a principle on paper, so every exception needs written approval and a quarterly review in aggregate. The structure-review trigger dies of threshold decay: two or more becomes three, then four, and the trigger becomes a souvenir. All four share one examination question: when did this device last actually fire? A device that cannot produce a date is dead.

    6 Boundaries with Adjacent Mechanisms

    Against goal displacement (Chapter 12): misaligned incentives is the interest structure, goal displacement is its technical implementation. The criteria divide the work. If what needs changing is the interest structure, meaning who carries the consequence, go to Chapter 17. If the interest structure is sound and the measurement is crooked, stay in Chapter 12. The two often need work at the same time, and the order is to plug the structure first, by beneficiary recusal from verification, and repair the measurement after.

    Against diffusion of responsibility (Chapter 18): dilution is nobody being responsible, with the signature chain ownerless; misalignment is someone being responsible in the wrong direction, with the assessment pointing the wrong way. The criterion is one question. Give the responsibility to one named person: does the situation change? If it changes, the disease is dilution. If giving it changes nothing because his assessment forces him to act against it, the disease is misalignment.

    Against horizon mismatch (Chapter 28): when the current-period tilt is amplified by short tenure, the horizon dimension stacks on top. The criterion: extend the decision-maker's tenure three times over, would the choice change? If it would, the main disease is in Chapter 28. If it would not, the main disease is in this chapter.

    7 Self-Check: Five Questions

    1. Write out the assessment objective of every link in your chain and see where the sum points. Does it point at the objective of the whole?
    2. Which problems in your organization have the beneficiary for a verifier? Can you list three?
    3. Has one of your decisions damaged another link's ledger? Did that damage appear in any assessment document of yours?
    4. If your role were given one more line, answerable only for the result of the whole, which line of your present assessment would have to be deleted to make room?
    5. Take an old incident in which every layer was blameless, write out each layer's rational choice in turn, and put the summed result on the last line. That line is the incentive layer's examination report.
