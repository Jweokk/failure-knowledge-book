    Finance | fi

    Five columns: record quality, feedback delay, coupling, the three deadliest mechanisms, and what outsiders most easily learn wrong.

    1 Record quality

    Enforcement documents are the gold mine. The SEC publishes litigation releases case by case, numbered and citable (5 fi cases here); FINRA's monthly disciplinary summary PDF covers the middle and long tail of brokerage violations; the CSRC publishes administrative penalty decisions in full (4 fi cases here, with body pages that can be scraped while the list page is loaded dynamically, all of it recorded in). UK FCA final notices were unreachable from this book's network environment [pending verification]. The structural gap is that enforcement documents record what got caught. The build-up of systemic risk, meaning leverage, concentration and liquidity mismatch, has almost no full text before it breaks: the material for a 2008-class event is a commission report written afterwards, not a supervisory document written before. Overall verdict: micro-level violations are recorded in great thickness, and macro-level risk depends on post-crisis ratification.

    2 Feedback delay

    Two clocks. On the market clock, price and liquidity feedback arrives in seconds, which produces the illusion of the high-frequency stop-loss; most risk does not report back on the market clock at all. On the regulatory clock, violation feedback arrives in years (the conduct period in fi-001 ran 2019–2021 with the penalty in 2026; the bulk fraud in fi-004 ran 2025–2026 up to indictment), and risk feedback arrives in cycles, since credit losses are invisible during a boom. The career incentives of a financial decision-maker are perfectly mismatched to the regulatory clock: the bonus is paid on the market clock and the penalty lands on the regulatory clock.

    3 Coupling

    The highest of any domain. Institutions are wired to each other through interbank liabilities, clearing and collateral networks, so a single default travels along the clearing chain. On the asset side they are wired through common holdings, which makes the diversification that rises and falls together a pseudo-diversification. On the information side, market sentiment transmits infection instantly. The isolation devices of finance, meaning margin, clearing houses and deposit insurance, essentially convert coupling from disorderly into orderly. Coupling itself never goes away; it is institutionalized into a settleable form. The supplier line of a risk register corresponds here to the counterparty line, and the checklist in Chapter 33 is a life-and-death document in this field.

    4 The three deadliest mechanisms

    Misaligned incentives (Chapter 17): the tension inside audit independence, where two firms failed in succession on the same subject (fi-008, fi-009), and the structural conflict between sales and risk control. Goal displacement (Chapter 12): the beautification of loan loss metrics (fi-001), and a risk rating that departs from actual risk, 24 of 53 (the GAO case in Chapter 12, the public edition of financial governance). Regulatory lag and capture (Chapter 31): fractional share trading falling outside an old classification (fi-011), bulk registration fraud (fi-004), the compliance gap around an AI financing narrative (fi-002). Next in frequency come narrative before evidence (the offering frauds in fi-002 and fi-005) and diffusion of responsibility (the four-signature chain in fi-007).

    5 What outsiders most easily learn wrong

    Wrong lesson one: read penalty documents as moral evidence. They record violations that can be proved, not the whole of an organizational culture, and using them to judge culture carries the same sample bias as the survivorship skew in Chapter 2. Wrong lesson two: rank severity by the size of the fine. The $125,000 in fi-013 and the $17.78 million in fi-005 do not measure the same dimension. Wrong lesson three: take diversification for safety. Diversification that synchronizes across peers is a single exposure under stress (Chapter 33), and most of the simultaneous failure of diversified portfolios in financial history carries a common factor's name.

    6 Three questions from an outsider

    Question one: what use are financial institution cases to an ordinary investor? A due diligence checklist grows straight out of them: auditor independence, cross-checking against the rating, the signs of a backlog of alerts. The five-step audit in Chapter 12 is a ready tool for investors. Question two: the fines look light, $125,000 against 2.56 million trades, so is breaking the rules a good trade? That is criterion 3 shown as arithmetic, where the fine becomes a membership fee (Chapter 31). A light penalty is not grounds for feeling lucky. It is evidence that supervision failed. Question three: is the compliance vocabulary too hard? The action sequences in these cases, cancel the order, release it, report it, are ordinary commercial actions, and the terms live in the source document titles. The mechanism layer is readable by anyone.

    9 The starter kit

    Three items for a reader new to Finance. Entry case: fi-001 (thousands of delinquent timeshare loans rescinded and loan loss metrics dressed up; SEC, 2026; link at Appendix B). Read it first for the house style and the grain size of a document in this field. Entry data source: the primary collection listed under finance in. Find the entrance, bookmark it, visit once a quarter. Entry mechanism: start with the top row of the section 7 list and read that chapter against a case from here. One mechanism gets two passes, once in the mechanism chapter for the criteria, once in this domain for the local parameters. The three items take about one working day, and at the end of it you own a first coordinate system for the field.

    10 Reservations

    Three limits sit on the judgments above. The case library samples toward events with rich public records, and the skew is described in Chapter 2. The frequency order reflects this book's library, not the true distribution in the field. Institutions and technology move, so this page has a shelf life measured in years, and any citation of it should carry the year. Those three are the book's general disclaimer, and the threshold check of Chapter 1 applies to this book as much as to any other artifact.

    11 The three layers in the finance accent

    The finance accent is that the incentive-layer breakpoint is the clearest of the three. Nearly every penalty decision describes one: the provisioning pressure in fi-001, the offering narrative in fi-005, the arbitrage of a rule gap in fi-011. The decision is itself the official confirmation of an incentive-layer breakpoint, which is why evidence grades in this field run high by nature, since C-grade documents are produced by a process that runs automatically. For the reviewer: in the nine-box walkthrough, the incentive row has the most complete evidence, and the decision can be quoted directly. The bottleneck is usually the technical layer instead, because the detail of the trading system gets compressed when the decision is written.

    13 Closing note

    A last piece of advice for finance readers. The lesson of these cases condenses to one word, basis. The accounting basis in fi-001, the classification basis in fi-011, the conversion basis in fi-012, the audit basis in fi-008: financial failure almost never happens at the trading layer. It happens at the definition layer, and the definition layer is the battlefield of Chapter 12. The self-check is to open your own metric definition document, walk it line by line with the basis-change register format from Chapter 12, and ask whether each change of basis is recorded. A basis change with no record is a potential fi-001.

    These cases carry an evidential advantage no other domain has. A penalty decision is a statutory document: SEC, FINRA, CNIL and CSRC decisions all pass through legal process, sit at evidence grade C, and their facts have been tested. The cases on this page therefore rank above the D and E grade cases of other industries as a matter of course. There is also a reading technique. The findings of fact are the section worth close reading, because they record the regulator's list of established facts rather than legal argument, and the basis of that list is the regulator's own visibility boundary (Chapter 31). The case count is moderate and the amounts span widely, from €80,000 to €325 million; the way to read that span is in Chapter 31 as well. What matters is the ratio of penalty to gain. The absolute figure is only the yearly dues on a membership.

    14 How the mechanisms vary here

    Goal displacement, finance variation: fi-001, the timeshare loan rescissions, is the archetype case of Chapter 12, and the variation specific to finance is that the metric coincides with the regulatory report. One number both assesses people inside the firm and gets disclosed to the regulator, so once displacement happens the distortion enters two loops at once. Read it this way: the disclosure obligation list for basis changes, meaning which metrics require an explanation to investors when their basis changes, is the core compliance asset of this field, and the failure point in fi-001 was the missing disclosure. A self-check for the reader: does the metric you are responsible for have a basis sensitivity note, saying which accounting judgments move the number and by how much.

    Narrative before evidence, finance variation: fi-002 (an AI startup overstating model feasibility) and fi-005 (a cannabis company fraud) show the narrative pressure of a fundraising scene, where the funding window, the competitive story and the founder's reputation all push toward an optimistic description of prospects. Read it this way: the ratio of narrative strength in an offering document to the support of the underlying evidence is the right object of due diligence, which is Chapter 37's question used in diligence. fi-003 and fi-004, a forged ADV and shell adviser entities, show the industrialized form of narrative: once false filings can be produced programmatically, supervisory verification density has to be matched to the production rate, and that is the point of the SEC's bulk cases.

    Misaligned incentives, finance variation: fi-008 and fi-009, two accounting firms failing on the same subject one after the other, are the structural form of the mechanism inside professional service intermediaries. The audit fee is paid by the audited party, so the economic foundation of independence tilts by construction. Read it this way: audit opinion shopping, measured by the frequency and stated reasons for changing auditors, and the quality curve across an auditor's tenure, are the two correct observation points on the incentive structure here. fi-010 through fi-013, filing errors and alerts closed in seconds, show the incentive layer of the compliance post: the cost of a compliance action is immediate and the probability of regulatory discovery is discounted. The SEC's monthly penalty summary, the main source for the fi series, is exactly the institutional device that raises that discovery probability.
