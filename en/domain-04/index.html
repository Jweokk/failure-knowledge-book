    Healthcare | me

    Five columns: record quality, feedback delay, coupling, the three deadliest mechanisms, and what outsiders most easily mislearn.

    1 Record Quality

    The latecomer that built the institution properly is the United Kingdom. The Health Services Safety Investigations Body (HSSIB, formerly HSIB) runs its investigations on the aviation pattern, and its reports carry the event sequence, the safety recommendations, and a national figure to anchor them, such as "roughly 192 wrong-site surgeries nationwide each year". Every me case in this book comes out of that archive. The original site was unreachable from the network used to collect material for this book, so the reports were read through Internet Archive snapshots, and records which snapshot stands behind which case.

    The American material is a step removed. AHRQ's Patient Safety Network (PSNet) and Web&M&M publish case commentaries, secondary curation written for teaching, strong on framing and weak as evidence. On the device side the FDA's MAUDE database holds reports in volume, with single-entry quality uneven.

    China's health commission bulletins are available only intermittently. The workable substitute is the judgment in a medical damage liability dispute, which supplies a dated sequence, expert opinion, and a finding on causation, at the price of a legal frame wrapped around a clinical event (Chapter 3).

    The verdict: full-text investigation is best in the UK, and its narrative depth is unusual because the patient's identity is protected while the whole chain stays legible. The sample per case is small, which is why the national figure carries as much weight as the report itself.

    2 Feedback Delay

    Three layers, and they run at wildly different speeds. At the medication and surgical layer feedback is counted in minutes: a wrong site or a wrong concentration shows on the table or by the end of the day. At the process layer it is counted in weeks and months: follow-up that does not happen, results that nobody claims, with the twelve-day reporting delay of me-005 as the type specimen. At the system layer it is counted in years: how far the reconciliation routine has drifted, what the identification-event rate is doing.

    What separates healthcare from the rest of the book is the spread inside a single shift. One nurse's day holds second-scale and year-scale risk at the same time, and a reflex trained at one scale misfires at the other.

    3 Coupling

    Inside a hospital, coupling is moderate. Processes are templated closely enough that a defect replicates isomorphically from ward to ward, and physical separation is comparatively generous, so a bad process does not take the building down with it. Across the industry, coupling is high. A process validated at one hospital gets adopted nationally and the defects travel with it: the medication-error shapes of me-001 through me-003 recur across HSIB's national figures instead of staying local. A national protocol is a single point with national reach. The colour coding of injectable medicines is the standing example; when the coding fails, it fails everywhere at once.

    4 The Three Deadliest Mechanisms

    Handover rupture (Chapter 30). Healthcare carries the densest handover surface in the book: shift to shift, ward to theatre, hospital to community, road to resuscitation room. me-006 carries an identity error from the prehospital crew into a duplicate record in the emergency department. me-005 leaves an abnormal chest film with no owner for twelve days. Every one of those surfaces is a candidate rupture, and the number of surfaces, not the diligence of the staff, sets the exposure.

    Compliant and unsafe (Chapter 32). The paperwork is done while the act is not. me-001's Stop Before You Block was signed off as the block went into the wrong leg. me-002 walked around the concentration coding system with an ordinary choice of syringe.

    The illusion of redundancy (Chapter 22). me-003 took out two defences in one event: the purple syringe that was meant to mark the route, and the second pair of eyes that was meant to catch it.

    Next in frequency, diffusion of responsibility (Chapter 18). me-008's incompatible implant came to light afterwards through a registry.

    5 What Outsiders Most Easily Mislearn

    Mislearning one: treating individual accountability as system improvement. Healthcare's review culture has been occupied for decades by the question of who was at fault, and the institutional innovation at HSSIB is the separation of investigation from blame. That structure is the transferable asset. No particular checklist is.

    Mislearning two: imagining that a hospital's strict processes are strict in execution. A checklist in use and a check performed are two different states, and the gap between them is where several cases in this book live (Chapter 32).

    Mislearning three: undervaluing the national figure. A single hospital's case teaches little on its own; the real contribution of an HSSIB report is a national frequency to divide by. Any lesson learned at one site should be read against that base before it is generalised.

    6 Three Questions from Readers Outside the Field

    Are the healthcare cases only for clinicians? No, and the reason is structural. Healthcare is the prototype site for decisions taken under time pressure, on incomplete information, with a lethal consequence attached, and the mechanisms transfer whole. A triage priority conflict and a medication check that breaks at a handover are shapes a trading desk or a control room meets as well.

    Will the hierarchy make the cases hard to follow? The twelve fields have already flattened clinical language into actions. A block injected into the wrong leg, a chest report that arrives twelve days late: the sequence is readable by anyone. Terminology stays in the source report for those who want it.

    Is the blame culture in medicine especially heavy? It varies widely by jurisdiction, and the second-victim problem, the injury done to the clinician who made the error, is a public topic in medicine earlier than anywhere else. The repair work on blame culture started here first, and its lessons travel (Chapter 19).

    8 How This Page Gets Updated

    A domain page is a live document. Once a year, take the Grade A and Grade B reports added to this domain, walk them against the five columns above and the table in Section 7, and revise. A mechanism that appears for the first time gets a line in Section 4. A shift in frequency updates the counts in Section 7. A new Grade A case goes into the library against the field standard of Chapter 5.

    The trigger, the owner, and the review date belong in the user's calendar (Chapter 39). A domain page that is not updated is dead in the same way a ledger is: the information does not rot, it simply stops describing the field.

    9 The Starter Kit

    Three items for a reader new to this domain, about one working day in total.

    The entry case is me-001, a nerve block for right ankle surgery injected into the left leg, investigated by HSIB (the predecessor of HSSIB) in 2018, with the link in Appendix B. Read it first for the format and the grain of the reports in this field.

    The entry source is the first-hand library listed under Healthcare in. Find the entrance, bookmark it, visit once a quarter.

    The entry mechanism is the first line of the Section 7 table. Read the mechanism chapter beside the case from this domain and take the pair twice, once for the criterion in the chapter and once for the parameters here. When the three items are done you own a first coordinate system for the field.

    10 Reservations

    Three limits apply to every judgement on this page. The book's case library samples toward events with rich public records, which is the record skew of Chapter 2. The frequency ranking in Section 7 describes the book's library, not the true distribution of the domain. Institutions and technology both move, so this page has a shelf life measured in years, and a citation should carry the year it was read.

    All three are the book's general disclaimer, and the threshold check of Chapter 1 applies to this book as much as to anything it examines.

    11 How the Three Layers Sound in This Domain

    The accent here is breakpoint one, the place where a technical signal never gets translated by the organizational layer, and it is the most crowded of the three. A clinical signal, whether a symptom, a laboratory value, or an imaging finding, reaches an organizational action such as a consultation, a referral, or a follow-up only through several translations: triage, handover, report routing. Each translation is a high-incidence breakpoint, and me-005's delayed chest film and me-016's broken discharge information both sit at one. The incentive layer takes the form of bed occupancy and turnover targets, with rewards going to the teams that move patients fastest, and that tension acts directly on handover quality.

    For the reviewer: in a healthcare nine-cell table the organizational layer usually holds the densest evidence, because the record system timestamps itself, while the technical layer often surfaces only in post-mortem pathology. Getting the timeline right is the whole of the walkthrough.

    13 Closing Address

    The last word to the governance layer in healthcare. The HSSIB reports are among the strongest accident analysis texts produced anywhere, and what they share is a method: no blame, and a system diagram instead of a verdict. The largest lesson in this book's healthcare cases belongs to system designers. me-005's ownership of follow-up, me-013's decision support, me-015's triage differential: in each case the defence lands on a structure, not on a person. That is the HSSIB method, and it is the spirit of the Systems Engineering Initiative for Patient Safety. A clinical reader who carries the structural analysis back to his own department has taken the chapter's delivery.

    The cases on this page come from independent HSIB investigation, and their common quality is a full reconstruction plus recommendations pitched at system level. The method, with its no-blame frame, its systems analysis, and its publicly tracked recommendations, is the most mature institutional experiment in safety that medicine has run, and these cases show what kind of knowledge product it yields. An HSIB report reads like a postmortem written with the nine-cell table; the three-layer analysis of Chapter 9 is its standard structure. For a medical student the practical use is to read these reports as an advanced form of case teaching. What is learned is systems thinking, and systems thinking is the safety net under clinical skill: the denser the net, the safer the patient.

    14 How the Mechanisms Vary in This Domain

    Handover rupture at maximum density. One inpatient admission passes through a dozen handovers (prehospital, emergency, ward, imaging, theatre, nursing shift), and every one of them reorganises the information once. me-005 and me-006 stand for the two lesions. In the first, a result has no owner and the loop never closes. In the second, the key is wrong and the record duplicates. The local measure is closure rate rather than record thickness: of the result-type messages sent out, how many were explicitly received and acted on within the window. Count it by department, and put Chapter 30's device wherever the rate falls below the threshold. Rupture in healthcare almost never presents as not knowing. It presents as assuming the other side knew.

    Normalization with a moral shell. "For the patient" is the most common licence for compressing a check in this domain. In me-011, where the piped oxygen supply fell short, the emergency itself exempted the verification steps. In me-016, where a three-month-old deteriorated after discharge, discharge criteria applied in bulk absorbed one infant's abnormality into the mean. The local instrument is a register with two columns, emergency exemption and convention exemption, counting every check skipped under the heading of urgency or of routine. The monthly totals are a thermometer for normalization. Exemptions cannot be abolished, since resuscitation is what it is, but each one must carry a name and a time. Anonymous exemption is the incubator.

    Truth-telling against a steeper gradient than any firm's. me-015 and me-016 both contain a junior doubt that was never escalated, and the authority structure of a medical team is what kept it where it was. The only reliable indicator of health here is the dissent record of departmental meetings: who raised a different view on which case, and what happened to it afterwards. An anonymous survey cannot measure a culture. Chapter 19's list of alarms has its healthcare counterpart in one question: is the difficult-case conference actually discussing the case, or endorsing it?

    15 Cross-Reading Suggestions

    Healthcare against maritime is the most surprising pair in the book. Both run small teams, high pressure, and explicit rank, and the bridge and the theatre turn out to share one problem in attention management: ma-002's alarm masked by music reads beside me-001's checklist degraded to a signature.

    Against Drugs and Devices: me-014's misread morphine concentration and the recalls in the ph series meet at the same design question, since the error space of a device label and the error space of a drug label are one space. Chapter 26 deserves conservative handling in this domain; me-013 shows what an absent decision support costs, and it does not follow that decision support is a cure.
