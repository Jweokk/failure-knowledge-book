    Military | mi

    Five columns: record quality, feedback delay, coupling, the three deadliest mechanisms, and what outsiders most easily mislearn. Public first-hand material is scarce in this domain. The case library holds one case (mi-001, a space mission provisionally filed here) and the judgements of the framework are marked as awaiting collection.

    1 Record Quality

    The thinnest public record in the book, and thin for institutional reasons: military accident investigations are mostly classified, and what is published comes as accident notices, declassified archives and select committee reports. The usable public samples are these. Independent investigation of space missions, where the Columbia Accident Investigation Board report is published in full and mi-001 is drawn from it, since NASA is a civil-military system and the investigation regime for crewed spaceflight is isomorphic with aviation's. Annual safety reports from national defence safety bodies, where the US military's safety centres publish statistics and lesson summaries while the deep case studies are mostly restricted. Historical declassified archives, with a time lag measured in decades. Collection directions are listed in (declassified archive collections and defence safety publications). Assessment: the structural judgement is trustworthy, in that the investigative institutions exist; the case material is still to be gathered.

    2 Feedback Delay

    Training and equipment operation give feedback on a mission cycle. Equipment acquisition gives feedback in decades, a heavier horizon mismatch than public infrastructure. Organisational culture gives feedback across generations. Crewed systems give feedback immediately and irreversibly, with the points of no return of Chapter 7 operating in their harshest form, which is why military aviation and spaceflight produced the world's strictest checklists and the Crew Resource Management regime. Institutional density runs in proportion to the irreversibility of the consequence.

    3 Coupling

    Coupling inside a mission system is high and peculiar. In combat and in training, communications, identification, the chain of command and the rules of engagement couple under pressure and on incomplete information. The 1994 Black Hawk shootdown is the prototype case for Snook's theory of practical drift (Chapter 15), where the divergences inside each link add up at an accidental intersection. Coupling across units is constrained by establishment and doctrine, and the interface in joint operations is a high-incidence break point (Chapter 30).

    4 The Three Deadliest Mechanisms (from CAIB and the Public Literature, Case Slots Pending)

    Normalization of deviance (Chapter 11): CAIB's review of the "in-family" classification of foam shedding (mi-001) is itself a specimen of normalization inside a military-grade organisation, and mission standards that move a step at a time through success are a disease of high-risk organisations generally. Diffusion of responsibility (Chapter 18): the separation of the command chain from the safety oversight chain, and committee-style mission review. Information attenuation up the hierarchy (Chapter 16): the filtering of upward information under mission pressure recurs throughout published military investigation reports. To be revised once the collection is done.

    5 What Outsiders Most Easily Mislearn

    Wrong lesson one: taking the availability of declassified files for the whole truth. What can be declassified has passed a double filter of time and classification, the military form of the record skew in Chapter 2, and statistical inference drawn from declassified material needs unusual care about its sample. Wrong lesson two: imagining military discipline as a vaccine against drift. Snook's finding runs the other way: drift inside a disciplined organisation takes the form of each link doing the right thing by its own logic, and discipline does not produce alignment by itself. Wrong lesson three: using a space case as an aviation case. The institutional isomorphism of CAIB and the NTSB, independent investigation, full publication, tracked recommendations, is worth learning, but the one-shot mission structure of spaceflight means its feedback cannot be compared directly with the repetitive operations of civil aviation.

    6 Three Questions from Readers Outside the Field

    Are military cases open to outside readers? What is public is the redacted version of an investigation report. CAIB was a congressionally mandated independent investigation and is published in full. The organisational and incentive-layer lessons transfer completely. Technical detail is abstract where classification applies, which does not impede learning the mechanism.

    Does a rank culture distort the lessons? Rank culture is the hardest testbed available for Chapter 19, on why the truth-teller goes first. CAIB's organisational analysis of Columbia reads most powerfully against exactly that background: a blameless review was still possible inside a strong rank culture, and the comparison drawn from the software industry shows this is not a cultural destiny.

    Where should a reader moving from military to civilian work start? With mi-001, Columbia. The report is a complete sample that reads outside the industry, and its organisational analysis is isomorphic with failure in any large organisation.

    10 Reservations

    Three limits apply to everything judged on this page. The book's case library samples toward events with rich public records, and the record skew is the subject of Chapter 2. The frequency ordering of mechanisms reflects the distribution inside this library, not the true distribution in the domain. Institutions and technology in the domain keep moving, so this page has a shelf life measured in years, and a reader quoting it should state the year of the quotation. All three limits are the book's general disclaimer, and the threshold check of Chapter 1 applies to the book itself as well.

    11 How the Three Layers Sound in the Military

    The three-layer accent in military cases is the highest rank density in the organisational layer. Every link in the chain of command is a translation, and Chapter 16's attenuation is institutionalised in military systems as the briefing. More translations do not mean information must decay, because the briefing format is a mature answer to translation loss: read-back, confirmation, standard phraseology. The civilian portability of the format is discussed in Chapters 16 and 30. For the reviewer: the technical-layer cell in a military case is often abstract because of classification, and abstract does not mean absent. Evidence in the organisational and incentive layers still carries the walk-through. Write "classified" in the cell, following the blank-cell discipline of Chapter 9.

    13 Closing Address

    A last word for readers of military cases: the public versions (CAIB, the military-related parts of JTSB) are the densest samples of organisational failure available. Rank culture, information pressure, cross-agency coordination, the whole set of difficulties is present. For a non-military reader their value is that of a mirror: how strong is your organisation's rank culture, how often has cross-agency coordination dropped the line, and does mi-001's organisational lesson have a counterpart where you work. The mechanism mapping here (Chapters 11, 13, 18, 19) is cross-industry public property, and classification blocks technical detail without blocking the structural lesson.

    This page holds one case, mi-001, Columbia, and the CAIB report, but that one case carries the weight of a small library. The report runs to ten volumes, and volume one alone is over 500 pages; the book cites pp. 39 and 122–129 of it. CAIB's organisational analysis chapters are the deepest writing on normalization of deviance anywhere in the book, and easier to read than Vaughan's original because they are verified after the fact. There is a second reason this page is thin. How public a military investigation is depends on who authorised it: a congressionally mandated investigation such as CAIB is published in full, while an investigation kept inside the military, such as av-019, the Xinjiang Yaxin general aviation report, was never published in full and could not enter the main text. The selection standard is therefore a double threshold, an accident large enough and an investigation open enough. What survives the double filter is the densest evidence in the book, and mi-001's weight was stated at the start of this page.

    14 How the Mechanisms Vary in This Domain

    The military variation of normalization of deviance: mi-001 is one of the origin cases of the book's work on normalization, and the distinctive form here is the flight rationale regime, in which risks that cannot be ruled out are written into the flight release document one at a time until the release document itself becomes the ledger of normalization. How to read the variation: the age distribution of "accepted risk" entries in the release documents, meaning how old the oldest entry is, is the domain's normalization thermometer. The older the entry, the more the risk assessment rests on past releases rather than current evidence. CAIB tabulated the flight-by-flight history of foam shedding (Chapter 11 cites p.124), which is precisely this thermometer made into an object.

    The military variation of narrative before evidence: the review culture around mi-001, where a foam strike was filed as a maintenance problem rather than a safety problem, shows the power of narrative classification. Which category a phenomenon is put into decides which processing pipeline it enters and what level of resource it can command. How to read the variation: does the risk classification standard provide a channel and a record for reclassification requests, who approves a downgraded classification, and on what evidence. The density of the paper trail on classification changes is the domain's measure of narrative immunity. The neighbouring av-017, a helicopter cleared for the helipad that landed on the runway, adds a second blind spot from a militarised organisation, academy-owned and under quasi-military management: the clearance travelled on a single-channel radio, and a clearance with no check is no clearance.

    The military variation of diffusion of responsibility: a multi-layer command structure is a natural hot spot for Chapter 18. In mi-001's lessons, jurisdiction over the foam problem circulated for years among the project office, the space shuttle programme and the contractor, and each layer could name a specific reason why the responsibility was not its own. How to read the variation: a single-claimant rule for cross-layer risk entries, where each entry carries one and only one name at or above a stated rank, is the targeted device for this domain. An entry that circulates for ten years with the same claimant is a healthy signal. A claimant that changes automatically as the organisation chart changes is a symptom.

    15 Cross-Reading Suggestions

    Read this page against aviation first (mi-001 and the av volume share the language of airworthiness and release, and aviation's mandatory reporting system is the clearest proof of the institutional domestication this page discusses). Against public policy: read mi-001's project governance beside the big projects of the po volume, where the governance defects of very large technical projects are isomorphic on both sides of the civil-military line. Against nuclear: the safety culture literatures of the two industries reference each other, with CAIB and the Chernobyl and Fukushima investigation reports citing one another, and readers of this page should take the independent verification variation in Section 14 of the nuclear page with them.
