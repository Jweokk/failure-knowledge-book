The collection holds 159 cases, and more than one case to a cell is the norm, since a single case can witness several mechanisms at once. Three ways to read it. Read a row: one mechanism's evidence across the domains, and the wider the spread, the more general the mechanism, with Hidden Coupling, The Invisibility of Maintenance and Practical Drift the three widest rows. Read a column: the mechanism density of one domain, and where a column is mostly blank the domain is either subject to recording bias (Chapter 2) or short of this book's sampling, so a reader testing a local sample should fill that column first. Read for blanks: a blank does not mean the mechanism is absent from that domain. It means the book holds no first-hand full text to put in the cell, and the reading method in Appendix C outranks the table. One last lesson in reading a table. The blanks in the matrix deserve more care than the IDs inside the cells, and every reading method in Appendix C applies to a blank. Before reading a blank, ask whether it is recording bias, a sampling gap, or a genuine absence of the mechanism. Three kinds of blank, three responses, and once the check is done the blanks have become the reader's own collection list.
