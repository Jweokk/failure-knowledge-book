# Healthcare | me

> Five sections: record quality, feedback delay, coupling, the three deadliest mechanisms, and what outsiders most easily mislearn.

## 1 Record Quality

The rising star of independent investigation is the United Kingdom: the Health Services Safety Investigations Body (HSSIB, formerly HSIB) conducts systemic investigations on the aviation model, with reports containing the event sequence, safety recommendations, and national-level figures (such as "roughly 192 wrong-site surgeries nationwide each year"); this domain's me-series cases come from its archive (the original site was unreachable from this book's collection network and was verified through Internet Archive snapshots; the record is in Appendix C). On the US side, AHRQ's Patient Safety Network (PSNet) and WebM&M offer case commentaries — secondary curation; the FDA's MAUDE device-adverse-event database covers the device side. On the China side, health-commission bulletins have limited availability, and adjudication documents in medical-damage liability disputes are the substitute channel (Chapter 3). Overall assessment: the United Kingdom leads in full-text investigation reports, with high narrative depth (complete event chains under patient-identity protection); single-case samples run small and need the national figures to supplement them.

## 2 Feedback Delay

Three layers. At the medication-and-surgery layer, feedback is measured in minutes (wrong site, wrong concentration, surfacing on the spot or within the day); at the process layer, in weeks and months (broken follow-up, results nobody claims — the 12-day reporting delay of me-005); at the system layer, in years (variations in reconciliation processes, patient-identification event rates). Healthcare's distinctiveness is the extreme unevenness of its feedback distribution: within a single nurse's day, second-scale and decade-scale risks arrive together.

## 3 Coupling

Intra-hospital coupling is moderate: processes are highly templated (which lets defects replicate isomorphically), but physical separation is comparatively ample. Industry-wide coupling is high: a process validated in one hospital gets copied nationwide, and the defects are copied with it (the medication-error patterns of me-001 through me-003 recur across HSIB's national figures). A national protocol (such as drug-administration color coding) is a single point; its failure is an industry-wide failure.

## 4 The Three Deadliest Mechanisms

Handoff discontinuities (Chapter 30): identity errors passed down the chain at prehospital handoff (me-006); imaging findings whose follow-up nobody claims (me-005). Healthcare has the world's densest handoffs — between shifts, departments, institutions, prehospital and in-hospital — and every handoff surface is a candidate discontinuity. Compliance without safety (Chapter 32): the checklist is in use while the verification does not happen (me-001's Stop Before You Block); the concentration coding system bypassed (me-002). The illusion of redundancy (Chapter 22): the purple syringes and the two-person check, two lines of defense failing in the same event (me-003). Next in frequency: diffusion of responsibility (me-008's implant incompatibility, discovered after the fact by a registry).

## 5 What Outsiders Most Easily Mislearn

Mislearning one: taking individual accountability for system improvement. Healthcare's postmortem culture has long been occupied by blaming individuals; HSSIB's institutional innovation is precisely the separation of investigation from blame. The structure, not any particular checklist, is what other industries should learn. Mislearning two: imagining that a hospital's strict processes are strict in execution. A checklist in use and a check performed are two different things (Chapter 32). Mislearning three: underestimating the value of the national figures. A single hospital's cases carry limited lessons; HSSIB reports' real contribution is establishing the national frequency baseline: any single-point learning should be divided by that base.

## 8 Mechanism Variations in This Domain

Handover rupture at maximum density: one admission passes through a dozen handovers, each re-organizing the information. The two NHS lessons mark the two病灶 — a result with no owner (the chest X-ray follow-up loop with no closure) and a key error (pre-hospital identity creating a duplicate record). The local metric is not record thickness but loop-closure rate: what share of issued results reach a named recipient with a disposition inside the window. In healthcare, rupture never looks like ignorance; it always looks like "I assumed they knew."

Normalization wears a moral coat unique to this domain: "for the patient" is the standing license to compress a check. Emergency resuscitation exempts verification steps by construction (the oxygen supply case); discharge criteria absorb the individual into the mean (the three-month-old readmitted within four hours). The instrument: register every check skipped as emergency-exempt or convention-exempt, count monthly, and require name and timestamp — anonymous exemption is the incubator.

Truth-telling runs against the steepest hierarchy in the book. The delayed dissection and the deteriorated infant both contain an unescalated junior concern. The only reliable instrument is the record of M&M-type conferences: who raised a dissent on which case, and what happened to them. Anonymous culture surveys cannot measure this; minutes can.
