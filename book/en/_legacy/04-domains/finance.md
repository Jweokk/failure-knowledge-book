> This page has five sections: record quality, feedback delay, coupling, the three deadliest mechanisms, and what outsiders most easily mislearn.

## 1 Record Quality

A gold mine of enforcement documents. The US SEC's litigation releases are published case by case, numbered and citable (5 fi cases in this book); FINRA's monthly disciplinary-action PDFs cover the mid and long tail of brokerage violations; the China Securities Regulatory Commission's administrative penalty decisions are published in full (4 fi cases in this book; article pages could be fetched, list pages are dynamically loaded — recorded in Appendix C). The UK FCA's final notices were unreachable from this book's network environment [to be verified]. The structural gap: enforcement documents record whoever was caught, while the accumulation of systemic risk (leverage, concentrations, liquidity mismatch) has almost no full-text record before it erupts; the material for a 2008-type event is a commission report written afterward, not supervision documents written before. Overall assessment: micro-level records of violations are extremely thick; macro-level records of risk depend on post-crisis confirmation.

## 2 Feedback Delay

Two clocks. The market clock: feedback on price and liquidity arrives in seconds (which creates the illusion of the high-frequency stop-loss: the feedback of most risk does not run on the market clock). The regulatory clock: feedback on violations arrives in years (fi-001: conduct period 2019–2021, penalty 2026; fi-004: bulk fabrication prosecuted 2025–2026); feedback on risk arrives in cycles (credit losses are invisible during the boom). The financial decision-maker's career incentives are mismatched across the two clocks in both directions: the bonus sits on the market clock, the penalty on the regulatory clock.

## 3 Coupling

The highest of any domain. Institutions are directly connected through interbank liabilities, clearing, and collateral networks (a default anywhere propagates along the clearing chain); assets are connected through shared positions (a "diversified" book that rises and falls together is pseudo-diversification); information is contagious through market sentiment in an instant. Finance's instruments of isolation (margin, clearinghouses, deposit insurance) are essentially a conversion of coupling from disorderly to orderly: coupling itself never disappears; it is institutionalized into a clearable form. The "suppliers" column of a risk register corresponds, in finance, to the counterparty, and Chapter 33's inventory is here a document of life and death.

## 4 The Three Deadliest Mechanisms

Misaligned incentives (Chapter 17): the tension inside audit independence (fi-008 and fi-009, two firms failing in succession on the same client); the structural conflict between sales and risk control. Goal displacement (Chapter 12): the cosmetic treatment of loan-loss metrics (fi-001); risk ratings diverging from actual risk in 24 of 53 cases (Chapter 12's GAO case, the public-sector edition of financial governance). Regulatory lag and capture (Chapter 31): fractional-share trading falling outside the old classification (fi-011); bulk registration fraud (fi-004); the compliance vacuum around AI financing narratives (fi-002). Next most frequent: narrative before evidence (the issuance fraud of fi-002 and fi-005); diffusion of responsibility (fi-007's four-signature chain).

## 5 What Outsiders Most Easily Mislearn

Mislearning one: treating penalty documents as evidence about morality. Enforcement documents record provable violations, not the whole of an organization's culture; using them to judge culture reproduces the sample bias of Chapter 2's survival skew. Mislearning two: ranking severity by the size of the fine (fi-013's $125,000 and fi-005's $17.78 million do not measure the same dimension). Mislearning three: believing diversification equals safety. Industry-wide synchronized "diversification" is a single exposure under stress (Chapter 33), and most of the simultaneous failures of diversified portfolios in financial history carry a common cause in the caption.

## 8 Mechanism Variations in This Domain

Goal displacement's original case lives here, with a doubling twist: the same number feeds internal appraisal and regulatory filing, so displacement distorts both loops at once. The local asset is the disclosure-obligation list — which metric calibers must be explained to investors when they move; the La Porte of this chapter failed at disclosure, not at the rescissions. The self-check: for every number you own, does a caliber-sensitivity note exist (which judgments move it, by how much)?

Narrative pressure here has a calendar: the raise. The deck is finalized and the narrative seals — which is why the AI-startup and cannabis cases read as one mechanism at two scales. The financial-grade check is the ratio of narrative intensity to evidence density in the offering document (Chapter 37's questions, in diligence mode). The shell-advisor cases add the industrialized form: when false filings can be manufactured by the batch, verification density must scale to production rate — which is what the SEC's sweep actions are.

Misaligned incentives wear the professional-intermediary form: audit fees flow from the audited. The two paired audit failures around one issuer are the structure speaking. The local observables: auditor-change frequency and reasons; quality curves over auditor tenure. And the compliance desk carries its own incentive layer — the cost of the compliance action is immediate, the probability-weighted penalty is not — which is what monthly published enforcement exists to correct.
