> Five sections: record quality, feedback delay, coupling, the three deadliest mechanisms, and what outsiders most easily mislearn.

## 1 Record Quality

The mandatory-reporting-plus-public-database form (Chapter 3). The US FDA has the most complete primary channel: recalls and enforcement reports are programmatically retrievable through the openFDA interface (three databases — drugs, devices, food; every ph-series case in this book was verified against API records), and the MAUDE device-adverse-event database varies in per-entry quality but not in volume; FDA safety communications that include an investigation timeline can be graded strong. Europe goes through EMA and the national agencies. China's NMPA bulletins, recalls, and adverse-reaction annual reports are the regular outlet, thinner in per-entry detail. Overall assessment: signals abundant, postmortems scarce — this domain records the "event" and rarely records the "why" (Chapter 3, Section 5: signal detection substituting for accident investigation).

## 2 Feedback Delay

The industry's structural feature is extremely long delay: clinical-trial feedback is measured in years, post-marketing risk feedback in years (an adverse-reaction signal needs epidemiological verification to move from accumulation to confirmation), and device fatigue and aging feedback is tied to intensity of use. Feedback in distribution and use runs faster (recall execution, adverse-event reporting), but the reporting rate itself is perturbed by awareness and by how hard causality is to judge. The decision maker's common illusion is reading "no signal yet" as "no risk yet."

## 3 Coupling

Global distribution is global synchronized exposure: one device enters multiple countries through reprocessing protocols (ph-007); one component's packaging defect travels with the kit into hospitals across the United States (ph-008). Regulatory coupling comes next: a single licensing decision (or its absence, ph-005) is exported along the distribution network to every market that recognizes the license or lacks a corresponding review. Production coupling concentrates in sterility assurance: failure of sterility is batch-level and crosses enterprise boundaries (the kit cases of Chapter 33).

## 4 The Three Deadliest Mechanisms

Boundary extrapolation (Chapter 23): single-use devices reused through reprocessing (ph-007); unlicensed models entering distribution (ph-005); device mismatches in the world of concentration scales (isomorphic with me-002 on the healthcare side). Coupled failure (Chapter 33): the kit model lets a component defect propagate across enterprises (ph-008). Regulatory lag and capture (Chapter 31): marketing without a license; reporting lag (ph-004's two years); new forms falling outside old classifications. Next in frequency: normalization of deviance (ph-001's missing sterility assurance, invisible before the consequence).

## 5 What Outsiders Most Easily Mislearn

Mislearning one: treating recall databases as safety rankings. Reporting culture, adjudication standards, and product usage volume are all unstandardized; comparing recall counts across firms or countries is meaningless. Mislearning two: taking "no adverse event reports" as evidence of safety (in a domain where the reporting rate sits far below the true rate, silence means nothing). Mislearning three: taking the Class grading as the size of the loss (Class characterizes a severity assumption about health hazard, not the actual scale of harm: small quantity with wide exposure and large quantity with narrow exposure are arithmetically different worlds — ph-004's two vials and mn-006's 5.9 million units are the two ends of the same page of the manual).

## 8 Mechanism Variations in This Domain

Compliance without safety takes a temporal form: the certificate covers an instant while the exposure runs a period. The sterile-assurance gap sat in production batches while the inspector sat elsewhere; the Tyvek seal voids emerged in packaging-line drift that batch sampling under-sampled. The local measure is sampling density against process-parameter drift — and note the inversion that offends outsider intuition: a jurisdiction with many recalls is usually one whose system is working; recall visibility is system sensitivity (record skew, positive form).

Boundary extrapolation lives in the cold chain. The enoxaparin excursion is the shape of it: registration studies cover one distribution of conditions; the real logistics network runs another, and exposure duration — not excursion count — determines the quality consequence. If the temperature data reports excursions but not durations against batches, the extrapolation risk is invisible. The domain's difference list (Chapter 23, Device 1) is a registered-conditions-versus-actual-logistics table, refreshed yearly.

For devices, maintenance invisibility becomes reprocessing validation aging: the trocar validated for N reuses, reused past N, with no counter anywhere. The local weak signal is a reprocessing-count ledger against the validation ceiling. Read it next to the forty-year-old French press: validation has a shelf life — devices count it in cycles, consumer goods in years.
