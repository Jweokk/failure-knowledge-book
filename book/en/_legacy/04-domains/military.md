# Military | mi

> This page has five sections: record quality, feedback delay, coupling, the three deadliest mechanisms, and what outsiders most easily mislearn. Public firsthand material is scarce in this domain; the case library holds one entry (mi-001, a spaceflight mission provisionally filed here), and framework judgments are marked as pending collection and verification.

## 1 Record Quality

The thinnest public record of any domain, thin for institutional reasons: military accident investigations are mostly classified, and the public stock consists mainly of incident notices, declassified archives, and special commission reports. Usable public samples: independent investigations of spaceflight missions (the Columbia accident investigation, CAIB, published in full; this book's mi-001 comes from it — NASA is a military-civilian dual-use system, and the investigation regime for crewed spaceflight is isomorphic with aviation's); the annual safety reports of national defense safety organizations (the US military's safety centers publish statistics and lessons-learned summaries; in-depth case studies are mostly in restricted circulation); historical declassified archives (lags measured in decades). For supplementary collection see Appendix C.6 (declassified archives and the public publications of defense safety organizations). Overall assessment: structural judgments are credible (the investigation institutions exist); case material awaits collection.

## 2 Feedback Delay

Feedback on training and equipment operation arrives in task cycles; feedback on equipment acquisition in decades (a heavier horizon mismatch than public infrastructure); feedback on organizational culture in generations. For crewed systems the feedback is immediate and irreversible (Chapter 7's point of irreversibility running in its harshest form) — which is why military aviation and spaceflight developed the world's strictest checklists and the institutions of Crew Resource Management: institutional density is proportional to the irreversibility of the consequences.

## 3 Coupling

Mission systems carry high and distinctive coupling: in combat and training environments, communications, identification, the chain of command, and the rules of engagement couple under pressure and incomplete information (the 1994 Black Hawk shootdown is the prototype case of Snook's practical drift — Chapter 15 — the divergences of individual links stacking up in a chance encounter). Coupling across units is constrained by force structure and doctrine, and the interfaces of joint operations are where breaks cluster (Chapter 30).

## 4 The Three Deadliest Mechanisms (judgments based on the CAIB report and the open literature; case slots to be filled)

Normalization of deviance (Chapter 11): the CAIB's review of the foam-shed classification as "within the family" (mi-001) is itself a specimen of normalization in a military-grade organization; the stepwise migration of training and mission standards under success is an occupational disease of high-risk organizations. Diffusion of responsibility (Chapter 18): the separation of the chain of command from the chain of safety oversight; committee-style mission reviews. Information attenuation through hierarchy (Chapter 16): upward filtering of information under mission pressure recurs throughout public military investigation reports. ## 5 What Outsiders Most Easily Mislearn

Mislearning one: taking the availability of declassified archives for the whole of the truth. Whatever can be declassified has been screened twice, by time and by classification (the military-strength version of Chapter 2's record skew); statistical inference from declassified material requires unusual care with the sample. Mislearning two: imagining military discipline as a vaccine against drift. The lesson of the Snook study is the exact opposite: in disciplined organizations, drift takes the form of each link doing right by its own local logic — discipline does not automatically produce alignment. Mislearning three: using spaceflight cases as aviation cases. The institutional isomorphism of the CAIB and the NTSB — independent investigation, full publication, tracked recommendations — is worth learning, but spaceflight's one-shot mission structure means its feedback cannot be compared directly with the repeated operations of civil aviation.

## 8 Mechanism Variations in This Domain

Normalization's founding case is filed here, in its most institutional form: the flight-reason system. Unexcludable risks were written into release documents flight by flight — the release file itself became normalization's ledger. The local instrument is the age distribution of "accepted risk" entries in current release paperwork: the older the entries, the more the risk assessment leans on precedent instead of evidence. The CAIB's flight-by-flight foam table was this thermometer rendered as a physical object.

Narrative classification shows its sharpest form: the same phenomenon — a foam strike — filed under maintenance rather than safety, routed to a pipeline with fewer resources and no stop authority. The local check: does the classification standard have a reclassification channel with a record (who downgraded, on what evidence)? Classification-change trace density is the domain's narrative immunity reading. The mis-landing case adds the rank-communication blind spot: a permission transmitted on one channel, with no read-back, is not a permission — it is a hope.

Responsibility dilution is structural in multi-layer command. The foam issue traveled between program office, shuttle program, and contractors for years, each layer able to state precisely why it was not the owner. The local device is the single-claimant rule: every risk entry carries exactly one name above a threshold rank, and the entry's health signal is that the name survives reorganizations — churn in owners is the disease, not the cure.
