## 1 Record Quality

The deepest institutional system in the world, with openness at two poles. Mandatory reporting and international sharing: the Incident Reporting System (IRS) is jointly operated by the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) and the OECD Nuclear Energy Agency, into which member states feed operational events for shared use; the US NRC requires licensees to submit reports on operating events (10 CFR Part 50.73). The closed half: the reporting and evaluation system of the World Association of Nuclear Operators (WANO) is closed-source, circulating inside the industry and invisible from outside. What the open world offers is the regulators' own documents — NRC inspection reports and event reports, French ASN bulletins — with the depth of detail set by the country concerned (retrieval channels and collection directions in Appendix C.6).

## 2 Feedback Delay

The longest of any domain in this book. Feedback on design defects and material aging is measured in decades; feedback on operating events in months, filtered through layers of review; the consequences of organizational and safety-culture problems run longer still — the lessons of Chernobyl and Fukushima each took on the order of decades to enter operating practice. The only short feedback loop sits at the training and simulator layer. This domain has almost no fast feedback: all learning depends on institutionalized memory (Chapter 30).

## 3 Coupling

Perrow judged nuclear power the prototype system scoring high on both interactive complexity and tight coupling: subsystem interconnections exceed anyone's complete understanding, and faults propagate faster than intervention. Seventy years of defense amount to taming the coupling inside multiple physical barriers and defense in depth; nuclear power is therefore the second exemplar of "high coupling institutionally suppressed," alongside aviation, with different means of suppression — aviation relies on the investigation system, nuclear power on physical barriers plus redundancy. Cross-plant coupling is low (sites are independent); cross-generation coupling is high (design lineages, standards lineages, and lesson sharing depend on international systems).

## 5 What Outsiders Most Easily Mislearn

Mislearning one: reading "WANO is not public" as "no failure records exist." The internal record density of the nuclear industry may be the world's highest — it is simply not open to outsiders. Taking it to mean "nuclear power has no learnable material" is a double misjudgment. Mislearning two: treating INES levels as a linear scale of accident severity (they are logarithmic, and weighted toward international transboundary impact rather than loss). Mislearning three: taking defense-in-depth's surface layers for the actual defense line: in the nuclear context, Chapter 22's common-cause check is a life-and-death question, not a compliance exercise.

## 8 Mechanism Variations in This Domain

Practical drift lives almost entirely at the document-to-action interface, and the interface is the tag-out. Both Gravelines cases share one shape: the tag sheet's logic correct, the field correspondence wrong — a wrong valve, a wrong cabinet — with the compensating measure recorded and nobody physically verifying. The generic drift criterion "rules nobody can follow" barely exists in nuclear, which is rule-saturated; the local criterion reads: "rules complete, verification steps skipped by habit." The check is whether verification has a second pair of independent eyes — a written requirement whose execution density is the real variable.

Normalization's home ground is the temporary-dispatch exemption. Tech specs set hard unavailability limits (24 hours for a standby diesel), beyond which the unit must shut down or power down — so "restore at hour 23:50" becomes routine. The local instrument: distribution of recovery times inside the window; a spike in the last hour of the allowed window means the limit has become a target (Goal Displacement's mirror image). Ledger states get a fourth field: how it was restored — in fact or on paper.

Invisible maintenance runs inverted here: the thickest maintenance ledgers in industry, and the thinnest verification ledgers. The EMSE lesson — a lost uranium sample, an unregistered americium source — is ledger upkeep on sources and samples resting on individual conscience, with no count-reconcile mechanism. The correct indicator is a three-way audit (on the books, on the shelf, on the shelf but not the books) at a fixed frequency; maintenance records without count-reconciliation are thick and empty.
