# Chapter 34: Path Dependence and Lock-in (David; Arthur)

> What this chapter covers: how an early, often accidental choice grows into a structure you cannot leave; David's and Arthur's classic theories, the criteria, two field sites plus an evidence-gap note, and the practice of the "from-zero replay."
> When to return: at selection reviews, in standards battles, and whenever "we can't afford to switch" has become a premise of the discussion.
> Reading time: 45 minutes.

Path lock-in is the only mechanism in this book in which no accident occurs at all: no casualties, no penalties, no investigation report; its cost is the sum of every alternative that never happened. Paul David used the century-long history of the QWERTY keyboard to show that a layout originally designed to solve a typewriter's mechanical jamming went on ruling the electronic keyboard for a hundred years after the jamming problem disappeared (David, "Clio and the Economics of QWERTY," American Economic Review 75(2): 332–337, 1985). Brian Arthur proved the generality of such lock-in with a mathematical model: increasing returns compound the early leader's advantage over time (Arthur, "Competing Technologies, Increasing Returns, and Lock-In by Historical Events," Economic Journal 99(394): 116–131, 1989; the Arthur citation has been verified against Crossref). Lock-in is not the same as error: the locked path is often performing well. The disease lies not in what was chosen but in the loss of the capacity to choose again.

## 1 What It Is

Definition: path lock-in means an early (often accidental) choice, through network effects, learning costs, complementary investment, and institutional fit, reinforces itself over time until the feasible space of the alternatives shrinks to nothing.

Three trigger criteria (if any one appears, the mechanism has already started):

① Alternatives no longer appear in selection discussions (not rejected — unmentioned);
② Migration costs only ever rise, and have never been re-estimated: every "we can't afford to switch" cites an estimate made years ago;
③ "We've always used this" has completed its promotion from a statement of fact to a statement of reasons.

## 2 How It Grows

Three generating conditions. Condition one: increasing returns. The more it is used, the better it works (ecosystems, plug-ins, talent pools, compatible parts), and the leader's advantage compounds over time (Arthur's core argument). Condition two: complementary investment hardens. The training, tools, contracts, and downstream interfaces built around the early choice are each an invisible tax on migration; each looks small alone, and together they form a wall of cost. Condition three: the atrophy of evaluation. After years without a selection exercise, the selection muscle (market scanning, files on alternatives) degrades; when the need finally comes, the organisation can no longer evaluate, and so inclines all the more to not switch. The three conditions are each other's cause and effect: what gets locked is not the system but the organisation's faculty of evaluation.

## 3 Field Sites

### Case 1 (Public): The Railway Locked In Ahead of Its Own Cancellation

po-004. Britain's High Speed Two (HS2). NAO report (High Speed Two reset, June 2026; reviewed for this book): since its establishment the project has passed through the 2020 reset, the 2023 cancellation of Phase Two, and the reset of January 2025, with 46.8 billion pounds spent and a potential cost of up to 102.7 billion pounds. Path lock-in appears here in its heaviest form: every inch of route, contract, and land purchase thickens the established path; after Phase Two's cancellation, every decision to continue Phase One cites the accomplished fact — the project's own bulk has become its largest argument. Chapter 27 (sunk cost and escalation) handles "why continue"; this chapter handles the fact that "continue with what" long ago stopped having any other option: once every kilometre of the line has grown into contracts, land deals, and political commitments, the feasible space of alternatives has shrunk to zero.

Stop at the decision point: the review season in which Phase Two was cancelled and Phase One's scope was redefined. On the table: the infrastructure investment already in the ground, the contracts and land purchases that have grown around the fixed route, and one question not on the table: if the railway network were planned from zero today, would this line still have this shape? 〔Outcome〕Phase One continued and the cost ceiling was raised. Primary source: NAO, High Speed Two reset, 2026; link in Appendix B, po-004.

### Case 2 (Manufacturing): The Product History of Five Million Glass Beakers

mn-006. Bodum USA French press coffee makers. CPSC recall announcement (reviewed for this book): 5.9 million French presses with replaceable glass beakers recalled, against 70 reports of breakage in use, 48 of them with burns; the product has been on sale since 1974. Across half a century of product iteration, the glass beaker as a replaceable part has been carried forward for fifty years: damage accumulates invisibly, the replacement-part ecosystem is mature, and user habit and brand perception have all grown around the one design. The lock-in here is benign in appearance: no design-defect-level accusation attaches to the product — but fifty years of sales history meant that "re-evaluating the beaker's material and failure modes" never entered the agenda, until 48 burns wrote it onto the agenda.

Stop at the decision point: any product-revision review — the last time the glass solution stood on the same stage as alternative materials may be many years back. On the table: replacement-part sales data, supplier relationships, brand consistency, and the fact that alternative materials were never seriously quoted. 〔Outcome〕5.9 million units recalled; 48 burns had already occurred. Primary source: CPSC, 2026; link in Appendix B, mn-006.

### Case 3 (Aviation): The Design Legacy of a Two-Stroke Engine

av-029. Ultralight aircraft fitted with ROTAX two-stroke engines (spark plugs facing down). The Spanish Aviation Safety and Security Agency's (AESA) safety bulletin BIS 01/24 (released 8 October 2024; the PDF was downloaded and reviewed for this book) reports that these engines' lubrication systems can be mis-installed, leading to multiple engine failures. The bulletin is two pages long and carries no per-case casualty figures (no public quantification); what it records is a design legacy: the spark-plug-down two-stroke configuration persists in ultralight aviation as the path continuation of one generation of types after another, the lubrication system's installation sensitivity is handed down with the configuration, and each generation of users faces only the one aircraft in front of them.

Stop at the decision point: every type certification of a new aircraft of the same configuration or approval of a modification — the moment at which past bulletins on lubrication-system installation errors either were or were not consulted. On the table: the certification requirements' line items, the archive of past notifications (AESA issued the consolidated bulletin only in 2024), and the individual owner's confidence that "my installation is fine." 〔Outcome〕AESA published the bulletin to alert the industry; the bulletin carries no binding force, and case-level disposition rests on operators' own initiative. Primary source: AESA, BIS 01/24, 2024; link in Appendix B, av-029.

### Evidence Gap Note

Deep cases of lock-in face one evidentiary difficulty: lock-in usually produces no accident report (its cost is opportunity, not casualties), and the strong cases in this chapter's library concentrate in two classes, public infrastructure and long-lived consumer goods. The primary documents of technical standards battles (historic power-transmission systems, videotape formats) mostly sit in the archives of the contending parties. The gap is marked honestly; supplementary collection directions: standards bodies' controversy archives and switching-cost arguments in antitrust reviews (Appendix C).

## 4 What Shows Earliest

Six weak signals, each with the reason that usually overrides it.

1. The agenda of the selection meeting contains no line for alternatives. Override: "there is nothing to choose; no point discussing it."
2. The talk of migration cost cites numbers from years ago. Override: "we evaluated it back then."
3. New projects inherit the old selection by default, with no argument required. Override: "consistency."
4. Attrition from the supplier or the technology (people, compatible parts) begins, and nothing is done. Override: "market fluctuations."
5. The organisation shows irritation at the hypothetical question "if we started from zero." Override: "a meaningless question."
6. The locked object's defects are systematically re-described as "features" or "industry practice." Override: "being pragmatic."

## 5 How to Defend

Device one: the from-zero replay. Once a year, a paper replay of the core selections: "If we started from zero today, what would we choose? At what price difference?" The replay promises no switch; it guarantees only that the faculty of evaluation does not atrophy. Its output is an annual difference list, and the speed at which the difference grows is the speed at which lock-in deepens — or at which the old choice's rationality is being reconfirmed.

Device two: re-estimating migration cost. Migration cost is re-estimated every two years and recorded: which costs have been amortised away, which technologies have made migration easier. Most "we can't afford it" becomes, after re-estimation, "we can afford it but it is troublesome" — and between troublesome and impossible lies the whole decision space.

Device three: exit and compatibility clauses up front. New selections write the exit-cost ceiling and the openness requirements for data and interfaces into the contract and the architecture: the depth of lock-in is priced on signing day — half a page of clauses up front beats a feasibility study ten years later.

Device four: a file of alternatives. For key selections, keep an archive of "the living outside world": one page a year per alternative technology (maturity, price, adopters). The file's reader is not the decision meeting; it is your future self. The opposite of lock-in is not switching — it is knowing there is something to switch to.

## 6 Boundaries with Adjacent Mechanisms

Against sunk cost and escalation (Chapter 27): lock-in explains the structure (why it cannot be switched); escalation explains the behaviour (why the investment continues). HS2 is claimed by both chapters: Chapter 27 governs the "continue" at each reset; this chapter governs the fact that "continue with what" long ago stopped having any other option.

Against the failure of success (Chapter 20): slack lets defensive investment atrophy; lock-in lets evaluative investment atrophy — both are by-products of "no bad news." Chapter 20's output ledger and this chapter's alternatives file are two line items of the same institution.

Against the rationality of path dependence: it must be said honestly that lock-in has returns (ecosystem stability, deep optimisation, compatibility dividends), and Chapter 37's question sets, group 34, specifically require that "the returns of lock-in be entered in the ledger." This chapter's defences promise no unlocking; they promise only that the option to unlock stays visible.

## 7 Self-Check: Five Questions

1. For your organisation's most core selection, when did an alternative last appear on a meeting agenda?
2. The "we can't afford to switch" estimate — which year's numbers does it cite? Has it been re-estimated?
3. Of your own post's skills, how many are bound to one particular platform or technology? Can you tell which of that is an asset and which is lock-in?
4. Has the from-zero replay (once a year, on paper) ever been run in your organisation? If not, who is stopping it?
5. Among the things you use that work "well enough," which have you not seriously compared in five years? Spend twenty minutes comparing one today; whatever the conclusion, it is worth it.

## 6bis Installing the Devices: Order, Failure Modes, and Cost

The from-zero rehearsal goes first — the thought experiment: one meeting, the largest shock, and the most reusable output. Migration-cost re-estimation follows (it prices the lock), the alternatives archive third (it prevents "nobody remembered another way existed"), and exit-and-compatibility clauses last (facing the next choice: today's contracts and architectures decide whether turning is possible in three years). First ninety days: week one, hold a from-zero session on the heaviest path (one agenda: starting from zero today, would we choose this — no positions assigned in advance; the disagreement is the record); weeks two and three, re-estimate migration cost on four dimensions — data, skills, contracts, customer habit — laid beside the estimate from three years ago (a migration cost that only ever rose, never re-checked, is the depth gauge of the lock); month two, open the alternatives archive (three alternatives to the current path: minimum viable prototype routes, switching triggers, a named maintainer; refreshed twice a year); month three, write exit and compatibility clauses into two new contracts and one new design (data portability, open interfaces, non-exclusive licensing — standard sentences).

Failure modes. The rehearsal always vindicates (every session proves the present choice wise; the rehearsal becomes a loyalty ceremony) — appoint a devil's chair whose sole job is arguing "from zero we would not choose this" (Chapter 13's refuter seat, same lineage; their output has no pass threshold). Re-estimates drift in caliber (two estimates, three years apart, different units — the trend unreadable) — lock the four-dimension calibers at first registration; only the numbers may move. The archive becomes a desert (built, unmaintained; the maintainer moves on and the archive orphans) — bind the archive to the rehearsal: the session reads it yearly, and an archive that survives the reading unchanged is invalid. Exit clauses turn hollow (a termination clause with penalties equal to the full contract value — a door painted on the wall) — economic review at signing: true exit cost over contract value goes into the negotiation record; a ratio near one means no exit.

Cost: the rehearsal is half a day a year; re-estimation is one to two weeks one-time then a day or two yearly; the archive costs half a day twice a year; exit clauses are negotiation cost. Against a rail project paying for its path reset in billions, a French press whose forty-year-old material path finally fractured in users' hands, or maintenance economics locked to an engine family — lock-in's bill is only ever presented at departure, and these devices do one thing: show the organization where the doors are while it can still walk.

Toolchain: the locator triggers on "selection age five-plus plus no alternative has appeared in selection discussions for years." Question set 34's item 1 ("when was the last from-zero run") audits the rehearsal. Post-mortems concluding "there was no other choice" feed the alternatives archive — no other choice usually means nobody was maintaining the memory of one. And Chapter 40's exploration declarations and the archive are two faces of one thing: the alternatives lying in the archive are the ready-made propositions for the next exploration declaration.
