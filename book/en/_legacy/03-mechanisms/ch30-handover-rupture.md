# Chapter 30: Handover Rupture; Organizational Forgetting

> What this chapter covers: where knowledge falls between one pair of hands and the next; Polanyi's tacit knowledge, the criteria, four field sites, the three-part handover template, and the shadow period.
> When to return: at personnel changes, project transfers, shift handovers, and supplier changes.
> Reading time: 50 minutes.

Michael Polanyi's epistemological proposition — "we know more than we can tell" (tacit knowledge) — is redeemed at every personnel change, shift handover, and supplier switch inside an organisation. Only one layer of knowledge can be written into handover documents: parameters, states, conclusions. The judgement, the hand-feel, and most of "why we did it this way in the first place" live in the person; when the person leaves, the paper is promoted to the whole, and everything beneath the paper is lost in transmission. What is special about the handover rupture is its painlessness: at the moment the rupture occurs there is no symptom — every item has been handed over, every item signed for — and the knowledge fell at the instant the departing person turned around.

## 1 What It Is

Definition: the handover rupture means critical knowledge is lost in transmission along the chain. It has two forms: the horizontal distortion of shift and stage handovers (information fails to travel between peers) and the vertical loss of personnel turnover (organizational forgetting — judgement and lessons leave with the person).

Three trigger criteria (if any one appears, the mechanism has already started):

① For a critical process, "why we do it this way" can be answered by no one, or the answer lives only in one person's head;
② The handover transfers documents and states but not judgement and lessons (the column "what has gone wrong here before" is missing);
③ After a veteran leaves, answers to the same question begin to diverge into versions.

## 2 How It Grows

Three generating conditions. Condition one: documents record only conclusions. Procedures write "what to do" but not "why" and not "what went wrong here in the old days"; the omission is usually caused by schedule pressure and by "it's obvious" — and the obvious is precisely what is easiest to lose. Condition two: staff turn over faster than documents update. Knowledge drains away and the paper never fills in; turnover rate multiplied by the tacit share of knowledge is the annual volume of loss. Condition three: the project-form organisation. Teams assemble temporarily and disband on completion, and cross-project memory has no institutional container; every project's end is a small organisational extinction.

The difference between the rupture and Chapter 16 (hierarchical attenuation) lies here: attenuation is a message deforming in vertical transmission; the rupture is knowledge falling out of horizontal and diachronic transmission. The common root of both is that the bandwidth of transmission has been compressed until only the conclusion survives.

## 3 The Mechanism in Four Domains

### Case 1 (Aviation): Two Understandings Short of the Stop Line

av-037. Japan Airlines Flight 516 (A350-941, JA13XJ) and the Japan Coast Guard DHC-8-315 (JA722A), 2 January 2024, Tokyo Haneda Airport. JTSB second interim report (released 25 December 2025; the PDF was downloaded and reviewed for this book): 5 coast guard crew died and the coast guard captain was seriously injured; all 379 aboard the airliner evacuated, with 1 passenger seriously injured and 4 lightly injured. The two crews' understandings of the runway state and the instructions did not coincide in the final moments before the collision; the transmission and read-back of the control instruction and the judgement at the stop line are the core passages the interim report continues to test. The handover load at this site was double: civil aviation and the coast guard are two operating cultures with two communication habits, and every radio transmission across the institutional boundary carried a doubled risk of interpretation. The final report has not yet been released (Chapter 3 uses this case to illustrate the time scale of investigations); the interim reports continue to be updated.

Stop at the decision point: the minute-scale window in which the coast guard aircraft entered the runway and each side made its own confirmation. On the table: the instruction and the read-back on the radio, the visual conditions on the runway, and each side's understanding of where "clearance" ended. 〔Outcome〕Collision and fire. Primary source: JTSB, second interim report, 2025; link in Appendix B, av-037.

### Case 2 (Healthcare): Three Stations of a Mistaken Identity

me-006. An NHS emergency chain in England. HSIB pilot investigation (Local integrated investigation pilot 2: Incorrect patient details on handover, 2022; archived snapshot reviewed for this book): a 93-year-old with a hip fracture was handed over from pre-hospital care with wrong identity details; a wrong record was created in the emergency department, and the error travelled down the in-hospital chain — surfacing only when a blood check before transfusion found the blood group incompatible. The hospital recorded 192 similar identification events within one year. The handover rupture here is literal: a handwritten sheet of pre-hospital information was received by every later station as a verified fact; each station extended natural trust to the one before it, the trust carried no discount along the vertical chain, and there was no re-check point.

Stop at the decision point: the few minutes in which emergency reception entered the pre-hospital information into the system. On the table: a patient who could not be verified, a busy triage desk, and the entirely legitimate priority of "treat first, verify later." 〔Outcome〕The wrong record was not caught on the spot; the report issued 5 local safety recommendations. Primary source: HSIB, 2022; link in Appendix B, me-006 (archived link, grade A−).

### Case 3 (Pharmaceuticals): Two Strengths in One Bottle

ph-003. Mixed Amphetamine Salts Tablets 5 mg, Teva Pharmaceuticals. FDA recall record (D-1285-2020; accessed via API and reviewed for this book): bottles were found to contain tablets of mixed strengths; 33,280 bottles recalled (Class II). On a packaging line there is a handover interface between batch change and strength change: clearance confirmation, material verification, first-article inspection. Where exactly the rupture sat was for the investigation to delimit; its type was textbook: information about the previous state (what remained in the line) did not pass intact to the operation of the next state. A production line's handover happens not between people but between states, which makes it stealthier than a shift handover: no shift briefing exists to raise the alarm; only the records can speak.

Stop at the decision point: the handover moment of switching strengths — clearance and first-article confirmation. On the table: the ticks on the clearance record, the visual similarity of the two strengths, and the pressure of the production schedule. 〔Outcome〕Class II recall. Primary source: FDA, D-1285-2020, 2020; link in Appendix B, ph-003.

### Case 4 (Agriculture): The Allergen That Never Reached the Label

ag-009. The 19th Hole Snack Mix (Better Business...Better Foods, Inc.). FDA recall record (event 96758 / F-0801-2025; accessed via API and reviewed for this book): the product contained undeclared almonds and sesame; 18,000 units recalled (Class II). Between the information of a recipe change and the process of label revision lies a rupture: allergen declaration depends on "the recipe room and the label room speaking the same language," and if a single change notice fails to arrive, the rupture becomes the product on the shelf. The consequence of a mis-declared allergen is priced by the most sensitive consumer: a person allergic to almonds or sesame does not need to misread an ingredient list — only the label needs to stay unupdated.

Stop at the decision point: the moment the recipe revision is finalised and the label artwork moves through its approval flow. On the table: the new recipe's ingredient list, the stock of old labels, and the distribution list for change notices. 〔Outcome〕Class II recall. Primary source: FDA, F-0801-2025, 2025; link in Appendix B, ag-009.

## 4 What Shows Earliest

Six weak signals, each with the reason that usually overrides it.

1. A newcomer asks "why" and receives "that's how it's always been." Override: "get them productive first."
2. The handover checklist holds only states and parameters, no "what has gone wrong here before." Override: "that belongs to the post-mortem."
3. The same question has different answers from different veterans, each claiming theirs is the standard practice. Override: "everyone has their own experience."
4. The handover period for a key post is under a week, or does not exist. Override: "the business cannot wait."
5. After a supplier or partner is replaced, the wording of quality complaints changes though the count does not. Override: "a bedding-in period."
6. The organisation repeats the saying "things went wrong after so-and-so left." Override: "hero worship." (Read the other way: hero worship is the symptom of an organisational rupture, not its cause.)

## 5 How to Defend

Device one: the three-part handover template. The handover document is fixed at three parts: state (current parameters and open items), why (the origin and evolution of the key settings), and what has gone wrong here before (this station's incidents and near misses, each with its post-mortem reference). Parts two and three are the rupture's high-incidence zone; a missing part means no signature. A drafting point for the template: parts two and three carry minimum line counts — blank means non-conforming, because "there is no why" and "nothing has ever gone wrong" are themselves statements that require verification.

Device two: the shadow period. For key posts, a double-occupancy period of no less than two weeks, in which the successor operates independently while the outgoing side supervises without substituting: the rupture surfaces at its cheapest during the shadow period. The shadow period's pass standard is not "the handover was completed" but "the successor completed N full operations independently." Where doubling is impossible — single-boat fishing crews, one-person departments — a written question-and-answer list substitutes: the successor asks, the departing answer in writing, and the Q&A record is the handover document.

Device three: continuity of decision records. Once Chapter 7's three-line decision records (irreversible point, confirmation seat, what was visible at the time) are archived in sequence, they form the core of the handover pack: a successor reading ten decision records beats ten pages of state description. Decision records turn the handover from "I tell, you listen" into "you read the table as it stood" — the lowest distortion rate.

Device four: a redemption plan for the whys. For every process where "only so-and-so knows," set a redemption deadline: N months before that person departs, the judgement is written down and verified by a second person executing it. The verification: the second person performs one complete operation from the text alone; every point of sticking is a breakpoint, to be rewritten on the spot. The redemption schedule is fixed at onboarding: begin only when the resignation letter arrives, and it is usually too late.

## 6 Boundaries with Adjacent Mechanisms

Against information attenuating through hierarchy (Chapter 16): attenuation is vertical deformation; the rupture is horizontal and diachronic loss. Chapter 16 repairs the channel (side-by-side reading, direct-access items); this chapter repairs the container (template, shadow period).

Against the invisibility of maintenance (Chapter 29): the loss of tacit knowledge is maintenance in its invisible form — the "upkeep" of judgement (post-mortems, apprenticeship, document updates) has no output and gets deferred just the same. Chapter 29's ledger principle applies to knowledge assets: the knowledge ledger must be kept too.

Against the truth-teller being culled first (Chapter 19): the commonest silence in a rupture-prone organisation is the newcomer who stops asking why (asking yields no answer, or embarrasses the veterans). Restoring the culture of asking is the precondition for this chapter's device four.

## 7 Self-Check: Five Questions

1. How many items on your own post can only you answer for? Does your departure plan contain redemption arrangements for each of them?
2. At your most recent takeover, did the handover material include "what has gone wrong here before"?
3. In your team, how many versions of the answer does one operating question have? Have those versions ever been checked against each other?
4. How long is the spoken part of your shift handover? And how much of it enters any record?
5. If your team's most senior person resigned tomorrow, which processes would have to be reinvented? Have you asked them for that list before they leave?

## 6bis Installing the Devices: Order, Failure Modes, and Cost

The three-part template goes first — low cost, high leverage: it turns handover from an oral tradition into a document structure. The shadow period follows (it puts a person alongside the paper), continuity of decision records third (a long knowledge project, starting with the next decision), and the why-redemption plan last (it faces ruptures already incurred: backfilling the reasons for historical judgments). First ninety days: weeks one and two, draft the template — facts (systems and processes as they stand), judgment (why it is this way, what was tried and failed), pits (what went wrong before, where the holes are), one page each; weeks three to six, trial it on the next real personnel change and revise; month two, set shadow-period rules (minimum overlap by role criticality); month three, start the redemption plan's first batch — three key processes where only "how" survives, and the veterans who know "why" recorded on video or in text.

Failure modes. The template's middle collapses (judgment and pits left blank; facts pasted from a system export) — acceptance criteria: judgment must carry at least three "why not the alternatives," pits must carry at least one real incident; an empty section means the handover isn't done. Shadow periods become labor (the newcomer hauling loads while the veteran does something else; handover in the calendar but not in conversation) — set a dialogue quota: weekly handover sessions with topics drawn from the template's three parts. Decision records lag forever — adopt Chapter 7's three-line rule as part of the decision itself: no record, no decision. Redemption runs out of subjects (the veterans retire, disconnect, pass away; the reasons evaporate) — prioritize by expected remaining tenure: those leaving soonest get recorded first.

Cost: two to three days to draft; half a day per handover to use; shadow periods are staffing cost (one to three months of overlap on critical seats); decision records cost ten minutes each; redemption takes about two hours of processing per recorded hour. Against a reversal move with no procedure to drift from, a pre-hospital identity mismatch, or tag-outs that lost their compensation checks across a shift — ruptures sit at the door between the one who knew and the one who doesn't; all four devices carry what's inside the door out.

Toolchain: the locator triggers on "fast personnel churn plus documents that only state conclusions." Question set 30's item 1 ("does the why-document exist") audits the judgment section. Chapter 38's traceability clause shares this chapter's discipline: judgment trails the decision. And the pre-mortem's participant advice (at least one outside view) hides a handover audit: a newcomer's questions are the most sensitive rupture detector in existence — half of why newcomers belong in pre-mortems.
