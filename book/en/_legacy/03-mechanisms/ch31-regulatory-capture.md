# Chapter 31: Regulatory Lag and Capture (Stigler)

> What this chapter covers: why the rules always run half a beat behind, and why the regulator may become the regulated industry's voice; Stigler's capture theory, the criteria, four field sites, and the defence of "diversified information sources."
> When to return: when assessing an industry's regulatory environment, predicting the risk vacuum of a new business form, or when your compliance team understands the business's loopholes better than the business team does.
> Reading time: 50 minutes.

Regulatory lag and capture are the only pair of mechanisms in this book in which the "invisible hand" appears in an entirely positive role: the market's hand steers the regulator's attention, personnel, and information channels toward the direction of the regulated industry's interests — and none of it requires a bribe or a conspiracy. George Stigler's classic 1971 paper ("The Theory of Economic Regulation," Bell Journal of Economics and Management Science 2(1): 3–21; Crossref carries the reprint citation, which has been verified) put the core proposition plainly: regulation is usually acquired by the industry and operates for the industry — regulation benefits the incumbents. Fifty years on, the platform economy, artificial intelligence, and gene technology have done no more than move his argument into new ledgers. This chapter treats lag (rules slower than technology) and capture (the regulator sliding toward the regulated) together, because they are each other's cause and effect: the lag period is capture's incubator, and capture extends the lag period.

## 1 What It Is

Definition: regulatory lag means the rules update more slowly than technology and business forms change, leaving the risks of the vacuum period unclaimed; regulatory capture means the regulator and the regulated fuse so deeply in personnel, information, and interest that the regulatory objective function drifts toward the regulated's own.

Three trigger criteria (if any one appears, the mechanism has already started):

① The regulator's main information source is the regulated party (the data is self-reported, the technology is explained by the other side, the people arrive from the other side);
② The gap between the rules' last update and the change in the business form exceeds one technology generation;
③ The expected cost of the penalty is lower than the expected return of the violation (the fine becomes a membership fee).

## 2 How It Grows

Three generating conditions. Condition one: industry concentration. Few and large, the regulated face the regulator with a full-time professional team, while the regulator's own staff is transient and its budget is appropriated; the asymmetry of expertise is capture's technical precondition — cognitive capture precedes interest capture. Condition two: insufficient regulatory resources. Unable to understand for itself, the regulator can only rely on the regulated party's explanations, and believes whatever is explained; the data arrives as the regulated party's self-report, the metrics are defined by the regulated party, the technical training is provided by the regulated party — the single information pipeline was sealed on the day the budget passed. Condition three: the revolving door. Two-way flow of talent between regulator and regulated places a future employer inside today's case file. The revolving door does not guarantee corruption; it guarantees an effect on how sharp the wording gets.

## 3 The Mechanism in Four Domains

### Case 1 (Aviation): An Investigation Four Years Without a Conclusion

av-038. China Eastern Flight MU5735, Boeing 737-800 (B-1791), 21 March 2022, Teng County, Guangxi. Civil Aviation Administration of China notifications (reviewed for this book): all 123 passengers and 9 crew members aboard died; the investigation progress notification of 20 March 2024 stated explicitly that the investigation had still not reached a conclusion on the nature and cause of the accident, and that the technical investigation continued. An investigation of such duration is itself a decay of regulatory signal: worldwide, targeted inspections of the same type and the same operation can only advance on generic directives, in the absence of a cause conclusion. The care this case requires is that it points at no cause at all — it points at how a "no-conclusion state" propagates through the global safety system: no conclusion, no targeted directive; no targeted directive, and every operator of the type is left auditing itself against generic rules.

Stop at the decision point: the moment of each annual progress notification — and the moment global regulators decide, on that basis, "whether to do a little more." On the table: a conclusionless progress report, the industry's requirement of operational continuity, and the risk of the waiting itself. 〔Outcome〕The investigation continues; the annual notification has become institutionalised. Primary source: Civil Aviation Administration of China, 2024; link in Appendix B, av-038.

### Case 2 (Chemical): Who Regulates Ammonium Nitrate

ch-002. The West Fertilizer storage and distribution facility (West, Texas), 17 April 2013. CSB page (reviewed for this book): the explosion killed 12 volunteer firefighters and 2 members of the public and injured hundreds. The CSB's conclusion points directly at the regulatory structure: the scale and manner of the facility's ammonium nitrate storage fell into the seams between federal, state, and local oversight — no fire separation distance required, combustible construction beside it, and every layer declining to classify it as high-risk. The seam does not mean nobody regulated it; it means every layer reasonably believed another layer regulated it: the federal layer held cross-state transport and homeland-security framings, the state held emergency-response framings, the local held building and fire framings — three layers summed, and not one was answerable for the whole question "this building holds this much ammonium nitrate."

Stop at the decision point: the annual cycle in which each layer's inspection completed and each layer filed its own records. On the table: each layer's list of its own powers, the facility's low-hazard label under the existing classifications, and the question no layer ever asked: taken together, do the three layers cover it? 〔Outcome〕The explosion triggered a regulatory review of ammonium nitrate storage; the repair of the structural seam is measured in years. Primary source: CSB, West Fertilizer Explosion and Fire, 2016; link in Appendix B, ch-002.

### Case 3 (Finance): 2.56 Million Fractional-Share Trades

fi-011. Raymond James & Associates. FINRA disciplinary action, August 2026 (the monthly summary PDF has been reviewed for this book): approximately 2.56 million fractional-share principal settlement trades went unreported as required, and a fine of 125,000 dollars was imposed. Fractional-share trading grew out of odd-lot investing and automatic reinvestment plans (DRIPs): the business form grew outside the trading classifications of the rules — these "trades" were processed as bookkeeping entries. The rules were written in an era when fractional shares did not exist or had no scale, and scaling the business form amplified a classification error into a reporting gap of millions of trades; the ratio between the fine (125,000 dollars) and the size of the gap (2.56 million trades) is criterion ③'s arithmetic demonstration of "the fine as membership fee."

Stop at the decision point: the memorandum in which, at launch of the fractional-share business, internal compliance determined its reporting status. On the table: the letter of the rule text, the shape of the new business form, and the industry practice of "everyone else does it this way." 〔Outcome〕The violation upheld; the rules' catch-up for the new business form began after the action. Primary source: FINRA, Disciplinary and Other FINRA Actions – October 2026, 2026; link in Appendix B, fi-011.

### Case 4 (Maritime): Custom in Place of the Assessment

ma-012. The crab-fishing vessel Amadeus (TH7), December 2023, UK waters. MAIB report (Report 13/2026; reviewed for this book): while hauling pots, a deckhand leaned out over the side, fell into the water, and died; the investigation found the task required an unstable leaning posture over the side with no fall-prevention measures, and industry custom had long stood in for a formal risk assessment. Small fishing vessels attract a naturally low regulatory density: where the rules cannot reach, custom is the law — and what custom ate was precisely the space the risk assessment should have covered. 〔Death toll and report number verified on the page; details of the custom are in the report body〕

Stop at the decision point: the moment the working method was first repeated unprotected and tacitly accepted across the fleet. On the table: the method's efficiency, a record of never having hurt anyone, and the impossibility of regulatory presence. 〔Outcome〕1 person died. Primary source: MAIB, Report 13/2026, 2026; link in Appendix B, ma-012.

## 4 What Shows Earliest

Six weak signals, each with the reason that usually overrides it.

1. The regulator's technical lectures, data platforms, and inspection manuals are drafted with the participation of the leading regulated firms. Override: "they know the business best."
2. A new business form has run for over three years with no corresponding rule and no explicit exemption. Override: "let it play out for a while."
3. Practitioners openly do the arithmetic of fines against violation returns. Override: "it's just a cost."
4. Regulators' exits concentrate heavily in the regulated industry. Override: "a market for talent."
5. The compliance team understands the business's real loopholes better than the business team, and the information never flows upward. Override: "that is compliance's whole value."
6. The industry association's "self-regulation code" substitutes for absent regulation, with no cost of breach. Override: "industry self-governance."

## 5 How to Defend

Device one: diversify the information sources. The regulatory and risk entries are forced to exceed the regulated party's self-reporting: independent sampling, third-party data, complaint and public-sentiment channels, cross-jurisdiction comparison. A single information source is capture's technical foundation; diversification does not prevent capture but holds the floor — and while the floor holds, everything else still has a chance.

Device two: vacuum-period registration. A new business form registers a "rules-blank checklist" at launch, and during the blank period carries heightened self-reporting obligations and margin requirements: the lag period is rewritten from "nobody's watching" to "conditional release," the condition being the deposit and the reporting frequency. Had fi-011's fractional-share business launched with vacuum-period registration, the classification problem of 2.56 million trades would have surfaced in year one, not at the millionth trade.

Device three: link penalties to returns. The penalty basis is tied to the return of the violation (a multiple of illicit gains), so the "membership fee" arithmetic fails; for repeat offenders, introduce business restrictions rather than linear fine escalation. A penalty's deterrent depends not on its absolute size but on its ratio to the return: below 1, the fine is simply the industry's admission price.

Device four: a cooling-off period on the revolving door. Departure from key regulatory posts into the regulated industry carries a cooling-off period, during which paid communication on matters concerning the former institution is prohibited; the reverse direction likewise. The cooling-off period cannot prevent conceptual capture, but it cuts the thickest pipeline of interest — and conceptual capture (criterion-line "believe whatever is explained") must be diluted by diversifying the information sources.

## 6 Boundaries with Adjacent Mechanisms

Against compliance without safety (Chapter 32): capture's organisational state is "every check passed" (TWA 800's certification philosophy, this chapter's seam inspections); Chapter 32 supplies its micro-mechanism: the gaps between compliant items belong to no one. The relation between the chapters: Chapter 31 is the climate; Chapter 32 is the weather.

Against misaligned incentives (Chapter 17): capture is misaligned incentives in the regulatory relationship — the regulator's own appraisal (budget, staffing, calm) is misaligned with its true objective (risk control). Chapter 17's whole-outcome signatory takes the form here of the independent parliamentary watchdog and the press.

Against boundary extrapolation (Chapter 23): operation inside a lag period is essentially one vast undeclared extrapolation — the business form extrapolates the old rules' applicability to the new shape. Chapter 23's declaration sheet, in the regulatory setting, corresponds to vacuum-period registration.

## 7 Self-Check: Five Questions

1. In which year were your industry's rules last updated? What changed in the business form over the same period? List three differences.
2. The largest loophole your compliance team ever found — through which channel did it reach the regulator or the governance level, and how long did it take?
3. Has anyone ever computed, for your industry, the ratio of fines to violation returns? What is the number?
4. The regulatory people you know — where did they go after leaving? What does that flow imply for your information strategy?
5. If your business entered a regulatory blank tomorrow, would you register it proactively? What does your answer depend on?

## 6bis Installing the Devices: Order, Failure Modes, and Cost

Source diversification goes first — it is the one device the regulated party can run unilaterally: change what the regulator eats. Gap-period registration follows (it governs the territory rules haven't reached: the months when new business outruns the rulebook), penalty-to-benefit linkage third (aimed at regulators and legislatures: the case for fines scaled to illicit gain), and revolving-door cooling periods last (a personnel institution that rides the industry-association reform cycle). First ninety days: week one, map every contact surface with the regulator — inspections, filings, consultations, penalties, informal exchanges — and profile what information each surface feeds (structured data or presentation decks); weeks two to four, start one proactive structured supply (technical ledgers, incident notices, industry data on a schedule, formatted for regulatory usability); month two, open the gap-period register (every business activity rules don't yet cover: the business, the risk, the self-imposed constraint, the registration date); month three, raise two discussions inside the industry body — penalty-to-benefit measurement, and a cooling-period convention — topic first, consensus later.

Failure modes. Structured supply turns promotional (submissions ever prettier; the regulator's picture systematically rosy) — a standing negative-data field, same rule as Chapter 16's quantity discipline: "none this period" is also a datum. Gap registration becomes an alibi (registered equals insured; self-restraint stays fictional) — make the self-constraint column mandatory: every registered gap names the substitute constraint in force and how it's checked; a blank column blocks the registration. Linkage advocacy idles for years — pilot first: industry self-fine conventions and bond schemes can run ahead of statute, and running pilots are the best brief a legislature can read. Cooling periods become bridge periods (the person cooled; the relationship renewed by consultancy) — register post-term engagements with the industry body.

Cost: the contact-surface map is a week; structured supply is a day or two a month, mostly reformatting data that already exists; the register is entry cost; the industry work is meeting cost. Against an ammonium-nitrate rulebook gap that cost fourteen lives, an airworthiness blind spot in the years before the rule caught up, an exchange frozen by a banking channel it couldn't rule, or 2.56 million unreported trades — regulatory failure compounds by the year and settles by the incident; the only way to move the settlement earlier is for the regulator to hold true information before the incident, and this section's devices are how the regulated supply it.

Toolchain: the locator triggers on "dense but captured oversight." Question set 31's five items — information sourcing, update lag, the penalty-to-benefit ratio, the revolving door, drafting authorship — are the full physical. Post-mortems involving penalties send a structured-supply-format copy to the regulator (converting tuition into the regulator's knowledge — Chapter 41's anti-narrative stance at industry scale). And Chapter 40's exploration declarations merge with gap registration in heavily regulated industries: one sheet managing both the internal exploration boundary and the external rule boundary.
