# Chapter 18 Diffusion of Responsibility

> What this chapter covers: how responsibility thins as the number of signatures rises; three criteria, four field sites, and the design of unilateral stop authority together with a ceiling on signature counts.
> When to turn back: when the organization runs a countersigning workflow, stands up a committee, or hands one matter to three departments.
> Estimated reading time: 50 minutes.

Responsibility dilutes quietly. Nobody refuses it. It is averaged away in the act of dividing the work. A fraudulent annual report carries four signature lines. A collision in a shipping lane has four classes of party. A level crossing's warning equipment spans two owners. A helicopter's landing rests on two sets of assumptions. Seen afterwards, every party signed, every party covered its own segment, and every party can state exactly what it did. No link in the chain is in charge, and every link sincerely believes that somebody else is watching. This chapter is about that somebody else.

## 18.1 What It Is

Definition: diffusion of responsibility is the psychological and structural phenomenon in which responsibility thins as the number of participants rises. The structural face is dispersal: staged approval, multi-party signature, committee decision. Each gate added is meant to add a line of defence. What it adds is one more contributor to the assumption that someone else will check. The psychological face is the office version of the bystander effect: the more parties involved, the higher the cost of unilateral intervention and the greater the social risk of making a fuss over nothing. Lay the two together and no link in the chain is in charge.

Three trigger criteria. Any one of them means the mechanism has already started.

1. Many participants, and the responsibility interfaces between them are not drawn in writing.
2. Every party can state exactly what it did; no party can state who owns the whole.
3. The organization holds no unilateral stop authority anywhere.

Criterion 2 has a self-test that is harsh and does work. After an incident the organization's first reflex is to find out who signed. The signature chain carries seven names. Seven signatures mean the step was confirmed seven times, which means not one of the seven confirmations was necessary.

The psychological face has a dated experimental origin. John Darley and Bibb Latané ran a series of experiments in 1968 in which subjects waited alone or in company while smoke came into the room next door, or a person fell next door. Subjects on their own reported the anomaly at a far higher rate than subjects in company. Company did not add up to more vigilance. It diluted each person's inclination to step in. The central variable is structurally identical to this chapter's criteria: the presence of others supplies the assumption that somebody has already dealt with it, and that assumption holds in every head at once. The office version differs in one respect. The others there carry documented roles, so the assumption looks better founded. The experiment also supplies the usable converse. Name one person, even if the only change is that one more person is nominally responsible for raising the alarm, and intervention rates return to the solo level. The antidote has sat in the literature for decades. Replace everybody with a name.

The structural face needs one more definition: what it means to be in charge. The test here is three observable conditions satisfied at once. You know what you are looking at, which is the information state. You know what you are allowed to do, which is the authority state. You know what follows if you do nothing, which is the consequence state. Take away any one and you are not in charge, even where the paperwork carries your name. The VTS operator in Case 1 (ma-008) lacked the second: the boundaries of authority were unclear. The two parties in Case 3 (ce-008) lacked the third and the first: nobody assessed what the equipment actually did to pedestrians. Auditing the three states beats auditing the name of the responsible person, because the paper name is usually written in a clear hand and the three states are usually all empty.

## 18.2 How It Grows

Three generating conditions.

Condition one is an institutional tradition of multi-party signature and staged approval. The intention is another line of defence; the cost structure of signing decides the behaviour. Checking every prior signature benefits the chain and costs the signer, so a rational signer checks his own cell. Defences stack on paper and run in parallel in practice.

Condition two is layered outsourcing and subcontracting. Each tier down drops the visibility of risk a grade and weakens the responsibility link a grade. Where a subcontract carries service clauses and no risk clauses, nothing but a commercial relationship remains between the tiers.

Condition three is committee decision. The resolution belongs to the collective, execution needs an assignee, and supervision has no post. What a collective passes has no individual claimant by construction, and the credential of having taken part makes each member feel the duty is already discharged.

The tragedy of dilution is that it is asymptomatic in ordinary times. Every link signs, approves and meets. The system looks deeply defended, which is Chapter 22's illusion of redundancy projected onto the responsibility layer. The symptom shows up only at the moment somebody has to call a stop. At that moment seven signatories equal none.

The arithmetic fits in a sentence. Responsibility divides by headcount. The drive to intervene declines by headcount. The two curves run in opposite directions. Let the cost of dealing with a risk be C. With n parties and no written interface, each party perceives its own share of "I ought to act" as roughly C/n. The larger n gets, the smaller each share, and the cheaper the waiting strategy of letting somebody else move first. The equilibrium of waiting strategies is the classic dilution scene: everybody waits and the exposure runs its full length. The design implication is as plain. Either squeeze n to one, a single accountable person, or put each party's perceived share back at C by writing the whole consequence into each party's assessment. Those two moves are Device 1 and Device 2.

n also has a time dimension. Dilution is not static. The participant count grows with the age of the process. A new process has one owner. Every small incident adds a countersignature. Every cross-departmental collaboration adds a party. n climbs monotonically with age, the perceived share falls monotonically, until some unpleasant event drags the chain onto an audit table. That is why old organizations carry the most signatures on their gates and not necessarily the strongest defence. The growth of n records the organization's history of frights; each signature is a souvenir of the last accident, the organizational-design counterpart of Chapter 11's deviation ledger. Device 4, the ceiling on signature counts, is aimed at exactly this monotonic growth. Souvenirs can stay in the minutes. They do not have to stay in the workflow.

Signing has one further behavioural layer: how far a signer rationally checks. Making every signature verify all preceding content grows the cost linearly with chain length while the benefit falls with the marginal contribution of each added signature. A rational signer checks his own cell. Chapter 1 set out that structure; its industry version is the signature chain in Case 4. The patch is not to ask signers to be more conscientious. It is to write the verification protocol into the system. Signatures come in two kinds. A verifying signature means substantive checking of the content in that cell. A passing signature means confirming only that the paper moved through. Mark the two in separate columns, and rule that a passing signature may never be cited as quality evidence. Once the columns exist, the defensive illusion of seven signatures has no raw material left.

The outsourcing chain has a contract-layer patch worth spelling out: vertical continuity of risk clauses. A subcontract usually carries service levels and nothing else. The risk clauses, meaning the duty to notify when something goes wrong, the duty to cooperate with an investigation, and the cap on liability, are signed only between main contractor and client. At the second and third tiers the obligations to risk fall to zero tier by tier. The continuous form is the flow-down clause: the main contractor writes the core risk clauses verbatim into every tier of subcontract, and no tier may override them with looser terms. Flow-down is established practice in aviation maintenance and maritime contracting. It treats the disease in condition two, the tier where nothing but a commercial relationship survives. Checking the penetration rate of flow-down clauses is the first move in auditing an outsourced chain.

## 18.3 The Shape in Four Domains

### Case 1 (Maritime): A VTS Whose Staff Did Not Know Their Own Authority

**Case ID**: ma-008.

**When and where**: 24 October 2023, 0455 (UTC+2). Eastern German Bight traffic separation scheme, collision point 54°01.5N 007°38.7E, 0.2 nautical miles inside international waters. The watch station was the German Bight vessel traffic service centre at Wilhelmshaven. Wind force 5 to 6 with gusts to 7, visibility between 10 and 31 km, dark (pp. 10, 24, 65).

**What and who**: the Bahamian bulk carrier Polesie (24,055 gross tons, built 2009, managed by Polska Żegluga Morska P.P., 20 crew, 32,997 tonnes of grain) and the Isle of Man general cargo ship Verity (2,601 gross tons, built 2001, managed by Faversham Ships Ltd, 7 crew, 3,262 tonnes of steel coils). The monitoring party was German Bight Traffic, operated by the Federal Waterways and Shipping Administration under the direction of the Federal Waterways and Shipping Agency (GDWS). Responsibility is recorded by post: the officers of the watch on both ships, the VTS nautical assistant, the VTS nautical supervisor (pp. 10, 32–33).

**What they were doing**: at 1940 on 23 October Polesie sailed from Hamburg for A Coruña with 32,997 tonnes of grain, draught 10 m, after seven days loading in port. At 2008 the same day Verity sailed from Bremen for Immingham with 3,262 tonnes of steel coils, draught 5.4 m, after five days loading (p. 12).

At 0310 on the 24th the pilots left both ships. On the Polesie bridge were the master, the second officer and a helmsman. The master handed control to the second officer and left. Course 273°, speed 11 knots, time in hand (p. 12). At 0410 the chief officer took the 0400 to 0800 watch. The starboard radar was on 6 nautical mile range with offset display. Verity was already on AIS at CPA 0.15 nautical miles and TCPA 45 minutes. Neither radar had its CPA alarm switched on, and the ARPA had not acquired the target (p. 15).

At 0423 Verity entered the northbound lane on course 335° at 8.3 knots, keeping to the eastern side. At 0444:30 Polesie passed the planned waypoint, where the plan was to alter to 256° onto the westbound lane; she was still making 273°. At 0446:03 she crossed the 256° planned track with no change of course (p. 16). Between 0448:48 and 0449:59 the chief officer entered nine successive course changes on the autopilot, taking the heading from 264° to 245°. The 28° port turn went in instalments, and from another ship or from the VTS screen it did not look like steering (pp. 17, 58).

Over the same period the VTS assistant was monitoring both ships, judged that there was a risk of collision, and noticed that Verity was not acting as the give-way vessel. In the preceding three minutes the assistant had also handled two routine ship reports; the rest of the watch's tasks are unknown. The Channel 79 west sector assistant was absent that night and one supervisor covered two sectors (pp. 17, 36).

**The decision point**: the first moment sits at the document layer. On 28 October 2013 Coral Ivory and Siderfly collided in the Kiel Canal, and the Federal Bureau of Maritime Casualty Investigation (BSU) recommended in report 330/13 that GDWS study changing the VTS ship-shore duplex channel to simplex (p. 53). Nobody died in that collision. Siderfly was at risk of sinking from damage below the waterline and the canal was shut for three days (p. 53). The report records no formal reply from GDWS. By the time the 2026 report was published, GDWS said a next-generation VTS system was in development with the simplex requirement included, over 2028 to 2034 (p. 69). For the ten years between, the two ships could reach each other only through a shore station relaying words.

The second moment is 0450:09. The assistant called Verity on Channel 80 with the ships less than 1.3 nautical miles apart, CPA 160 m, TCPA 8 minutes 11 seconds. Three sentences: the CPA is very small; what is your intention; under the rules you must take avoiding action (pp. 17, 60). The report notes that the VTS's own post-event report does not explain why the intervention waited until TCPA was down to 7 minutes 28 seconds (p. 60). When the call ended the ships were a nautical mile apart, CPA under 0.2 nautical miles, TCPA 5 minutes 16 seconds (p. 17). The call carried no IALA WARNING marker and the tone was the tone of ordinary conversation (pp. 17, 61).

The third moment runs from 0453:43 to 0454:00. The supervisor called Polesie on Channel 80, confirmed radio contact, then in a raised, urgent voice said "do not go left, do not go left, turn right, Polesie, alter course to starboard" (p. 20). The chief officer answered "yes, starboard", and nine seconds later went to hand steering and hard starboard (p. 21). The VTS post-event report states that the supervisor was acting under Rule 17(c) of the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (p. 61). The report's analysis holds that the chief officer was more likely acting under Rule 17(b), and that the restriction in 17(c) did not apply in that situation (p. 62). At the moment the instruction went out the ships were under half a nautical mile apart, and the instruction finished within 90 seconds of the collision (pp. 20, 62). Polesie was still inside German territorial waters, where the VTS had authority to instruct her. Half a mile away Verity was already in international waters, where the VTS could only advise (p. 65). Two ships half a mile apart, two sets of authority.

None of the three had a second person check it. The document was issued by GDWS. Both calls were decided by the watchkeeper on duty. Annex A holds only time, call sign and the words as spoken (p. 72).

**What was visible then**: on the Polesie bridge, both pilots were off the ship and the master was aboard but not on the bridge (p. 12); two tracks on radar and ECDIS, with the CPA and TCPA figures from AIS; the territorial sea baseline drawn on the ECDIS at 0433, the moment Verity passed from German territorial waters into international waters (p. 16); at 0443 a readout of CPA 0.11 nautical miles and TCPA 14 minutes 42 seconds (p. 16); at 0452:37 the Verity AIS icon selected, reading 0.83 nautical miles, CPA 0.2 nautical miles, TCPA 3 minutes 40 seconds (p. 19); four written instructions from the master, one of them "call the master in any doubt", specifying a call at 5 nautical miles or more (p. 30).

At the VTS station: a chart terminal fusing radar and AIS, on whose reconstruction of 0450:09 the two ships show a CPA under 0.1 nautical miles with 8 minutes left (p. 18); the automatic alarm for CPA under 0.5 nautical miles muted because there were too many small craft, with the indication still on screen (p. 35); no German territorial sea boundary on the display (p. 65); Channel 80 duplex, so the shore station could hear the ships but the ships could not hear each other (p. 37); one assistant short on the watch (p. 36); a three-shift rotation with the night shift from 22:00 to 06:00 (p. 35); no written guidance on what to do at which CPA (p. 35).

On the Verity bridge: two X-band radars with ARPA, AIS, ECDIS, BNWAS (p. 30); a steady 8.3 knots along the eastern side of the lane since 0423, nearly 8 nautical miles without a change of course (p. 57); no voyage data recorder, not then required on a ship of 2,601 gross tons (pp. 11, 70).

What became known only afterwards: the 28° port turn by Polesie was hard to make out on radar or by eye (p. 58); Verity was in fact turning into the track of Polesie (p. 59); the CPA between the two ships never exceeded 0.53 nautical miles from entry into the traffic separation scheme to the collision (p. 56); Polesie entered international waters at 0454:18, 1 minute 10 seconds before the collision (pp. 21, 65); had the port turn continued there would have been no collision (p. 61); the investigation found that the chief officer "felt obliged to comply" with the supervisor's instruction (p. 62); the investigation found no evidence that either the operator or the supervisor knew the geographic limits of their authority (p. 65).

You are in the seat now, with the material above in front of you.

> **Stop here. Decide before you turn the page.**
>
> 1. What would you do? Write the action, not "pay more attention".
> 2. Which item here are you most likely to miss, and why does it look unimportant?

**What happened**: at 0455:28 the bulbous bow of Polesie struck Verity on the starboard side at a relative speed of about 12 knots, some 11 m forward of the accommodation (p. 22). Verity sank in 36 m of water, about 5 minutes after the collision. Five of the seven crew died; two were rescued, the second officer and one seaman (pp. 9, 23, 54). The search ran into the night. Four crew were unaccounted for, and two of the bodies were found inside the vessel during the recovery (p. 64).

Go back to the three questions. The commonest answer to the first is to intervene earlier and to use the WARNING marker. In this case the cost of that action does not sit in speaking. It sits in what follows speaking. After the first call at 0450:09 the VTS internal report records that "at 1 nautical mile VTS had no further opportunity to intervene" and that it was "satisfied that the earlier interaction had produced a collision avoidance solution" (p. 36). Three and a half minutes later it spoke again, and that second sentence pulled the chief officer out of a port turn that had already been working. The item most easily missed under the second question is the absent territorial sea boundary on the display. It looks like a charting detail. It was the shared blind spot of all three people on watch that night. The answer to the third question lands before anybody speaks: establish which side of the water the ship is in, then decide whether the sentence is advice or instruction (p. 65).

**Options ruled out**: a broadcast on Channel 16 so both ships hear the same words. The report states there is no indication that Channel 16 was considered, and concedes that a broadcast has drawbacks, but it does offer a way to clarify what is happening (p. 63). Having the two ships move to a working channel and talk directly: not possible on a duplex Channel 80, which needs a separately agreed channel; the VTS would not be a party to that call, and once advice had been given it would not know what the ships had agreed (pp. 62–63).

A single instruction from the VTS to both ships: on the Verity side the VTS had advisory authority only (p. 65). Intervening before 0450: the VTS internal report sets out as experience that small craft in the area often give way late, need little swinging room and are reluctant to leave their course (p. 36). The experience held, and the price of it was a threshold pushed out to 1.3 nautical miles. Continuing to intervene inside 1 nautical mile: the internal report sets its threshold at "at 1 nautical mile VTS had no further opportunity to intervene" (p. 36). The chief officer carrying the port turn begun at 0452:55 through to the end: the reconstruction shows no collision, but he went to hand steering nine seconds after the supervisor's instruction (pp. 21, 61). The master taking the bridge: his written instructions required a call at 5 nautical miles or more in any doubt (p. 30), and the master was called after the collision (p. 22).

**Early signals**: 1. On 28 October 2013 Coral Ivory and Siderfly collided in the Kiel Canal, and BSU report 330/13 recommended that GDWS study changing the duplex channel to simplex (p. 53). Reason it was overridden: the canal was closed three days, nobody died, and the recommendation stopped at "study". 2. On 11 February 2015 Ever Smart and Alexandra 1 collided inside the Jebel Ali port limits, and the container ship's bridge sighted the other vessel seconds after the port control call (p. 52). Reason it was overridden: a one-off inside port limits where both pilotage and port control had failed. 3. On 11 December 2013 Paula C and Darya Gayatri collided in the Dover Strait, and the investigation found that a timely and well-meant VHF intervention by the coastguard almost certainly pushed the Paula C officer of the watch into the wrong action (p. 53). Reason it was overridden: blame landed on the officer of the watch for losing situational awareness, and the intervention itself was never questioned. 4. The VTS compulsory refresher scenario VK02-K-GBT-De trains exactly the case of a risk of collision where the VTS does not intervene (p. 36). Reason it was overridden: the training supplies the right answer, and the boundary was only ever exercised in one direction. 5. In 2022 the IMO member state audit of Germany raised no finding or observation on the VTS (p. 36). Reason it was overridden: the external audit passed, so the system was taken as confirmed. 6. A shipowner inspection record of 19 October states that the bridge personnel knew all navigational safety procedures; the master's written instructions were a verbatim copy of the Liverpool to Hamburg version of 12 October, signed by the master and three officers (p. 30). Reason it was overridden: complete paperwork was read as execution.

**The point of escalation**: 0451:36 to 0451:50. The assistant called Polesie with the sentence: "the Verity on your port side will pass astern of you, he tells me he will turn to starboard and pass astern of you, keep a check on the situation" (p. 19).

Before that sentence each ship held its own picture. Verity held course and speed, expecting Polesie to give way to the lane and let her pass ahead. Polesie expected Verity to pass astern (pp. 57, 19). The two pictures contradicted each other and neither ship had spoken to the other. After that sentence, the Polesie picture had been confirmed by the shore station. The chief officer answered "yes, I will keep a watch, a little to starboard" (p. 19). The report notes that this answer did not meet the closed-loop readback standard of IALA 1132, and that had the assistant used the WARNING marker before the call, the ambiguous reply would not have been let pass (p. 61).

The escalation took the form of a relayed sentence. It sent each ship a confirmation that the other would give way, and the two confirmations pointed opposite ways. It also put the VTS itself in the position of a problem already handed over: for the next four minutes nobody spoke, until 0453:43. In those four minutes Verity only began to turn to starboard at 0452:26, while Polesie at 0451:02 set the autopilot from 245° to 252° and stopped the port turn that was under way (pp. 17, 19). Who approved it: the assistant issued it, the supervisor sat at the same watch station, and there is no record of a review. The words are in Annex A (p. 72).

**Failure chain**: technical layer. The duplex Channel 80 kept ship from speaking to ship, leaving the VTS as the only channel and reducing it to relaying what was meant (pp. 60, 62). The automatic alarm for CPA under 0.5 nautical miles was muted (p. 35). The terminal did not show the territorial sea boundary (p. 65). Both ships made small incremental alterations on autopilot that did not read as steering at a distance (pp. 56–57). Verity had no voyage data recorder (p. 11). Neither ship had its CPA alarm on. Polesie's seamen were on a lookout rota and by custom did not keep lookout (pp. 15, 29). Whether Verity had an extra lookout that night, the report records as impossible to establish (p. 55).

Organizational layer. Authority was cut into two grades of post, supervisor and assistant, and three grades of measure, traffic information, traffic assistance and instruction, with the assistant empowered to issue instructions only on the supervisor's authority (p. 33). The three sentences at 0450 belong in the files to information and assistance, yet they went past plain notification ("under the rules you must act"), and the authority to instruct was not the assistant's (pp. 17, 33). The ordering rule that traffic assistance is provided when traffic information has failed to achieve its purpose came with no CPA or TCPA threshold and no statement of when to stand back (pp. 34, 62). The supervisor issued the instruction on the basis of Rule 17(c), and the report holds that 17(b) was the rule that applied (pp. 61–62).

Incentive layer. The VTS was not assessed on CPA at all; assessment fell on whether interaction followed procedure and whether records were kept. Four posts of thirty were vacant and the watch ran anyway, with cover arranged "opportunistically" (p. 36). The two ships were assessed on schedule reliability and surface compliance. Both had time in hand that night (p. 55) and both kept the CPA inside 0.5 nautical miles (p. 56).

The seam broke between the document and the experience. The document left blank who must speak at what distance. The watchkeeper filled the blank from experience. In that experience small craft always give way late, so silence read as a judgement.

**Minimum counterfactual**: add one line on standing back to the intervention clause of VV-WSV 2408. When TCPA falls below 5 minutes, the VTS issues warnings only and no instructions. One action, dated before October 2023, needing no new equipment and no extra staff. The report states plainly that neither VV-WSV 2408 nor VV-GDWS 24-2 gives guidance on the CPA or TCPA at which intervention becomes counterproductive (p. 62). With that line in place, the "turn to starboard" at 0454:00 would not have gone out. The report's reconstruction shows that had the chief officer continued his port turn the ships would not have collided (p. 61). The figure of 5 minutes has support in the report: when the supervisor spoke at 0453:43 the TCPA was under 3 minutes, and the report describes that band as counterproductive for intervention (p. 62). The report did not put the threshold into a recommendation. 2026/117 asks GDWS only to "provide guidance", with no number attached (p. 70). The same line would hold for next time: the decision to stand back becomes citable, and therefore answerable.

**Outcome and sources**: Verity was a total loss, five people died, Polesie suffered slight damage to bow and starboard side with minor oil pollution (p. 10). The report makes seven recommendations, numbered 2026/115 to 2026/121. The IMO should amend SOLAS Chapter V/20 to bring internationally trading cargo ships of 500 to 3,000 gross tons within the voyage data recorder requirement. GDWS should review the use of duplex channels and provide guidance to intervention staff covering when to intervene, the use of information markers, and when to stand back (p. 70). IALA has revised Guideline 1141 to require shore relay wherever a duplex channel is used (p. 69). The limits of the investigation are also recorded: Verity carried no recorder, key witnesses were not interviewed, and the internal decision process at the VTS could not be reconstructed (pp. 9, 11).

Primary source: MAIB, *Report on the investigation of the collision between the bulk carrier Polesie and the general cargo ship Verity*, Report No 5/2026, February 2026, 82 pages, with Annex A a translation of the German Bight VTS report and Annex B extracts from the Collision Regulations. Link in Appendix B, ma-008.

The four-party shape of Case 1 is worth reading again against the three states, because it is the complete specimen of this chapter's criteria. Information state: each of the four parties held a fragment (the two ships each knew their own constraints, the VTS could see both tracks), and the whole picture existed only on radar, never in a judgement. Authority state: the authority clauses for operator and supervisor were vague on what could be required, whether it could be enforced, and who carried the failure if enforcement failed, which was the fatal gap on the monitoring side. Consequence state: each bridge knew the physical consequence of a collision, and the assumption that the other would act thinned the urgency of it. Each state has a patch: a common picture table for information, an authority list for authority, and making assumptions explicit for consequence, where the watch rule requires "I think the other will move" to be said aloud, at which point it becomes something that must be verified. The MAIB recommendations fall broadly inside that frame, and the reader can check them against the original.

### Case 2 (Healthcare): The Incompatibility Only the Registry Knew

me-008. Joint replacement surgery in the English NHS. HSIB report (*Implantation of wrong prostheses during joint replacement surgery*, 2018; the archived snapshot has been checked against the original for this book). During surgery a 62-year-old patient was implanted with prostheses from different manufacturers that were not compatible with each other, and the defect surfaced days later when data at the national joint registry were matched. Surgical team, instrument supply, pre-operative check, registry entry: each person at each link could explain how his own step met that link's process. The theatre took implants against the implant list, the supplier shipped against the order, the check ran against the checklist. No link carried full responsibility for whether the two components matched each other, because matchability straddled the seam between four links, and seams do not sign.

The stop point is the instant on the operating table between the implantation of the first component and of the second. On the table: implants each carrying its own label, process records showing every link verified, and a registry that nobody thought to query on the spot. **What happened**: the patient underwent a second operation; the report names the registry's detect-afterwards pattern, and detection afterwards means nobody was responsible beforehand. Primary source: HSIB, 2018, link in Appendix B, me-008 (archived link, grade A−).

"Seams do not sign" can be pushed a step further. The seam went unclaimed because matchability straddles the catalogue systems of two manufacturers, and neither catalogue contains the other. The gap in the information structure comes before the gap in responsibility: even a person who wanted to own it would have no data to check against. The order of repair for seams of this kind is therefore data first, responsibility second. Until a compatibility tool exists, one that queries both catalogues together, naming a responsible person only creates a post with a duty and no instrument. The HSIB report makes the registry's after-the-fact detection a principal finding, which is an acknowledgement that the data layer was missing, and the direction of its recommendations agrees with this chapter's order. The generalisation for the reader: at a check point spanning two systems, ask first whether the tool exists, then whether the responsibility exists. Responsibility written where the tool is absent is written in the air.

### Case 3 (Construction): The Governance Interface at a Level Crossing

ce-008. The Staniforth Road level crossing of the Supertram network in Sheffield, 22 June 2025. RAIB report (Report 10/2026, pages checked against the original for this book): a tram struck two pedestrians, one seriously injured and one slightly injured, both taken to hospital. The pedestrians did not notice the tram coming, the audible and visual warnings had no effect, and risk management between the infrastructure owner and the operator at the crossing was weak. The crossing's warning equipment straddles the governance interface of two bodies. Ownership of the equipment, its maintenance, its assessment for effectiveness and its actual effect on pedestrians sit on different responsibility lists, and the unworded seam between those lists falls exactly on the question of whether a pedestrian can cross safely.

The stop point is the last moment at which the crossing's warning equipment was assessed on the measure of actual effect on pedestrians. On the table: the equipment status record, which reads normal, the owner's and the operator's respective duty documents, both complete, and the seam between them that neither party has ever signed. **What happened**: two people went to hospital; the report lists the unclear risk interface among its investigation findings. Primary source: RAIB, Report 10/2026, 2026, link in Appendix B, ce-008.

The governance interface here has one feature worth marking. The equipment status record reads normal. Normal means the equipment has power and is working. What failed was the measure of actual effect on pedestrians, meaning whether the warning is enough for a pedestrian to notice the tram. The split between the two measures falls along the split between the two bodies: the owner manages equipment condition, the operator manages operational safety, and the measure of effect sits in neither assessment. This is the responsibility version of the negative substitution in Chapter 12, where the information channel for the real goal was never wired into either party. The repair copies Chapter 12's general rule: build a quantity for the effect, here a frontline observation of whether pedestrians notice, then assign it an owner under this chapter's device. On governance interfaces like a level crossing the two chapters' defences always arrive as a pair.

### Case 4 (Finance): The Ex-Post Section Through a Signature Chain

fi-007. The decision against the responsible persons in the Qingyue Technology fraudulent issuance case (〔2026〕No. 59, pages checked against the original for this book): chairman Gao Yudi, warning, a fine of 9 million yuan, an 8-year market ban; executive deputy general manager Mu Xinju, a fine of 9 million yuan, a 6-year ban; chief financial officer and board secretary Zhang Xiaobo, a fine of 6 million yuan, a 5-year ban; supervisory board chairman Wu Lei, a fine of 4 million yuan, a 3-year ban (the same case as fi-006). The penalty decision is a section cut through responsibility diluted in advance. The fraudulent offering document and annual report carried four names, and the four signatures made up the organizational narrative of layer upon layer of checking. Afterwards the regulator took each signature back to its true degree of knowledge, one by one: the layering of signatures was the shape of dilution beforehand and the order of accountability afterwards.

The stop point is the stretch of process in which the fraudulent content was finalised and the four signatures went down in sequence. On the table: what each signatory actually knew, the route by which each document arrived already signed above, and the half step by which each signatory pushed the duty to check onto the signature before or after his own. **What happened**: fines totalling 28 million yuan, with market bans of 3 to 8 years. Primary source: China Securities Regulatory Commission, 〔2026〕No. 59, 2026, link in Appendix B, fi-007.

The layering in Case 4 has one more thing to read. The fines and ban lengths fall away in strict order down the four signatures, because the regulator prices by degree of knowledge and by the influence of the post. The counterpart of that pricing beforehand is that knowledge and influence ought to weigh a signature. Dilution flattens them into the same undifferentiated "signed". The lesson for a board is written in the pricing. The further down the chain a signature sits, the greater the signatory's information disadvantage, so the designer of the chain owes the lower signatories an independent information route, whether an outside adviser with direct access or a channel for dissent that goes straight up. Without one, the lower signature is only a guarantee given by the upper, and dilution runs from the first signature to the last.

Case 4 and Case 1 of Chapter 17 (fi-008, the audit firm's side of the same issuer) are a mirror pair for this chapter. The firm is the outsourced end of the chain failing, the four company signatures are the core of the chain failing, and the same fraudulent annual report sits between them. The regulator's handling of the two ends, put together, is the full section through dilution: the structural disease at the outsourced end, which is paying for strictness, meets the structural disease at the core, which is the flattening of the signature chain, on one issuer. The two cases are placed side by side so the reader can see the whole-chain shape of dilution. It does not stop at the boundary of an organization, at committees and countersignatures. It also runs between organizations, through commissioning, outsourcing and subcontracting. The instruments that defend against it are the same set. The governance interface register, Device 3, carries across organizations unchanged, and the requirement to name four parties holds across an organizational boundary as well.

#### Case 6 (Public Policy and Administration): The Railway That Kept Redrawing Its Frame (condensed)

po-007. Northern Powerhouse Rail in England. NAO report (March 2026, the pages checked against the original for this book; the NAO rates its own evidence at the weak-judgement grade). A regional rail programme was re-scoped substantially again and again over more than ten years, and the realisation of benefits slipped accordingly. In this chapter's reading, the repeated changes of scope mean the programme had no single owner of its direction: at each change of strategic leadership or fiscal cycle the scope was recut to the new preference. Each cut had its own reasons, and the sum of them is that the benefits are always still to come. Minimum counterfactual: once the programme's scope is set, any substantial change must be confirmed by an independent assessment that outlasts a single administration, with a record kept. Primary source: NAO, *Northern Powerhouse Rail*, 2026, link in Appendix B, po-007.

The contrast between Case 6 and Case 5 deserves a sentence. One programme has gone thirteen years without a decision (Case 5) and the other has redrawn its scope for more than a decade (Case 6). The symptoms look opposite, one frozen and one churning, and the structural disease is the same: neither deciding nor changing has a document to carry it. Freezing means nobody is obliged to choose; churning means nobody is obliged to hold still. The state between them, where a choice once made is neither overturned nor shelved, is what a carrying document buys. A reader who governs public projects can turn that into a self-check: for your long-horizon programme, who made the last major decision, in which document, and under which procedure. A programme that cannot answer all three is on the road between Case 5 and Case 6.

#### Case 7 (Agriculture and Food): The Relay of a Recall Along the Distribution Chain (condensed)

ag-004. The retail end at Whole Foods, Whatcom Blue bulk sliced cheese. FDA enforcement report (event 97931 / H-0210-2026, the API record checked for this book). Two days after the upstream recall, the retail end followed with a recall of 411 pounds, Class I. Responsibility passing along a distribution chain takes the form of a relay here: the manufacturer starts the recall and distribution and retail each follow at their own pace. Two days is not slow in the context of food recalls, and no link in the chain was required to answer when every downstream location would be clear. Recall effectiveness is measured at the end of the chain while recall organisation is measured by link, and the gap between the two measures is the shape of Case 7. Minimum counterfactual: generate the list of downstream locations and a clearance deadline when the recall is opened, and close it out location by location. Primary source: FDA, event 97931, 2026, link in Appendix B, ag-004.

The two-day relay is worth reading against the time frame of food safety. The exposure window for STEC contamination, Shiga toxin producing E. coli, is counted in days, and every day the product sits in the chain is another day of exposure at the consumer end. The reason this case belongs in a chapter on dilution is that every link's notion of "as soon as possible" was sincere, since every link's own procedure requires prompt action, and promptness was defined separately by each link: the manufacturer counted from issuing the notice, distribution from receiving it, retail from the internal transfer order. Nobody owned the total elapsed time of the three promptnesses strung together. The counterfactual of closing out by location works as an antidote because it moves the definition of prompt from each link's own measure to the measure at the end of the chain: the clearance deadline is worked back from the endpoint, and each link is allotted not the time it considers reasonable but the time the endpoint needs.

Working back from the endpoint has a further organizational meaning. It turns the chain's responsibility from participation into delivery. Under participation, every link's action counts as a contribution. Under delivery, only the state of the endpoint counts, meaning downstream locations cleared, and the actions of the links are process. Switching between the two measures is not difficult. The first week is difficult, because every link will object that it does not control the next link. The objection is true and the answer is structural: the part you do not control is exactly the part to write into the interface register, meaning the duty of the upstream party to notify the downstream party and the duty of the downstream party to confirm back. Once the register is written, what you do not control becomes traceable. That conversion, from objection to register, is the most everyday use of Device 3.

#### Case 8 (Nuclear): Inadequate Supervision Among Four Violations (condensed)

nu-004. Acuren Inspection Inc., Alaska site. NRC press release (October 2026, checked for this book): a proposed civil penalty of $36,000 for four violations, namely failure to perform radiation surveys, violation of operating procedures, inadequate supervision, and inadequate instructions for responding to alarms. The one worth stopping on is inadequate supervision. The supervisory post existed, since a violation list that names it shows the post is in the establishment, and the act of supervision did not happen: nobody did the surveys and nobody watched the procedures. A supervisory post is the classic hiding place for diluted responsibility, because its duty points by nature at whether others are doing things right while its assessment usually points at its own output of paperwork. Penalising the four violations together prices supervision and work the same: a failure of supervision is not an aggravated case of management failure, it is a violation in its own right. Minimum counterfactual: keep a record of the supervisory post's survey checks, who checked, when and what, and treat a missing record as a supervision violation. Primary source: NRC, 2026, link in Appendix B, nu-004.

Inadequate supervision also yields three lines of design advice for whoever creates a supervisory post. The output of the post is findings, meaning what it caught, not the number of inspections, meaning how many it ran, so the assessment should point at the findings register. The reporting line should be independent of the party being supervised, at least one level across, or supervision becomes an internal function of the supervised. The post's establishment budget should be carried separately, with no shared cost centre with the supervised party's output targets. The logic behind all three is to make the supervisory post itself a single point that is in charge: information in the findings register, authority in the independent reporting line, consequence in its own budget and assessment. Supervisory posts are a disaster area for dilution precisely because they span several parties by nature. Make the post a single point first under Device 1, and only then is it fit to supervise anybody else.

## 18.4 What Shows Earliest

Six weak signals, each with the reason that usually overrides it.

1. Every box on the process chart has a person and the whole has no owner. Override: "the division of labour is clear."
2. After a problem surfaces, the first meeting called is a meeting to establish who is responsible, not a meeting to fix it. Override: "we have to learn the lesson."
3. A committee resolution has no named executor, or the executors are not the people who passed it. Override: "collective decisions are steadier."
4. The outsourcing contract carries service clauses and no risk clauses and no stop clauses. Override: "commercial is commercial."
5. Three departments could all take charge of one matter, nobody does before an incident and everybody does after it. Override: "joint management."
6. The frequency of "that is not mine" rises in internal communication. Override: "follow the process."

## 18.5 How to Defend

Device 1, the single accountable person clause. For every critical process and every risk point, the system names one person who carries the responsibility, with the person replaceable and the seat not allowed to stand empty. Every other signatory carries a verification duty rather than a gatekeeping duty, and the verification duty is defined in writing: your signature states only that the items you checked are true. Had the four signature lines in the Qingyue case said beforehand what each one verified, the regulator would not have had to divide it up for them afterwards.

Device 2, unilateral stop authority. At least one post holds independent authority to stop a critical irreversible action, written into the job description and into the contract (Chapter 7), with the cost of stopping carried by the organization, which is the interface with the termination reward in Chapter 27. Stop authority that does not land on a named person is not stop authority, and stop authority that lands on a person with no budget behind it is authority nobody dares use.

Device 3, the governance interface register. For any object maintained jointly by several parties, whether crossing equipment, shared data, an interface system or a matchability check, build an interface register that names for each item the owner, the operator, the assessor and the party that accepts the effect, with no two of the four allowed to be the same party. Once a year run a drill against the register: if this thing breaks, who finds out first. Write the drill's answer into the register in place of the assumption.

Device 4, the ceiling on signature counts. Any process that carries more than three gatekeeping signatures on one action is restructured by force, because gatekeeping past three signatures equals no gatekeeping at all. The extra signatures either convert to informed copy, which does not constitute gatekeeping, or are deleted, and every signature kept must state the specific items it verifies. The resistance will be heavy, since every deleted line will claim to be critical. The way through is to have each line write down what it verifies, and to delete automatically any line that cannot name its items.

The four devices on this chapter's cases. Device 1 applied to Case 1: the VTS authority list states the trigger conditions for a mandatory instruction and where the responsibility falls, turning the operator's "what must I do" from a vague clause into three lines. Device 2 applied to Case 3: either party at the crossing gains authority to suspend use of the crossing unilaterally, with the cost of suspension paid from a shared fund, because authority nobody can afford to use goes unused. Device 3 applied to Case 2: prosthesis matchability is entered as an interface item, with the owner, the supplier, the assessor and the accepting party all named and none of them the same. Device 4 applied to Case 4: each of the four signature lines on the offering document states what it verifies, and any that cannot is converted to informed copy.

The failure modes of the four devices. The single accountable person dies of departure: a change of person carries no handover list, the name changes and the three states reset to zero. The patch is to write the three states into an appendix of the job description, so they travel with the post and not with the person. Stop authority dies of nobody daring to use it: the authority is on paper and the psychological cost of using it sits in the culture. The patch is to publish the record of use at intervals, since stop authority used three times is authority that is alive. The governance interface register dies of staying static: a change to the object does not trigger an update. The patch is to link the register to the change note. The ceiling on signatures dies of column drift: informed copy quietly gets promoted into a source cited by gatekeeping signatures. The patch is citation review, returning automatically any document that cites copied circulation as quality evidence. All four patches share one principle. The state of a device has to be observable, and a device that cannot be observed is treated as absent.

## 18.6 Boundaries with Adjacent Mechanisms

Against misaligned incentives (Chapter 17): misalignment is a question of direction, where the assessment points at the wrong target, and dilution is a question of existence, where no carrier of the direction is there at all. The test question: if you hand the responsibility to one named person, does the situation change. If it does, the case is dilution. If handing it over changes nothing because his assessment forces him to act against it, the case is misalignment. Colgan 3407 (Case 3 of Chapter 17) has both layers: the economic structure of the regional carrier was misalignment and the absent stop authority was dilution.

Against the invisibility of maintenance (Chapter 29): unclaimed maintenance debt often has dilution as its cause. The Westminster restoration (po-005) shows both faces, with responsibility scattered across two Houses and several bodies as the dilution and the debt itself as invisible maintenance. Classify by the principal contradiction: repair the responsibility structure first, and the debt will reach the agenda.

Against information attenuation up the hierarchy (Chapter 16): attenuation is vertical distortion and dilution is horizontal dissipation. In an organization that has both, bad news neither travels up nor gets claimed sideways, and Chapter 16's direct channel and this chapter's single accountable person are a pair of parts that have to be fitted together.

## 18.7 Five Self-Checks

1. Which of your organization's recent processes ran a gatekeeping signature chain longer than three? Can you write down the specific items each signature verified?
2. For the matter you are responsible for, is unilateral stop authority in your hands? Have you used it? Who paid the cost when it was used?
3. Take one shared object that spans departments, whether equipment, data or process, and write its four-party register. If you cannot write down the assessor and the party that accepts the effect, who owns it now?
4. In the last committee resolution you took part in, was the executor named in the text of the resolution?
5. After an incident, does your organization call the repair meeting or the responsibility meeting first? Where is that order written down?
