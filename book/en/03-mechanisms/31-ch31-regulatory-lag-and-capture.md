# Chapter 31 Regulatory Lag and Capture | Stigler

> What this chapter covers: why rules always arrive half a step late, and how a regulator can end up speaking for the regulated; Stigler's capture theory, the criteria, four field sites, and the defence of a diversified information supply.
> When to turn back to it: when you are assessing a sector's regulatory environment, forecasting the risk window of a new business model, or when your compliance team understands the business's real holes better than the business team does.
> Reading time: about 50 minutes.

Regulatory lag and capture is the only pair of mechanisms in this book where the invisible hand makes a fully frontal appearance. The market's hand steers the regulator's attention, its personnel and its information pipes toward the interests of the regulated, and none of it needs a bribe or a conspiracy. George Stigler's 1971 paper, *The Theory of Economic Regulation*, Bell Journal of Economics and Management Science 2(1): 3-21 (the reprint record held by Crossref has been checked), makes the core claim: regulation is usually requested by the industry and operated for it, and regulation benefits the incumbents. Fifty years on, the platform economy, artificial intelligence and gene technology have merely moved his argument onto new ledgers. This chapter treats lag (rules slower than technology) and capture (the regulator sliding toward the regulated) together, because each causes the other: the lag period is the seedbed of capture, and capture lengthens the lag.

The extension of Stigler's argument needs one main line drawn through it: capture theory has moved from interest capture to *cognitive capture*, in which the regulator's way of thinking is assimilated by the industry, so that even with personnel and interests kept separate, the frame through which a problem is seen is already the industry's frame. Cognitive capture is harder to govern than interest capture because it leaves no transaction that could be prohibited. Nobody rotated through the door, yet everyone's whiteboard carries the same set of industry terms. Detection therefore runs only on indirect indicators: the similarity between the wording of regulatory reports and industry white papers, the density of dissent recorded inside the regulator, the share of independent research the regulator cites. All three are measurement ideas discussed in the literature, and this book hands them to the reader for one use, self-check: has your organization's risk and compliance function undergone cognitive capture, is the language of risk now entirely in tune with the language of business? Capture of an internal relationship sits closer than capture of a regulatory one. It lives in your own building.

One institutional design direction for cognitive capture deserves to be written down: diversify the regulator's information diet (a deepening of Device 1). The extreme form is a technical body fully independent of the industry, a national-laboratory type of institution that serves no company and lives on independent testing. The product of such a body, independent test data and methodology, is the culture medium the regulator grows on; without it the regulator's technical judgment can only grow out of the regulated party's white papers. Neutral bodies are traditional in nuclear power and aviation, in the form of national laboratories and government test centres, and are generally absent in the new sectors (platforms, algorithms, genes). An absent neutral body is the deepest pit in a lag period. If your sector has a policy discussion about founding one, this chapter's position is to support it: it is the most expensive and the most fundamental of all the defences.

## 1 What It Is

Definition: regulatory lag means rules updating more slowly than technology and business models change, so that risks inside the window sit unclaimed. Regulatory capture means a deep fusion of regulator and regulated across personnel, information and interest, so that the regulator's objective function slides toward the regulated party's.

Three criteria start it. Any one is enough.

1. The regulator's main information source is the regulated party: data reported by them, technology explained by them, people supplied by them.
2. The time between the rule's last update and the change in the business exceeds one technology generation.
3. The expected cost of a penalty is below the expected gain from the violation, so the fine becomes a membership fee.

## 2 How It Grows

Three generative conditions.

Condition one is industry concentration. The regulated parties are few and large, and the regulator faces a full-time, professional opposing team while its own staff turn over and its budget arrives through an approval process. Asymmetry in specialist knowledge is the technical precondition of capture. Cognitive capture arrives before interest capture.

Condition two is insufficient regulatory resources. What the regulator cannot understand it can only have explained by the regulated party, and it believes what it is told. Data are submitted by the regulated, definitions are set by the regulated, technical training is provided by the regulated. The single information pipe was settled the day the budget passed.

Condition three is the revolving door. People move in both directions between regulator and regulated, which puts tomorrow's employer into today's case file. A revolving door does not necessarily corrupt. It necessarily affects how sharp the wording is.

## 3 The Shape in Four Domains

### Case One (Aviation): Thirty Days, Two Years, Four Documents

av-038.

**When and where**: 21 March 2022, 14:16 to 14:21:40 Beijing time; a valley running south-east to north-west near Molang Village, Langnan Town, Teng County, Wuzhou, Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region. The main impact point was a water-filled crater about 45 square metres in area and 2.7 metres deep, at 23°19′25.52″ N, 111°06′44.30″ E. The issuing office for the bulletin of 2022-04-20 was the Aviation Safety Office.

**What and who**: the technical investigation team organized by the Aviation Safety Office of the Civil Aviation Administration of China (CAAC), under the Regulation on the Technical Investigation of Civil Aircraft Events (CCAR-395) and Annex 13 to the Convention on International Civil Aviation; China Eastern Yunnan Airlines as operator; Boeing as design and manufacturing party; the US National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) carrying out the recorder readout, the work performed by NTSB personnel with CAAC personnel present to supervise and record (NTSB report p. 4). Positions involved: head of the investigation team, authorised representative of the State of Design, safety director of the operator, airworthiness inspector of the regional administration. The book has not obtained the team membership list or the appointment documents.

**What they were doing**: at 13:16 the aircraft took off from runway 21 at Kunming Changshui Airport; at 13:27 it reached cruise altitude 8,900 metres; at 14:17 it entered the Guangzhou control area along airway A599; 14:16 was the last normal air-ground communication (CAAC bulletin 2022-04-20, paras 3 and 5). At 14:20:55 Guangzhou area control radar raised a "deviation from assigned altitude" alert. The aircraft left cruise altitude, the controller called the crew, and no reply came. At 14:21:40 the last radar record showed standard pressure altitude 3,380 metres, ground speed 1,010 km/h and heading 117 degrees; the signal then disappeared (same bulletin, para 3). Records before departure were clean: the last A check (31A) and C check (3C) both inside maintenance programme limits; that day's pre-flight and transit release with no fault report and no deferred defect; loading within requirements; no cargo or mail declared as dangerous goods; flight crew and cabin crew holding valid licences and certificates; flight, duty and rest times meeting the rules; the pre-flight medical examination passed that day (2022-04-20 bulletin, para 5; 2024-03-20 bulletin, para 6). The site survey followed: wreckage fragments were concentrated on and under the ground between bearings 0 and 150 degrees from the impact point; the trailing edge of the right winglet was found about 12 kilometres from the main impact point; vegetation in the forest showed signs of burning (2022-04-20 bulletin, para 4).

**The decision point**:

One. On 23 March 2022 the crash-survivable memory unit of the cockpit voice recorder (CVR) was recovered, and on 27 March the same component of the flight data recorder (FDR). On 28 March CAAC and NTSB personnel formed a recorder data recovery group (NTSB report pp. 4, 5, 14). The legal basis was written into the bulletin: CCAR-395 and Annex 13 (2022-04-20 bulletin, para 2). CAAC had already tried to download the CVR in Beijing using a substitute chassis; decompression threw an error and produced no intelligible audio (p. 5).

Two. On 20 April 2022, day 30 of the accident, CAAC published a bulletin on the preliminary report. It states its basis plainly: Annex 13 requires the State conducting the investigation to send a preliminary report to ICAO and to the States participating in the investigation within 30 days, with content "usually the factual information obtained so far, not including analysis of the causes and conclusions" (2022-04-20 bulletin, para 2). The same bulletin described the recorders as "severely damaged by impact, with data recovery and analysis still in progress" (para 5). That day is the boundary of "when did they know". Control knew within 55 seconds that the aircraft was down. On day 30 the investigators did not know why.

Three. On 1 July 2022 the NTSB issued the Cockpit Voice and Flight Data Recorder Combined Download Report. All four audio channels (observer, first officer, captain, cabin) were rated Excellent. The first three ran about 120 minutes each and the cabin channel about 180 minutes, with the accident segment recorded in full (NTSB p. 13, Table 1). The recovery group had examined the CVR board microscopically, repaired pins and replaced connectors, and the final download ran without error (pp. 11-13).

Four. 20 March 2023 and 20 March 2024, two anniversary bulletins. The first called the accident "very complex, extremely rare", and recorded that over the year more than 100 pieces of significant wreckage had been tested and that verification work had been run on flight simulators and on a real aircraft (2023-03-20 bulletin, paras 2 and 3). The second itemised crew licences and medicals, airworthiness certificates, maintenance personnel qualifications, pre-flight faults, airport support, air traffic control qualifications and equipment, weather, loading and dangerous goods, concluded that no anomaly had been found in any of them, and closed with one line about continuing experimental verification and cause analysis (2024-03-20 bulletin, paras 6 and 7).

**What was visible then**: the seat is March 2024, the Aviation Safety Office of CAAC, the person drafting that year's anniversary bulletin. On the desk: the exclusion list from the 2022-04-20 preliminary report, eight items, each with its basis (para 5); the statement in the same bulletin that the preliminary report had gone to ICAO and the participating States under Annex 13 (para 2), which means this list is all that other States' operators received (para 5); the download report of 2022-07-01, four channels with quality ratings and durations, ten minutes to read, not one word about cause (NTSB p. 13); the draft of the 2023-03-20 bulletin and the questions that followed its publication; the test records and submission lists for more than 100 pieces of wreckage (2023-03-20 bulletin, para 2); the plans and results of the simulator and real-aircraft verification; operating data from the same type continuing in normal service at home and worldwide; and two sets of questions that have to be answered, from the families and from international civil aviation. One further item needs a decision at this desk: whether to keep the draft sentence "repair the damaged recorders and analyse the recovered data". The NTSB download report had recorded all four channels as Excellent on 1 July 2022 (NTSB p. 13), and by this day that is 20 months. Information that became known only afterwards: the specific content of the accident-segment recorder data (released in April 2026 through the US Freedom of Information Act process, and this book quotes no parameter from it); the sequence of control inputs before ground impact; confirmation of any single point of failure.

**What happened**: all 123 passengers and 9 crew on board died, 132 people (2022-04-20 bulletin, para 1). The official documents published since number three: the preliminary report bulletin of 2022-04-20 and the progress bulletins of 2023-03-20 and 2024-03-20. There is no cause conclusion, and therefore no targeted airworthiness directive based directly on this accident, and no grounding or restriction of the 737-800 fleet. At the 2025 and 2026 anniversaries no comparable bulletin from CAAC was seen (Deutsche Welle citing Reuters, 2026-03-22, Grade E). The investigation is in its fifth year. Back to the three questions. The most common answer to the first is "wait another year until the experiments finish". The cost of that action in this case is one anniversary briefing and one meeting with the families each year, and the price is that the new material available for each bulletin shrinks year on year. The item most easily missed under the second is the download report of 2022-07-01. It looks like a technical annex, and it is the only document in the case that can strike "the data could not be read" off the list of explanations. The checkpoint for the third belongs at the bulletin that follows the data readout: if a bulletin cannot say what it adds to the one before, it is only a mark of time passing.

**Options ruled out**: grounding the 737-800 (the global fleet runs into the thousands, a grounding without technical basis is not feasible and has no precedent); issuing a targeted airworthiness directive (an AD requires first establishing an "unsafe condition", and no cause means no finding); publishing the recorder content (Annex 13 constrains disclosure of investigation records, and criminal and family litigation are running in parallel); writing a cause into the preliminary report (Annex 13 states expressly that a preliminary report contains no cause analysis, a sentence the bulletin itself quotes); transferring the investigation to the State of Design (Annex 13 puts the organizing duty with the State of Occurrence, with the State of Design sending an authorised representative); closing the case as "pilot error" (the 2024-03-20 bulletin excluded anomalies in licences, medicals, duty and rest, and training records item by item, and the existing documents do not support that conclusion); an independent third-party review (Annex 13 provides no such procedure and there is no precedent).

**Early signals**: (1) From the alert at 14:20:55 to loss of signal at 14:21:40, 45 seconds, the aircraft fell from 8,900 metres to 3,380 metres at a ground speed of 1,010 km/h on heading 117 (2022-04-20 bulletin, para 3). That profile does not resemble a gradual loss of control, and it was pressed flat by "wait for the wreckage analysis". (2) Between 14:16 and 14:20:55, 4 minutes 55 seconds, no abnormal call and no distress call (same paragraph). It was pressed flat by "the communications were normal". (3) The main crater is 45 square metres and 2.7 metres deep, the right winglet trailing edge came down 12 kilometres away, and the forest burned (para 4). That distribution points to high speed, a steep angle, and parts leaving the aircraft first. It was read past by the bulletin's sentence that the aircraft broke up after hitting the ground. (4) Both recorders were severely damaged, every download attempt in Beijing with a substitute chassis failed, and the memory boards had to go to the NTSB laboratory (NTSB pp. 5, 15). It was pressed flat by "send it out and it will be read", when the readout took three months. (5) More than 100 pieces of wreckage were tested and still produced no single point of failure (2023-03-20 bulletin, para 2). The phrase "complex, rare" absorbed it, and for two years afterwards no public document mentioned the sentence "there is still no failure point" itself.

**The point of escalation**: 1 July 2022. The data could be read, the accident segment was complete, and all four channels were rated Excellent (NTSB p. 13). For the investigating side this was the day of highest availability: a 33-page report, four channels readable, the accident segment complete, after which any claim that technical conditions do not permit can only point at analysis and no longer at recovery. The report's structure carries the same message. It concerns removing boards, repairing pins, replacing connectors, correlating time and rating quality; there are two appendices, an audio quality rating table and a table of verified parameters, and not a page discusses a possible cause (pp. 4-32). For the industry nothing happened that day: the fleet kept flying and the bulletins continued. There was no press conference and no Chinese-language material mentioned the report. It went into the file and stayed there for nearly four years, until it surfaced through the US Freedom of Information Act process in April 2026 (media record, Grade E).

**Failure chain**: technical layer. A rapid descent from cruise at 8,900 metres to 3,380 metres in 45 seconds at a ground speed of 1,010 km/h. The debris distribution and the winglet trailing edge 12 kilometres away point to an in-flight breakup. Crew, maintenance, fuel, loading, air traffic control and weather were excluded item by item (2024-03-20 bulletin, para 6). No single point of failure has been confirmed, and that layer is still blank.

Organizational layer. The organizer of the investigation is also the industry regulator of the operator. CCAR-395 places the duty to organize the investigation with CAAC, and the bulletin's wording is drafted and approved by agencies in the same chain. The technical investigation team was formed "together with the relevant departments" (2022-04-20 bulletin, para 2), so member units and the regulated entity sit inside one administrative sequence. Annex 13 mandates only the 30-day preliminary report; the timing of the final report is held by the investigating State. Without a cause conclusion the airworthiness authority cannot write an "unsafe condition" into a directive, and the fleet's response can go no further than generic notices and operator self-checks.

Incentive layer. The 737-800 is a global fleet, the cost of a grounding runs into the tens of billions of dollars, and a grounding without technical basis is not feasible. The longer the investigation runs, the shorter the industry's memory, the more new topics arrive in the same period, and the lower the marginal return on pressing the question. In a period with no cause, "wait for the report" is the least trouble for every party.

The junction breaks between the organizational and incentive layers. For the regulator, "no conclusion" is a tolerable steady state. For the industry it is an exposure with no maturity date.

**Minimum counterfactual**: when the preliminary report was published on 20 April 2022, publish an investigation timetable alongside it: the date of the next public bulletin, the categories to be published (wreckage, recorder data, simulation verification), and a commitment that if there is still no cause by then, two lists will be published, items excluded and items under examination. One action, needing no new evidence, no prior finding of cause, and no one changing a conclusion. A timetable promises no conclusion, only nodes. Once the nodes are public, the reason for any delay has to be public too, and the length of the window shifts partly from "scientific progress" back to "governance commitment".

**Outcome and sources**: three official bulletins (CAAC 2022-04-20, 2023-03-20, 2024-03-20, HTML full text, official release without page numbers; this card numbers the paragraphs of the official text, and a paragraph-order cross-reference is in the evidence file); NTSB, Cockpit Voice and Flight Data Recorder Combined Download Report, report number DCA22WA102, 1 July 2022, 33 pages, released through the Freedom of Information Act process after April 2026, page numbers as PDF pages. The passage on the absence of new bulletins at the 2025 and 2026 anniversaries rests on media records (Grade E); the book has not obtained the final accident report, nor any internal document of the investigation. Link at Appendix B, av-038.

The no-conclusion state deserves one more reading, from the side of the global safety system. The force of an international civil aviation special telegraph, in which a State's regulator sends safety advice to its own operators after a major accident, depends on a cause conclusion. In a period without a cause, what goes out can only be generic notices about stronger training and procedure checks, and the incremental constraint such notices place on operators of the same configuration is close to zero. The no-cause period is therefore not only the waiting of the State concerned. It is a public window for the whole industry, and its length is set by the scientific progress of the investigation, not by any governance intention. This book passes no comment on how fast any individual case moves. It writes down the structure: a prolonged investigation is itself an industry-level risk exposure, and the hedges against that exposure (tighter generic inspection, voluntary self-checks by operators of the same configuration) fall inside the operator's own discretion during the window. On how reliable discretion is when there is no external compulsion, see Chapter 11.

### Case Two (Chemical): Forty to Sixty Tons, Five Regulatory Layers, Zero Storage Inspections

ch-002.

**When and where**: 17 April 2013, smoke and fire reported at 19:29, detonation at 19:51; West, McLennan County, Texas, United States, at the West Fertilizer Company fertiliser warehouse and one side of the rail spur. The warehouse was built in 1961 and in production by 1962; in a 1972 photograph the nearest house stood about 265 feet from the plant boundary; the US Geological Survey recorded the explosion as magnitude 2.1 (CSB p. 20, p. 84).

**What and who**: West Fertilizer Company (WFC; privately held; fertiliser blending, retail and distribution; the same site held the grain silos of Adair Grain, another family company). Five regulatory layers: the US Department of Labor Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA); the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA); the Department of Homeland Security Chemical Facility Anti-Terrorism Standards (CFATS) programme; the Texas Office of the State Chemist (OTSC) and the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ); the McLennan County Local Emergency Planning Committee (LEPC) and the West Volunteer Fire Department (WVFD). Positions involved: the company manager, the fire chief and assistant chief, the county LEPC coordinator, the state SERC duty line. The report does not state a company safety officer position.

**What they were doing**: 40 to 60 tons of fertiliser-grade ammonium nitrate (FGAN) sat in wooden bins inside the warehouse, and one railcar on the spur was not fully unloaded, about 100 tons (p. 19). The same site held 17 tons of anhydrous ammonia, with 34,000 pounds actually present in April 2013 (p. 19, p. 180). The company blended to customer formula, and blended material counted under EPCRA as "work in progress" rather than "finished goods for sale" (p. 206). When the warehouse was built in 1961 the surrounding land was open and the town had no zoning. The town then grew toward the warehouse: a park less than 150 feet away, an apartment building at 450 feet, a nursing home at 500 feet, a middle school at 552 feet (p. 20, p. 16). The 2012 Tier II form listed FGAN for the first time, submitted to WVFD, the county LEPC and the state health services department (p. 204). On 12 February 2013 pallets and branches were burned on site and the neighbouring elementary school was evacuated for about 30 minutes; the school had no advance notice, and 911 dispatch did not confirm that this was a notified burn (p. 203). Fire broke out on the evening of 17 April. The cause remains undetermined, and law enforcement kept three possibilities open: an electrical wiring fault, a short circuit in an electric golf cart, arson (p. 18). The day before, a farmer had bought a bag of FGAN and ammonium sulphate blend, and that bag became the only sample in later testing that represented the material inside the bin before the blast (p. 80). The fire department sent two pumpers, two small attack vehicles and a tanker. The usable hydrant stood by the high school, 1,600 feet away, and after laying 1,000 feet of four-inch hose the crews were still about 700 feet short (p. 31).

**The decision point**:

One. 2004, change of ownership. An EPA inspector came on site and issued a citation for failure to resubmit the risk management plan (RMP) registration; the company then hired its insurer to write the anhydrous ammonia RMP (p. 174). On 21 June 2006 TCEQ issued a violation referral for anhydrous ammonia without an air permit (p. 174, note 440). Both visits concerned ammonia. The report also records that the owner "did make corrections" after being notified of non-compliance (p. 174). The RMP that followed covered anhydrous ammonia, not ammonium nitrate; the report does not state the inspection date, the inspector's name or the checklist, nor whether the inspection reached the warehouse side.

Two. 2008, when DHS extended the Top-Screen submission period for agricultural facilities serving farmers by 73 FR 1640, expressly not applicable to chemical distribution facilities (p. 178). WFC never submitted. Against the CFATS threshold of 2,000 pounds, it held at most 120,000 pounds on site, sixty times the threshold. The CSB's conclusion sentence reads: until the explosion in 2013, DHS did not know that this facility stored FGAN (p. 178).

Three. The 2012 Tier II submission. The form carried the quantity range for FGAN, the storage method and the on-site location. The county emergency response plan had last been formally revised in 2010, and Annex Q required every EPCRA-regulated facility in the county to be listed; WFC was not on the list (p. 203-204). The written judgment of the county LEPC was that WFC's ammonia and FGAN "appeared" to qualify for the agricultural exemption, and that judgment was confirmed by the state SERC (p. 205). WFC also appears on no attendance roster of any county LEPC meeting across a span of more than 21 years (p. 203).

Four. 17 April 2013, 19:29 to 19:51, twenty-two minutes. The 19:29 alarm came from a member of the public; a patrol officer arriving saw flames and called dispatch to upgrade the call to a structure fire (p. 30). The assistant chief, on arrival, advised officers to begin evacuating nearby residents and radioed for a ladder truck to stand by at the apartment building; none was available. Full evacuation was still being assessed when the explosion came, with flames then pouring from the roof skylight and the north-east door to a height of 40 to 50 feet (p. 32). In those twenty-two minutes not one of the five regulatory layers was present. The only thing that could arrive was a volunteer fire department with no pre-plan.

**What was visible then**: the seat is March 2013, coordinator of the McLennan County LEPC. On the desk: the 2012 Tier II form, with annual maximum and daily average ranges for FGAN, the storage method and the on-site location, submitted including to the county fire department (p. 198, p. 204); the county plan in its 2010 revision, body plus 22 annexes, Annex Q being the hazardous materials and oil spill response annex, whose regulated facility list omits WFC (p. 203); the fact that Tier II electronic submissions are received through the state health services department and the county's copy went down the same channel (p. 199); the LEPC attendance rosters, with WFC absent from every one from the committee's founding in 1992 to 2013 (p. 203); the school evacuation record of 12 February 2013 and the school's demand that future burns be notified in advance (p. 203-204); the state SERC position on the agricultural exemption, on which the county concluded that WFC's ammonia and FGAN "appeared" to qualify (p. 205); the fire department's training records, which show anhydrous ammonia drills and no FGAN drill (p. 122). What was not on the desk is equally clear: an impact-range calculation for this facility, a drill record for this facility, a federal inspection report dated after 1985 (p. 171, p. 244). The Tier II form itself omits three things: the material of the bins, the spacing inside the building, and how far a blast would reach. Its fields are name, quantity range, storage method, location, and whether the location is confidential (p. 198). Information that became known only afterwards: the bins were wooden, with no sprinklers and no detection; the roof was combustible asphalt shingle; the seed room stood next to the bins (p. 244); blended material was not eligible for the agricultural exemption, and an EPA letter to the Fertilizer Institute dated 3 September 2010 stated that "blending must be reported" (p. 206); the Top-Screen was never submitted; the 1971 provision 1910.109(i) had always applied to facilities storing more than 1,000 pounds of AN (p. 169).

**What happened**: 15 people died, 12 of them fire and rescue personnel and 3 members of the public; 252 people were injured directly by the explosion, and the report counts more than 260 injured (p. 16, p. 33). More than 350 homes were damaged and 150 destroyed; a middle school, a high school, a 22-unit apartment building and a 145-bed nursing home were demolished (p. 16, p. 243); more than 150 buildings around the facility were damaged. Insurance-related losses came to about 230 million dollars, and federal disaster relief exceeded 16 million dollars (p. 90). WFC held a general liability limit of 1 million dollars and property cover of about 4.45 million dollars, and the company went bankrupt afterwards (p. 89, p. 16). On 9 October 2013 OSHA issued 24 citations, 9 of them serious violations of 1910.109(i): inadequate ventilation, non-fire-resistant construction, non-compliant stacking and bin height (p. 173); the case was settled without an admission (p. 174). The CSB entered the site on 18 April 2013, and for the first four weeks the scene was controlled by the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives and the State Fire Marshal's Office as a criminal case, which delayed evidence collection and interviews (p. 18). The report was unanimously approved at a public meeting in Waco on 28 January 2016; of its 19 recommendations 3 remain open, one of them requiring fertiliser-grade ammonium nitrate to be listed as a regulated substance under the federal Risk Management Program, still marked open (CSB project page). Back to the three questions. The most common answer to the first is "add the facility to Annex Q and schedule a joint inspection". The cost of that action here is one site visit and one item on a quarterly meeting agenda, and the price is that nobody has the authority to make it happen. The item most easily missed under the second is the 2012 Tier II form. It looks like an annual return, and it is the only document that states both how many tons and where they sit. The checkpoint for the third belongs in the week the information arrives: within 30 days of the form arriving, did the facility enter Annex Q.

**Options ruled out**: on 31 January 1994 the EPA proposed listing commercial high explosives under RMP, with a threshold set at 3.0 psi lethal overpressure (59 FR 4487, see p. 190, note 530); on 6 January 1998 the category was removed after a settlement with the Institute of Makers of Explosives (63 FR 640, same note). In the late 1990s OSHA considered listing FGAN under PSM, and it did not happen for "resource constraints and other priorities" (p. 180). The 2008 DHS extension went to farms only, not to distributors (p. 178). Until Texas amended its commercial fertiliser rules in June 2014, the Office of the State Chemist held sales registration authority only, with no authority to inspect storage areas (p. 217). The listing criteria for county Annex Q covered only facilities holding extremely hazardous substances, and FGAN was not on that list (p. 197); adding it to the EHS list would require changing the federal list, which is not a county decision. OSHA itself had two provisions it could not cite: in 1910.109(i), the two clauses on approval of large-scale storage and on continued use of existing buildings reserve authority to the "authority having jurisdiction" (p. 175). HB 417, introduced in 2015 to give the insurance commissioner rulemaking authority, was still in committee in December 2015 (p. 218). The industry's voluntary guidance, self-assessment every three years, arrived only after the event, in February 2014 (p. 219).

**Early signals**: (1) OSHA last entered the site in 1985 and cited anhydrous ammonia, respiratory protection and recordkeeping, not ammonium nitrate; the CSB found no 1910.109(i) citation against the facility at all, and no inspection record for the following 28 years (p. 171). It was pressed flat by "it was inspected last time and fixed". (2) Across the United States 1910.109(i) was cited 19 times between 1974 and 1999 and zero times between 1999 and 2013; no facility with the same industry code was cited under that provision in the same period, and the GAO concluded in 2014 that before West there had been exactly one citation in the whole country for ammonium nitrate storage at a fertiliser facility (p. 173). It was pressed flat by "nobody fines it, so it does not apply". (3) The annual survey forms of the insurer Triangle between 2006 and 2009 recorded ammonium nitrate on site, handled it as a security question, ticked "yes" to compliance with state storage and transport rules, and the state had no such rule at the time; the same forms examined grain dust and anhydrous ammonia closely against federal standards (p. 89). It was pressed flat by "the form passed". (4) On 12 February 2013 the school was evacuated for 30 minutes, 911 dispatch did not confirm a notified burn, and the school district afterwards asked only for advance notice (p. 203). It was pressed flat by "it was only a burn". (5) The 2012 Tier II form had already reported FGAN to the fire department, and the report's own words are that "no mechanism ensured that a drill or inspection would follow" (p. 122). It was pressed flat by "reported means managed".

**The point of escalation**: 2010, when Triangle declined to renew, on the grounds that WFC had repeatedly failed to act on its survey recommendations, replacing corroded wiring among them; WFC moved to U.S. Fire, with general liability of 1 million dollars (p. 89, p. 244). The CSB asked U.S. Fire for its site inspection records for the policy period and did not receive them; its outside counsel told CSB investigators that a policy of 1 million dollars "did not require much site activity" (p. 90). The report does not state the policy signing date or the signatory. The 2012 Tier II form carried one further column, asking whether the owner elected not to disclose the location to the public (p. 198): the design of the form itself presumes that reporting is not publishing. From the change of insurer in 2010 to April 2013 there was no external party holding both site access and a motive to find fault. OSHA had stopped, the state had no storage inspection authority, and the insurer had changed. The 2012 form added knowing. It did not add looking.

**Failure chain**: technical layer. Wooden bins, no automatic sprinklers, no fire detection, a combustible roof and combustibles hard against the pile; radiant heat from the fire warmed the surface of the pile, and soot, molten asphalt and PVC melting from the conveyor above contaminated the ammonium nitrate into an explosive mixture (p. 244). This layer is not in dispute.

Organizational layer. Five layers each covered a segment and each left a gap. OSHA's standard stopped in 1971, and from the mid-1970s the agency lost the authority to bring in new consensus standards (p. 169, note 403); the standard allowed wooden bins "treated to resist impregnation" without defining impregnation, and required sprinklers only for bagged product above 2,500 tons (p. 167); the PSM list omitted FGAN, a reactivity class 3 material (p. 180); the 50 per cent test in the retail exemption let anhydrous ammonia out of scope as well (p. 182). The EPA's RMP list held no FGAN, and WFC's 2011 hazard review listed toxic release, equipment failure and earthquake, with no fire and explosion (p. 188). The EPCRA agricultural exemption was read by county and state as a blanket exemption (p. 205). CFATS has a threshold and a list, and where a facility does not report, DHS does not know (p. 178). The state handled sales registration and not storage (p. 217). The county plan's annex did not list the facility (p. 204). The fire department had no pre-plan, for the stated reason that "there was no such regulatory requirement" (p. 244). No federal agency regulates municipal fire departments (p. 122).

Incentive layer. From 1999 to 2013 enforcement citations for this class of violation were zero, so the expected cost of a violation was close to zero (p. 173). The insurance market stopped pricing the risk in 2010, and the remaining underwriter sized its site investment to the premium (p. 90). The town's housing spread toward the facility, with the nearest apartment at 450 feet, the nursing home at 500 feet and the elementary school at 552 feet (p. 16), and no party in that expansion had an incentive to set a separation distance.

The junction breaks between the organizational and incentive layers. All five layers collected information, and none converted information into an obligation to inspect the site. Without that obligation, no layer's budget had to answer for how many tons stood inside that building.

**Minimum counterfactual**: one line added to the checklist of the 2004 EPA site inspection: is ammonium nitrate stored on this site, in what quantity, in what packaging, and in what building material. The inspector was already on site, and one line needs no legislation, no new budget, and no one changing their view. An answer of 40 to 60 tons in wooden bins with no sprinklers would have put the facility inside the scope of RMP or PSM, or inside the 30-foot combustible separation requirement that Texas only wrote into its rules in 2014: nine years earlier. One action, nine years before the event, at the cost of one question.

**Outcome and sources**: the primary source is the final report of the US Chemical Safety Board, West Fertilizer Company Fire and Explosion, Report 2013-02-I-TX, approved by the board on 28 January 2016, 267 pages as a PDF; page numbers here are PDF pages, equal to the printed page plus 3 (printed p. 201 is card p. 204). Relevant chapters: Chapter 5 on insurance (p. 85 ff), Chapter 7 on emergency response (p. 107 ff), Chapter 8 on regulatory analysis (p. 167 ff), Chapter 10 on key findings (p. 243 ff), Chapter 11 on recommendations (p. 245 ff). The status of the recommendations (19 in total, 3 open, one of them the listing of FGAN as a regulated substance under the Risk Management Program) comes from the CSB project page. Link at Appendix B, ch-002.

Case two's gap carries one more footnote from the sociology of fire. The concentration of volunteer firefighter casualties is the dark note of this subject: the absence of separation distances hits small communities hardest, since facilities in large cities get professional inspection and facilities in townships rely on volunteers. Gaps are distributed with strong geographic inequality, because regulatory density falls as urbanisation thins, and the bill for a gap is therefore posted mostly to small communities. That is the common distribution of every gap-type regulatory failure. Repair priority should run against the urban gradient, low-density places first, and the repair tool is a listed cross-layer joint check: turning this chapter's test, do the three layers together cover it, into an annual cross-layer joint verification checklist.

Filling that checklist has one technical point. Fill it by object, not by jurisdiction: the rows are this facility, this business line, this product; the columns are the regulatory layers; each cell records whether that layer covers the object and under which provision. A list filled by object exposes the empty cells directly, and an object covered by no layer is visible at a glance. A traditional list filled by jurisdiction only demonstrates each agency's diligence. Once the empty cells are exposed, the handling is the registration of Device 2 in this chapter: empty-cell objects go on the window-period list, with a self-reporting duty and a bond attached. The long-run fix for an empty cell is legislative gap-filling, assigning the ownerless object explicitly to one layer. Joint check, registration and assignment form the annual cycle of gap governance, chaired by the joint meeting of the layers, where what counts is the consolidation of the object lists; the meeting is only the occasion.

### Case Three (Finance): 2.56 Million Fractional Share Trades

fi-011. Raymond James & Associates. The FINRA penalty of August 2026 (the monthly summary PDF has been checked by this book): failure to report about 2.56 million fractional share trades settled in principal, fined 125,000 dollars. Fractional share trading grew out of odd-lot investing and automatic dividend reinvestment plans (DRIP). The business grew outside the trade classifications of the rule, and these trades were handled as bookkeeping entries. The rule was written in an era when fractional shares did not exist or did not reach scale, and the scaling of the business turned one classification error into a reporting gap in the millions. The ratio between the fine, 125,000 dollars, and the size of the gap, 2.56 million trades, is the arithmetic display of criterion 3, the fine as membership fee.

Stop at the decision point: the memorandum written when the fractional share business went live, in which internal compliance determined its reporting status. On the desk: the literal text of the rule, the shape of the new business, and the peer practice, that others do it too. **Outcome:** the violation was established, and the rule catch-up for the new business began after the penalty. Primary source: FINRA, Disciplinary and Other FINRA Actions - October 2026, 2026, link in Appendix B, fi-011.

Case three's fractional shares carry a lesson in taxonomy worth writing down. Rule classification always lags, so at the moment a business goes live its classification decision is the last line of defence, and the business side's compliance memorandum is the only gate during the lag period. The quality of that memorandum sets the strength of the gate. In this case the memorandum classified fractional shares as a bookkeeping matter, which was a misclassification; the misclassification was reinforced by peer practice, since others reported the same way, and peer practice then became the evidence for the memorandum, which closed the loop of judgment on itself. Only one question breaks such a loop: if this classification is wrong, what is the worst consequence. The answer in this case, a regulatory penalty for the reporting gap, could have pushed the memorandum toward a regulatory consultation, and asking the regulator is an order of magnitude cheaper than guessing the regulator. The either-or lesson, in one sentence for the compliance side of every new business: with a classification you are unsure of, ask and do not guess. Whether the regulator answers is the regulator's business. Whether you ask is your compliance duty.

### Case Four (Maritime): Convention Replaced the Assessment

ma-012. The pot-fishing vessel Amadeus (TH7), December 2023, UK waters. MAIB report (Report 13/2026, pages checked by this book): a deckhand leaned outboard during pot hauling, went into the water and died; the investigation points to an operation that required leaning outboard in an unstable posture with no fall protection, and to an industry practice that had long stood in for a formal risk assessment. Regulatory density on small fishing vessels is low by nature: where the rule cannot reach, convention is the law, and what convention eats is exactly the space a risk assessment was meant to cover. (The death and the report number have been checked against the pages; the detail of the convention is in the body of the report.)

Stop at the decision point: the moment this method of working was first carried on without protection and accepted by the rest of the fleet. On the desk: the efficiency of the method, a record of never having had an accident, and the impossibility of a regulator being present. **Outcome:** one person died. Primary source: MAIB, Report 13/2026, 2026, link in Appendix B, ma-012.

Case four's substitution of convention for assessment has one structural footnote from the fishing industry. On a small vessel the crew are the owners, working self-employed, and the cost of regulatory reach is very high, with boarding inspections covering a single-digit percentage of the fleet, so the self-copying of convention has no external calibration at all: what a new crew member learns from an old one has never been checked against a written standard. The workable lever for governing convention therefore sits in industry organization rather than in inspection: the operating guidance of a fishermen's association, and the operational requirements carried in insurance rates, since insurers board vessels more often than regulators do and a rate lever reaches far more operators than a fine. The insurance lever deserves its own line in this chapter. Insurers are among the few external calibrators whose interest runs alongside the regulator's in loss reduction, and in a sector where insurance rates carry the safety standard, the self-copying of convention gains one more external check.

The theme of external calibration carries the chapter into the signal list of section 4. Put the six signals against this chapter's cases and its reasoning. Signal one, manuals drafted by the leading firms, matches the gap in case two: each layer's inspection manual is written to its own jurisdiction, and no one drafts the joint manual written from the object's side. Signal two, a new business three years old with no rule, matches cases three and six: fractional shares and crypto assets are both windows on the three-year scale. Signal three, fines that get costed, matches case three: 125,000 against 2.56 million trades is the arithmetic an operator runs. Signal four, departure destinations concentrated in one industry, is the main signal of cognitive capture, and the three indirect indicators in section 1 are its operationalisation. Signal five, compliance findings that do not travel upward, matches case three: the classification doubt in the internal memorandum never reached the level of a regulatory consultation. Signal six, a self-regulatory code with no cost of breach, matches case four: industry convention is exactly self-regulation at zero cost.

Reorder the six signals by the cost of the evidence. Signals three and two are cheapest, since they need only the timestamps on fines and on rules. Signals one and six sit in the middle, needing the drafting list and the self-regulatory text. Signals four and five are most expensive, needing personnel trajectories and a trace of internal information flow. The starting recommendation is signal two: list the new business models in your sector, annotate each with the year of the corresponding rule, and take the list of windows older than three years straight to the governance layer. That list is the entire stock of lag in your sector.

#### Case 5 (Drugs and Devices): One Month Shipping Without Clearance (condensed)

ph-005. Adept Medical's ear ventilation T-tube (9mm). FDA device recall (Z-0966-2025, API record checked by this book): distributed without 510(k) clearance, 6,280 units recalled (2,500 in the United States, 3,780 outside it), distributed to Canada, Australia and New Zealand. The regulatory position on the device, its classification and its clearance pathway, was itself clear. What failed was the internal release gate: the decision chain that shipped before clearance arrived had no blocking position. Regulatory lag gives way here to a collapse inside regulatory execution, in which the rule was in place and the gate had no latch fitted. Minimum counterfactual: a hard validation of clearance status in the release system, so that no shipping order can be generated without it. Primary source: FDA, 2025, link in Appendix B, ph-005.

#### Case 6 (Fintech): The Banking Channel Is the Regulation (condensed)

st-002. An Indian cryptocurrency exchange (peak monthly trading volume 246 million dollars, December 2017). Failory Cemetery archive quoting the CEO Rahul Raj's post-mortem verbatim, checked by this book: it kept operating for fourteen months after its banking channel was frozen and then shut down. Regulatory uncertainty in crypto, meaning blank and shifting rules, travelled through the banking compliance channel and arrived as real pressure: the regulator said nothing, and the financial infrastructure spoke first. The case shows the true position of the regulated during a lag period. An absent rule is not freedom; it is uncertainty in the form of access to infrastructure, and the price that uncertainty sets on innovation, the cost of fourteen months of struggle, is carried by the company alone. Minimum counterfactual: a business continuity plan for a period of regulatory uncertainty, with the cost of diversifying channels calculated in advance. Primary source: Failory Cemetery (quoting Rahul Raj's post-mortem), 2019, link in Appendix B, st-002 (evidence grade E).

## 4 What Shows Up Earliest

Six weak signals, each with the reason usually used to press it flat.

1. The regulator's technical lectures, data platform and inspection manuals are drafted with the participation of the leading regulated firms. Reason: "They understand it best."
2. A new business has existed for more than three years with no matching rule and no explicit exemption. Reason: "Let it run a while."
3. The ratio of fine to violation gain is being costed openly by practitioners. Reason: "It is only a cost."
4. Where regulatory staff go when they leave is concentrated in the regulated industry. Reason: "Talent is a market."
5. The compliance team knows the business's real holes better than the business team, and that knowledge does not travel upward. Reason: "That is exactly what compliance is for."
6. The trade association's self-regulatory code has become a substitute for the missing rule, and breaching it costs nothing. Reason: "Industry self-governance."

## 5 How to Prevent It

Device 1: diversify the information source. Force the regulator's and the risk function's information inputs to exceed the regulated party's own reporting: independent sampling, third-party data, complaint and public sentiment channels, cross-region comparison. A single information source is the technical base on which capture stands. Plurality does not prevent capture; it preserves a floor, and while the floor holds, everything else still has a chance.

Device 2: register the window. A new business registers a list of blank points in the rules at launch, and during the blank period it carries a higher self-reporting duty and a bond. This rewrites the lag period from "nobody is watching" into "conditional release", and the condition is the deposit plus the reporting frequency. Had the fractional share business in fi-011 gone live with a window registration, the classification problem in 2.56 million trades would have surfaced in the first year rather than at the millionth trade.

Device 3: link the penalty to the gain. Set the penalty base against the gain from the violation, as a multiple of illegal proceeds, so that the membership fee arithmetic stops working; for repeat offenders, restrict the business rather than adding a linear penalty. The deterrent force of a penalty does not depend on its absolute size but on its ratio to the gain. Below a ratio of 1, the fine is the sector's cost of entry.

Device 4: a cooling-off period on the revolving door. Staff leaving key regulatory posts for the regulated industry serve a cooling-off period, during which paid communication on matters concerning that agency is prohibited, and the same applies in reverse. A cooling-off period cannot prevent capture of the mind, but it cuts the coarsest interest pipe. Cognitive capture, the "believes what it is told" of criterion 2, has to be diluted by diversifying the information source.

Set the four devices against this chapter's cases. Device 1 on case two: the object list for the cross-layer joint check is verified with the participation of a third party outside the trade association, an academic group or an insurer. Device 2 on case three: the registration list for a fractional-share-type new business is submitted at launch, and the window period carries a monthly flash report on the structure of trading. Device 3 on case three: the penalty base for a reporting violation becomes the number of trades or the number of clients involved, linked to scale, so that the fine is no longer decoupled from it. Device 4 keeps the personnel policy as written and adds symmetric execution of the reverse clause, with the regulated party's waiting obligation when hiring a former regulator written into the contract as well.

The four devices also have four ways of dying. Diversifying the information source dies when the definitions converge across the multiple sources, with third-party data still collected under definitions the regulated party wrote; the patch is independent authority over definitions, so that at least one source holds the right to define them. Window registration dies when the registration deadline drifts out of control, a three-year window becoming ten and the registration turning into a permanent licence; the patch is a sunset clause, with registration valid for one year and renewal requiring a fresh assessment whose report is published. Linking penalty to gain dies in the accounting contest, with the determination of illegal proceeds dragged into a numbers game; the patch is to objectify the penalty base on business volume measures such as trades, clients or data volume, which bypass the determination of proceeds. The cooling-off period dies when the list is marginalised, with the definition of a key post narrowed until most leavers fall outside it; the patch is to reverse the burden on the list, with the regulator deciding whether a post is key and the regulated party holding no right to apply for exemption. All four patches share one health-check question: when was this device's parameter, its definitions, its deadline, its penalty base or its list, last revised independently by the regulator.

The enterprise-side mirror of these defences belongs in the same section, because most of this chapter is written for regulators and the regulated party needs its own set of moves for surviving a lag period in a captured environment. Four of them. Treat the regulatory window as a risk exposure, accruing the risk reserve for window-period business on the assumption that no regulator will catch the fall. Register voluntarily, which is the enterprise side of Device 2, since a window registration buys a predictable expectation from the regulator and costs less than a penalty afterwards. Keep a direct channel for compliance, so that a compliance finding reaches the governance layer with a record, which is the cure for signal five. Cool the revolving door yourself, setting an internal cooling-off period for key posts when hiring former regulatory staff even where the law does not require it, since this is the cheapest reputational insurance a firm can buy in a captured environment. The common logic of the four: when the regulatory environment fails, the structural moves on the enterprise side are the only part still under its own control.

## 6 Boundaries with Adjacent Mechanisms

Against compliant and unsafe (Chapter 32): the organizational form of capture is "every inspection passed", seen in the certification philosophy around TWA800 and in the gap inspections of this chapter, and Chapter 32 supplies the micro-mechanism, in which nobody owns the space between the compliance items. The relation between the two chapters: Chapter 31 is the climate, Chapter 32 is the weather.

Against misaligned incentives (Chapter 17): capture is misaligned incentives in its regulatory form, where the regulator's own measures (budget, headcount, calm) point away from its real objective (risk control). The version of Chapter 17's overall-result signatory, in this setting, is the independent parliamentary scrutiny and the press.

Against extrapolating past the validated domain (Chapter 23): operating inside a lag period is in essence a large-scale extrapolation that nobody declared, with the business extending the applicability of an old rule to a new form. The counterpart of Chapter 23's declaration form in a regulatory setting is window registration.
